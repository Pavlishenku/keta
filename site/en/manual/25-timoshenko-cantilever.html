Chapter 25 Timoshenko cantilever

25.1 Statement#

Three timber cantilevers of rectangular section b × h = 0.20 × 0.50 m, with spans L = 1, 2 and 5 m, carry a vertical force P = 50 kN at their free end. The timber has a Young's modulus E = 11,760 MPa and a shear modulus G = 784 MPa; the shear factor of the rectangular section is κ = 1.2. The deflection of the free end and the part due to the shear force are sought.

25.2 Model#

The ratio E/G = 15 of timber corresponds to no isotropic Poisson's ratio (it would require ν = 6.5): the shear modulus is given directly by the parameter g of add_material (Chapter 7). The shear area is A/κ. One bar per cantilever is enough: under a nodal load, the Timoshenko bar gives the theoretical deflection. The same analysis without shear area (sy and sz set to None) isolates the bending deflection.

25.3 Script#

Exampleexamples/manual/app_console_timoshenko.py
# Timber cantilevers of 1, 2 and 5 m loaded at the tip: share of the shear deformation.
import keta

E, G = 11.76e9, 0.784e9                  # timber: E / G = 15
b, h, kappa, P = 0.20, 0.50, 1.2, 50e3   # section, shear factor, tip load
A, I = b * h, b * h**3 / 12

def deflection(L, shear):
    m = keta.Model()
    timber = m.add_material(e=E, nu=0.3, g=G)             # G given: no nu yields E / G = 15
    av = A / kappa if shear else None                     # None: rigid in shear
    sec = m.add_section_props(sx=A, iy=I, iz=I, ix=I, sy=av, sz=av)
    a, c = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 0)
    m.add_beam(a, c, sec, timber)
    m.fix(a)
    m.add_point_load(c, fz=-P)
    return -m.analyze().case(1).displacement(c)[2]

print("   L     bending    Timoshenko   closed form    diff.   shear part")
for L in (1.0, 2.0, 5.0):
    d0, d = deflection(L, False), deflection(L, True)
    df, dv = P * L**3 / (3 * E * I), kappa * P * L / (G * A)
    diff = 100 * (d - (df + dv)) / (df + dv)                     # in %
    print(f"{L:4.1f} m  {d0 * 1e3:7.4f} mm  {d * 1e3:7.4f} mm  {(df + dv) * 1e3:9.4f} mm  "
          f"{round(diff, 4) + 0:6.4f} %  {100 * dv / (df + dv):6.2f} %")
    assert abs(d0 - df) < 1e-9 * df and abs(diff) < 1e-7
Output

25.4 Results#

The deflection of the Timoshenko cantilever is the sum of the bending deflection and the shear deflection:

δ=3EIPL3​+GAκPL​(25.1)

The ratio of the two terms is 3κEI/(GAL2)=4.5h2/L2 for these data: the shear force provides 53 % of the deflection of the 1 m cantilever, 22 % for 2 m and 4.3 % for 5 m. keta gives the same values for both terms. An Euler-Bernoulli beam would underestimate the deflection of the short cantilever by 53 %: for a stocky member, and for timber whose shear modulus is low, the shear area must be given.
