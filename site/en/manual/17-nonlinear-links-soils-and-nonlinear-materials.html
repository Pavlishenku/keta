Chapter 17 Nonlinear links, soils and nonlinear materials

17.1 Link laws#

Syntax
m.set_link_law(link, comp, kind, k=0.0, param=0.0, kp=0.0, points=None)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
link——elastic link (add_elastic_link)
comp——component in the axes of the link (0 to 5)
kind——'tension' (stiffness k in tension only), 'compression' (stiffness k in compression only), 'gap' (gap ± param then stiffness k), 'bilinear' (stiffness k up to the force param, then kp), 'points' (tabulated law)
k, kpN/m0stiffnesses (N·m/rad for a rotation component)
paramm or N0gap (gap) or limit force (bilinear); rad or N·m for a rotation component
pointsm, NNonenon-decreasing tabulated law [(d, F), …]; F(0) may be non-zero (force present at rest)

The law replaces the linear stiffness of the component. The other components keep their stiffness. Deformations and forces follow the convention of the link: elongation and tension positive along the x axis of the link (from ni to nj).

Syntax
m.analyze_link_laws(case=None, stations=11)  →  LinkLawResult

Computes the equilibrium of a load case with the nonlinear laws (exact Newton method on the convex energy). The result has the methods of a static case, including link_force(link), and the number of iterations iterations. The results cannot be superposed.

Syntax
m.link_laws_path(case, node, comp, targets, steps=20, dead_case=None, families=[], arc=None)  →  [dict]

Follows the equilibrium path of a model with softening laws by controlling the displacement (node, component) according to the values targets, each segment being divided into steps steps; dead_case is a case applied in full beforehand. With arc=dict(length, steps=100, psi=0, stop, sign=1), the path is followed by arc length (snap-backs). families describes hinges whose moment-rotation law depends on the axial force: dict(link, comp=5, axial_comp=0, N=[…], laws=[[(θ, M), …], …]). Each point of the path is a dict(lambda, u, d, F).

Syntax
keta.hinge_law_at(N, laws, n)  →  [(θ, M), …]

Interpolates point by point a family of moment-rotation laws tabulated at increasing axial forces N, for the force n.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_lois_liaison.py
# Nonlinear links: tension-only hanger, support with a gap,
# and equilibrium path of a softening link under displacement control.
import keta

E, I, L = 210e9, 1e-4, 3.0
kb = 3 * E * I / L**3                                  # stiffness of the cantilever at its tip
for P in (-10e3, 10e3):
    m = keta.Model()
    a, c, g = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 1)
    m.add_beam(a, c, m.add_section_props(1e-2, I, I, 1e-4), m.add_material(E, 0.3))
    m.fix(a)
    m.fix(g)
    link = m.add_elastic_link(g, c)                    # link x: from g to c (downwards)
    m.set_link_law(link, comp=0, kind="tension", k=5e6)
    m.add_point_load(c, fz=P)
    r = m.analyze_link_laws(case=1)
    print(f"P = {P / 1e3:+.0f} kN: uz = {r.displacement(c)[2] * 1e3:8.4f} mm, hanger force = "
          f"{r.link_force(link)[0] / 1e3:7.3f} kN ({r.iterations} iterations)")
print(f"expected: {-10e3 / (kb + 5e6) * 1e3:.4f} mm (hanger active), {10e3 / kb * 1e3:.4f} mm (slack)")

# Support with a 2 mm gap (stiffness 1e8 N/m beyond it) under the cantilever
m = keta.Model()
a, c, g = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 0)
m.add_beam(a, c, m.add_section_props(1e-2, I, I, 1e-4), m.add_material(E, 0.3))
m.fix(a)
m.fix(g)
link = m.add_elastic_link(g, c)
m.set_link_law(link, comp=2, kind="gap", k=1e8, param=0.002)
m.add_point_load(c, fz=-20e3)
r = m.analyze_link_laws()
print(f"gap of 2 mm: uz = {r.displacement(c)[2] * 1e3:.4f} mm, reaction of the stop {r.link_force(link)[2] / 1e3:.3f} kN")

# Softening spring (peak of 100 N at 10 mm, plateau at 40 N) under displacement control
m = keta.Model()
ground, n1 = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(0, 0, 0)
m.fix(ground)
m.add_support(n1, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True, ry=True, rz=True)
link = m.add_elastic_link(ground, n1)
m.set_link_law(link, comp=0, kind="points", points=[(0, 0), (0.01, 100.0), (0.03, 40.0), (0.1, 40.0)])
m.add_point_load(n1, fx=1.0)
path = m.link_laws_path(case=1, node=n1, comp=0, targets=[0.05], steps=10)
for p in path[::2]:
    print(f"   u = {p['u'] * 1e3:6.2f} mm: lambda = {p['lambda']:7.3f}")

17.2.2 Reaction laws#

Syntax
keta.soil_py_soft_clay(c, gamma, X, D, eps50, J=0.5)  →  [(y, p), …]
keta.soil_py_stiff_clay(c, gamma, X, D, eps50, n=16)
keta.soil_py_sand(phi, gamma, X, D, k, cyclic=False, n=16)
keta.soil_tz_clay(tmax, D), keta.soil_tz_sand(tmax), keta.soil_qz(Qp, D)
keta.soil_earth_law(P0, Pa, Pp, K)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
cPa—undrained cohesion
gammaN/m³—submerged unit weight
Xm—depth
Dm—pile diameter
eps50——strain at 50 % of the strength
kN/m³—initial subgrade reaction modulus (sand)
tmax, QpN/m, N—maximum skin friction, end bearing resistance
P0, Pa, Pp, KN, N, N, N/m—at-rest, active and passive forces and stiffness of the trilinear earth pressure law

These functions return laws [(displacement, force)]: per unit length for the p-y curves (API RP 2A and Welch-Reese) and t-z, as a tip force for the q-z law. They are used by set_link_law(..., kind='points') or by the soil springs below, the laws per unit length after multiplication by the tributary length.

17.2.3 Soil springs and earth pressure#

Syntax
m.add_ground_spring(node, direction, points)  →  int
m.add_earth_pressure(wall, away, up, layers, width=1.0, distribution='consistent')  →  [int]
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
node——node connected to the soil
direction——direction of the spring (global vector)
pointsm, N—law [(d, F)]: d displacement along the direction, F > 0 in tension
wall——chain of nodes of a wall or a pile
away——direction of the active state (from the soil outwards)
up——upward vertical direction
layers——layers: dict(top, bottom, sv_top, gamma, Ka, K0, Kp, c, Kac, Kpc, kh_A, kh_B, kh_kappa); kh = kh_A + kh_B (top − y)kh_kappa
widthm1width of the wall
distribution—'consistent'distribution of the forces to the nodes: 'consistent' or 'lumped'

add_ground_spring creates a zero-length spring between the node and a new fixed node, and returns the identifier of the link. add_earth_pressure creates at each node of the wall a trilinear spring: at-rest pressure at zero displacement, active and passive plateaus; it returns the list of the links created (0 for a node without a spring). Both are used with analyze_link_laws.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_sols.py
# Soil-structure interaction: earth pressure coefficients, p-y, t-z and q-z curves,
# nonlinear soil springs and earth pressure on a wall.
import math
import keta

phi = math.radians(30)
print(f"Rankine: Ka = {keta.soil_ka_rankine(phi):.4f}, Kp = {keta.soil_kp_rankine(phi):.4f}; "
      f"Jaky: K0 = {keta.soil_k0_jaky(phi):.4f}")
d = math.radians(20)
print(f"Coulomb (delta = 20°): Ka = {keta.soil_ka_coulomb(phi, d):.4f}, Kp = {keta.soil_kp_coulomb(phi, d):.4f}")
py = keta.soil_py_sand(phi=phi, gamma=10e3, X=3.0, D=1.0, k=16e6)
print("p-y sand (X = 3 m, D = 1 m), last points [(y m, p N/m)]:", [(round(y, 5), round(p)) for y, p in py[-4:]])
print("t-z clay:", [(round(z, 4), round(t)) for z, t in keta.soil_tz_clay(tmax=40e3, D=1.0)][:4], "...")
print("q-z tip:", [(round(z, 4), round(q)) for z, q in keta.soil_qz(Qp=2e6, D=1.0)][-3:])

# Pile of 10 m (tube 1.0 x 0.02) in sand: p-y springs every metre, horizontal force at the head
m = keta.Model()
nd = [m.add_node(0, 0, -1.0 * i) for i in range(11)]
tube = m.add_section_tube(d=1.0, t=0.02)
for i in range(10):
    m.add_beam(nd[i], nd[i + 1], tube, m.steel() if i == 0 else 1)
for i, x in enumerate(nd[1:], start=1):
    law = keta.soil_py_sand(phi=phi, gamma=10e3, X=float(i), D=1.0, k=16e6)
    m.add_ground_spring(x, direction=(1, 0, 0), points=[(y, p * 1.0) for y, p in law])   # 1 m of pile per spring
m.add_support(nd[-1], dz=True)
for x in nd:
    m.add_support(x, dy=True, rx=True, rz=True)
m.add_point_load(nd[0], fx=300e3)
r = m.analyze_link_laws()
print(f"pile: head displacement {r.displacement(nd[0])[0] * 1e3:.3f} mm, rotation {r.displacement(nd[0])[4] * 1e3:.4f} mrad")

# Wall of 3 m pushed 5 cm outwards: the thrust tends towards the active state
m = keta.Model()
w = [m.add_node(0, 0, -z) for z in (0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0)]
for x in w:
    m.fix(x)
layer = dict(top=0.0, bottom=-3.0, sv_top=0.0, gamma=18e3, Ka=1 / 3, K0=0.5, Kp=3.0, kh_A=1e7)
links = m.add_earth_pressure(w, away=(1, 0, 0), up=(0, 0, 1), layers=[layer], distribution="lumped")
for x in w:
    m.add_support_disp(x, dx=0.05)
r = m.analyze_link_laws()
F = sum(r.link_force(l)[0] for l in links if l)
print(f"wall: total thrust {abs(F) / 1e3:.3f} kN/m (active Ka gamma H²/2 = {18e3 * 9 / 2 / 3 / 1e3:.3f} kN/m)")

17.3 Bars on zoned soil and pseudo-plastic soil#

Syntax
m.pse(soil, sub_per_zone=10, max_iter=50, tol=1e-8, exact=True)  →  PseResult
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
soil——soil of the bars: {bar: dict(zones=[dict(kfx, kfy, kfz, kmx, xl)], plastic=dict(side, ffx, ffy, ffz, fmx))}
zones——successive zones of the elastic foundation: moduli kfx, kfy, kfz [N/m per m] and kmx [N·m/rad per m], end abscissa xl [m]; the last zone extends to the end of the bar
plastic——nonlinear behaviour: side = 'pos', 'neg' or 'both' (side on which the soil reacts; a tensionless soil under a beam loaded downwards is 'neg'), capping thresholds ffx, ffy, ffz [N/m] and fmx [N·m/m]
sub_per_zone—10subdivisions per zone for tracking the soil states
max_iter, tol—50; 1e-8iterations and tolerance
exact—Truebeam on elastic foundation solved analytically, without discretisation, if no bar on soil has a hinge or a variable section (otherwise, and with False: consistent bed with cubic functions, the bars on soil to be subdivided)

The soil reacts by p = k·u (u local displacement); depending on plastic, it reacts only on one side (uplift) or is capped at the given threshold. Each load case is solved separately. With a purely elastic soil, the combinations of the model are formed (r.n_combos gives their number); with a one-sided or capped soil, the results cannot be superposed and a model that carries combinations is refused. r.case(i) returns a case result with displacement, reactions, bar_end_forces, iterations, plastic (true if a threshold or an uplift is reached) and soil(bar): dict(p, m, total_local, total_global, total_torque, segments), each segment being dict(x0, x1, state, p_mean, r_mean), where state gives the state ('elastic', 'capped', 'inactive') of the components fx, mx, fy, fz.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_sol_zones.py
# Soil by zones and pseudo-plastic soil: quasi-rigid footing of 10 m loaded with 1 MN
# with an eccentricity, on a soil without tension, then on a soil with a capped pressure.
import keta

P, L, kz = 1e6, 10.0, 1e7                      # load [N], length [m], modulus x width [N/m per m]

def footing(e, plastic):
    m = keta.Model()
    c = m.add_material(e=2e11, nu=0.3, rho=0.0)
    s = m.add_section_props(sx=1.0, iy=1e4, iz=1e4, ix=1e4)          # quasi rigid
    xs = sorted(set([L * i / 10 for i in range(11)] + [L / 2 + e]))
    n = [m.add_node(x, 0, 0) for x in xs]
    b = [m.add_beam(n[i], n[i + 1], s, c) for i in range(len(n) - 1)]
    m.add_support(n[0], dx=True, dy=True, rx=True, rz=True)
    m.add_point_load(n[xs.index(L / 2 + e)], fz=-P, case=1)
    r = m.pse({bi: dict(zones=[dict(kfz=kz)], plastic=plastic) for bi in b}, sub_per_zone=20)
    return n, b, r.case(1)

# 1) Soil without tension (p = k w <= 0 in compression: only the 'neg' side remains), e = 3 m > L/6
n, b, case_ = footing(3.0, dict(side="neg"))
c = 3 * (L / 2 - 3.0)
print(f"no tension: {case_.iterations} iterations, yielding = {case_.plastic}")
print(f"  settlement at the loaded end {case_.displacement(n[-1])[2] * 1e3:.3f} mm "
      f"(contact over c = {c:.1f} m, maximum pressure 2P/c: {-2 * P / c / kz * 1e3:.3f} mm)")
print(f"  uplift of the opposite end {case_.displacement(n[0])[2] * 1e3:.3f} mm")
soil = case_.soil(b[-1])
print(f"  last bar: soil resultant {soil['total_global'][2] / 1e3:.2f} kN; segments (components fx, mx, fy, fz: fz):")
for s in soil["segments"][::5]:
    print(f"     [{s['x0']:.2f}; {s['x1']:.2f}] m  state {s['state'][3]:8}  mean p {s['p_mean'][3] / 1e3:9.2f} kN/m")

states = sorted({s["state"][3] for bi in b for s in case_.soil(bi)["segments"]})
print("  soil states found along the footing:", states)

# 2) Soil with a pressure capped at 160 kN/m, e = 1.5 m
n, b, case_ = footing(1.5, dict(ffz=1.6e5))
segs = [s for bi in b for s in case_.soil(bi)["segments"]]
print(f"capped: resultant {sum(case_.soil(bi)['total_global'][2] for bi in b) / 1e3:.2f} kN, "
      f"{sum(1 for s in segs if s['state'][3] == 'capped')} segments on the plateau out of {len(segs)}")
Output
no tension: 5 iterations, yielding = True
  settlement at the loaded end -33.333 mm (contact over c = 6.0 m, maximum pressure 2P/c: -33.333 mm)
  uplift of the opposite end 22.222 mm
  last bar: soil resultant -305.56 kN; segments (components fx, mx, fy, fz: fz):
     [0.00; 0.05] m  state elastic   mean p   -279.17 kN/m
     [0.25; 0.30] m  state elastic   mean p   -293.06 kN/m
     [0.50; 0.55] m  state elastic   mean p   -306.94 kN/m

With an eccentricity of 3 m, greater than one sixth of the length, the footing on tensionless soil bears only on c = 3 (L/2 − e) = 6 m; the pressure there is triangular, with maximum value 2P/c, and the opposite end lifts off.

17.4 Nonlinear material laws#

Syntax
keta.uniaxial_path(law, params, path, stress_driven=False)  →  [(ε, σ), …]

Follows a cyclic uniaxial law along a strain (or stress) path: laws 'steel_mixed' (E, fy, H, iso: mixed hardening), 'asym_von_mises' (E, ft0, Ht, fc0, Hc), 'damage_simo_ju' (E, r0, A, B), 'plastic_damage_oliver' (E, fy, h, r0, A, n). These laws serve to characterise a material; the example of Chapter 22 gives an illustration.

The design laws without memory can also be followed with it and are used by fibre sections (fiber_frame):

LawParametersText
'sargin'fck (Table 3.1), or fcm, Ecm, eps_c1, eps_cu1EN 1992-1-1 §3.1.5, Expression (3.14), compression only
'steel_hardening'E, fy (design), k, eps_uk, eps_udEN 1992-1-1 Figure 3.8, inclined top branch
'strand'Ep, fp01k, fpk, eps_uk, eps_ud, inclined=1, gamma_s=1.15EN 1992-1-1 §3.3.6, Figure 3.10
'concrete_tension_softening'fcd, Et, ft, Gf, lc (eps_c2, eps_cu2, n)parabola-rectangle and regularised tension softening (energy Gf per band lc)
'bfup_t3'fck, fcm, Ecm, fctm, K, fctk_el, fctfk, eps_u_lim (gamma_c=1.5, gamma_cf=1.3, alpha_cc=0.85)NF P18-710, Figures 3.202 and 3.206 (UHPFRC of class T3, thick elements)
Syntax
m.fiber_frame(sections, case=None, load_steps=10, corotational=False, control=None, stages=[])  →  dict
m.continuum_nonlinear(case=1, lambdas=[1.0], substeps=4, plastic={}, large_displacements=False,
                      times=[], frictional={}, stop_at_limit=False, temperature={})  →  dict

fiber_frame computes a frame some bars of which are described by fibres: sections = {bar: dict(rect=(b, h, ny, nz), law=dict(kind, …), rebars=[dict(y, z, A, law)])} with the laws 'steel_kinematic' (E, fy, b), 'steel_mixed' (E, fy, H, iso), 'concrete_kj' (fcd), 'elastic' (E) and the design laws of the table above. The computation runs under load control, or under displacement control by control=dict(node, comp, targets, steps_per_leg); it accepts construction stages with creep (Chapter 13). The result contains node_disp, trace, lambda, time, stage_end and humidity. continuum_nonlinear handles continuum elements with von Mises plasticity (plastic={material: (σy, H)}, with optional creep (σy, H, A, n, m[, Q])), Mohr-Coulomb or Drucker-Prager criteria (frictional), large displacements (elastic materials only), and stop at the limit load.
