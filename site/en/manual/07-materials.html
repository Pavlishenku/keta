Chapter 7 Materials

A material is defined by its elastic constants, its density and its coefficient of thermal expansion. It can in addition receive a time-dependent behaviour law (creep and shrinkage) used by staged construction (Chapter 13).

7.1 Predefined materials#

Syntax
m.steel()  →  int
m.concrete()  →  int

They create a common material and return its identifier:

MethodEνρα
steel()210 GPa0.37850 kg/m³1.2·10⁻⁵ /K
concrete()33 GPa0.22500 kg/m³1.0·10⁻⁵ /K
Note

Each call creates a new material. To assign the same material to several bars, its identifier is kept in a variable.

7.2 General material#

Syntax
m.add_material(e, nu, rho=None, alpha_temp=None, name=None, g=None, damping=None)  →  int
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
ePa—Young's modulus
nu——Poisson's ratio; the shear modulus is G = E / (2 (1 + ν))
rhokg/m³Nonedensity; None: massless material (neither self-weight nor dynamic mass)
alpha_temp1/KNonecoefficient of thermal expansion; None: none (a thermal action on this material is then refused)
name—Nonename of the material
gPaNoneshear modulus of the bars when it is not E / (2 (1 + ν)) (timber: E/G ≈ 16); None: isotropic. Such a material is refused by shells and continuum elements
damping—Nonedamping ratio of the material, between 0 and 1, read by m.strain_energy_damping (Chapter 14); None: 0

The behaviour is linear elastic isotropic. Nonlinear material laws belong to the analyses that use them: fibres (Chapter 17), plastic continuum elements (Chapter 17), fire (Chapter 18).

The thermal actions example of Chapter 11 uses alpha_temp: the axial force of a heated bar fixed at both ends is −E·A·α·ΔT there.

7.3 Time-dependent behaviour of concrete#

7.3.1 Principle#

Under a stress σ applied at age t₀, the strain of a concrete at age t is written:

ε(t) = σ / E(t₀) + φ(t, t₀) · σ / Eref + εcs(t)

where φ(t, t₀) is the creep coefficient, Eref the modulus to which it refers and εcs the shrinkage strain (negative in shortening). For a variable stress history, keta applies Boltzmann's superposition principle to each stress increment, with its own loading age. These laws act only in staged construction (m.phased, Chapter 13), between the dates of the stages; the static analysis analyze uses only the modulus E of the material.

7.3.3 User-defined law#

Syntax
m.set_concrete_law_functions(material, phi, E_ref, shrinkage=None)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
phi——Python function phi(t, t0) of the creep coefficient (ages in days)
E_refPa—function E_ref(t0): reference modulus of φ for a loading at age t0
shrinkage—Nonefunction shrinkage(t) of the shrinkage strain (negative in shortening); None: no shrinkage

The functions are called by the kernel during the staged construction analysis; they can represent a test-based law or a code law that is not available.

Important

The instantaneous modulus used for each loading increment is the modulus E of the material (add_material), whatever the age of the concrete. E is therefore chosen consistent with the law (for example the mean modulus at 28 days). The time-dependent laws and the Python functions are not kept by to_json.

7.3.4 Example#

A 4 m column, of section 0.40 × 0.40 m, is loaded at 28 days with 2 MN. Its shortening is computed at several dates for different laws; the line “EC2, creep only” subtracts the shortening of an identical unloaded column (shrinkage only).

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_lois_beton.py
# Time-dependent concrete laws: shortening of a 4 m column under 2 MN applied
# at 28 days, for several creep and shrinkage laws.
import math
import keta

def column(rule=None, functions=None, loaded=True, **params):
    m = keta.Model()
    c = m.add_material(e=35e9, nu=0.2, rho=0.0)
    if rule:
        m.set_concrete_law(c, rule, **params)
    if functions:
        m.set_concrete_law_functions(c, *functions)
    s = m.add_section_rect(0.4, 0.4)
    a, b = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(0, 0, 4)
    m.add_beam(a, b, section=s, material=c)
    m.fix(a)
    m.add_support(b, dx=True, dy=True, rx=True, ry=True, rz=True)
    m.add_point_load(b, fz=-2e6 if loaded else 0.0, case=1)
    dates = [28, 128, 365, 3650, 10000]
    r = m.phased([dict(name=f"{t} days", time=t, cases=[1] if t == 28 else []) for t in dates],
                 creep_substeps_per_decade=16)
    return [r.phase(i).displacement(b)[2] for i in range(len(r))]

u0 = -2e6 * 4 / (35e9 * 0.16)
print(f"elastic shortening: {u0 * 1e3:.4f} mm")
print("dates [days]          :       28      128      365     3650    10000")
cases = [("COURBON", dict(e_direct=35e9)),
         ("EC2", dict(fc=35e6, rh=70.0, h_mm=400.0, cement="N")),
         ("EC2, creep only", None),
         ("BPEL", dict(fc=35e6, rh=70.0, rm_cm=10.0)),
         ("FIB2010", dict(fc=35e6, rh=70.0, h_mm=400.0, cement="N"))]
for name, kw in cases:
    if kw is None:    # shrinkage alone (unloaded column) subtracted from the EC2 law
        u = [a - b for a, b in zip(column("EC2", **cases[1][1]),
                                   column("EC2", loaded=False, **cases[1][1]))]
    else:
        u = column(name, **kw)
    print(f"{name:17} [mm]: " + " ".join(f"{x * 1e3:8.3f}" for x in u))

# User-defined law: phi(t, t0) = 1.5 (1 - exp(-(t - t0) / 100)), constant modulus
phi = lambda t, t0: 1.5 * (1 - math.exp(-(t - t0) / 100)) if t > t0 else 0.0
u = column(functions=(phi, lambda t0: 35e9))
print(f"{'user-defined':17} [mm]: " + " ".join(f"{x * 1e3:8.3f}" for x in u))
print(f"COURBON check at 10000 days: u0 (1 + phi) = {u0 * (1 + 2 * (1 - math.exp(-0.006 * 9972))) * 1e3:.3f} mm")
Output
elastic shortening: -1.4286 mm
dates [days]          :       28      128      365     3650    10000
COURBON           [mm]:   -1.429   -2.718   -3.907   -4.286   -4.286
EC2               [mm]:   -1.429   -2.847   -3.512   -4.475   -4.609
EC2, creep only   [mm]:   -1.429   -2.583   -2.984   -3.559   -3.643
BPEL              [mm]:   -1.429   -2.961   -3.630   -4.584   -4.819
FIB2010           [mm]:   -1.429   -2.808   -3.328   -4.551   -5.040
user-defined      [mm]:   -1.429   -2.783   -3.498   -3.571   -3.571
COURBON check at 10000 days: u0 (1 + phi) = -4.286 mm

Shrinkage acts only from the first stage (28 days), the date at which the structure enters the analysis. With the Courbon law, the final shortening is u₀ (1 + φ∞).

7.4 Functions of the time-dependent laws#

The time-dependent behaviour laws are also available as functions of time, for section calculations, checks or sensitivity studies.

7.4.1 Coded law#

Syntax
law = keta.concrete_law(code, **parameters)  →  ConcreteLaw
law.E(t), law.Ev(t), law.E_ref(t0), law.fc_mean(t), law.phi(t, t0), law.eps_sh(t, ts), law.n_equiv(),
law.compliance(t, t0)

The codes and the parameters are those of set_concrete_law (Table 7.1). The object gives the instantaneous modulus E(t), the long-term modulus Ev(t) of the BPEL and BHP rules, the reference modulus of creep, the mean strength at age t, the creep coefficient, the shrinkage at age t for a drying starting at ts (negative in shortening), the modular ratio and the creep function J(t, t₀) = 1/E(t₀) + φ(t, t₀)/Eref(t₀). Ages are in days.

7.4.2 Formulas of EN 1992-1-1#

Syntax
keta.ec2_phi(t, t0, fck, rh=70, h0=0.3, cement='N')
keta.ec2_shrinkage(t, fck, rh=70, h0=0.3, cement='N', ts=3, autogenous=True)
keta.ec2_ecm(fck)
keta.aaem_modulus(E, dphi, chi=0.8)
keta.nonlinear_creep_factor(sigma_c, fcm_t0)

Creep coefficient (Annex B), total drying and autogenous shrinkage (Section 3.1.4), mean secant modulus, age-adjusted effective modulus E / (1 + χ Δφ) and nonlinear creep factor exp(1.5 (kσ − 0.45)). h0 is the notional size 2 Ac/u in metres.

7.4.3 Relaxation and losses#

Syntax
keta.ec2_relaxation_ratio(t_hours, sigma_pi, fpk, cls=2, rho1000=2.5, seuil=None, capped=False)
keta.bpel_relaxation(t_days, sigma, fprg, rho1000)
keta.ip2_relaxation(t_hours, sigma_pi, rg, rho1000, rho3000)
keta.ec2_deferred_loss(eps_cs, Ep, d_sigma_pr, Ecm, phi, sigma_c, Ap, Ac, Ic, zcp)
keta.ip2_lump_sum(sigma_b, sigma_pi, fprg, Ep, rh, final_relaxation, n=6.0, eps_r=None)  →  dict
keta.bpel_lump_sum(sigma_b, sigma_pi, fprg, Ep, rh, rho1000, n=6.0, eps_r=None, mu0=None)  →  dict
keta.bpel_mu0(rho1000)  →  μ0

Relative relaxation loss of EN 1992-1-1 (Eq. 3.28 to 3.30; with capped=True, the time is capped at seuil hours, or at 500,000 hours for seuil=0; without seuil, it is not capped), relaxation loss of BPEL [Pa] and of the Instruction provisoire n° 2, and time-dependent loss of Equation 5.46 of EN 1992-1-1 [Pa].

ip2_lump_sum and bpel_lump_sum give the lump-sum time-dependent losses of the Instruction provisoire n° 2 and of BPEL from the final concrete stress at the level of the tendon sigma_b (compression positive), the initial stress sigma_pi, the guaranteed strength fprg, the modulus Ep and the relative humidity rh in per cent. They return elastic (n/2 σ_b, i.e. 3 σ_b with the regulatory ratio n = 6), shrinkage (ε_r E_p), creep (2 n σ_b for IP2, 2.5 n σ_b for BPEL), relaxation, viscoelastic and total = elastic + viscoelastic, in pascals. For IP2, the final relaxation is given and the time-dependent total is shrinkage + creep + relaxation × (1 − (shrinkage + creep) / (σ_pi − 0.55 f_prg)), without relaxation when the stress loss exceeds this margin. For BPEL, the relaxation is 6/100 ρ1000 (σ_pi / f_prg − μ0) σ_pi, zero below μ0, and the time-dependent total is shrinkage + creep + 5/6 of the relaxation; μ0 follows ρ1000 (0.43 up to 2.5 %, 0.35 up to 8 %, 0.30 beyond) unless mu0 is imposed. The lump-sum shrinkage ε_r is largest in a dry climate: 4·10⁻⁴ below 65 % humidity and 3·10⁻⁴ above for IP2, 3·10⁻⁴ and 2·10⁻⁴ for BPEL; eps_r imposes it.

7.4.4 Hereditary integral#

Syntax
keta.log_time_grid(t0, dt_min, dt_max, n)  →  [t]
keta.volterra_strain(J, t, sigma)  →  [ε]
keta.volterra_stress(J, t, eps)  →  [σ]
keta.relaxation_function(J, t0, t)  →  [R]
keta.fit_kelvin_chain(J, t0, n_units=8, tau_min=1e-2, ...)  →  dict(a0, tau, a, max_rel_error)

J is a coded law or a Python function J(t, t₀). These functions compute the strain under a stress history, the stress under a strain history, the relaxation function, and fit a Kelvin chain to the creep function, on a logarithmic time grid.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_fonctions_differees.py
# Functions of the time-dependent laws: EN 1992-1-1 creep and shrinkage, relaxation,
# time-dependent loss (Eq. 5.46), coded law, hereditary integral and Kelvin chain.
import math
import keta

fck, rh, h0 = 35e6, 70.0, 0.3
print(f"Ecm = {keta.ec2_ecm(fck) / 1e9:.3f} GPa")
for t0 in (7, 28, 90):
    print(f"phi(10000, {t0:2d}) = {keta.ec2_phi(10000, t0, fck, rh, h0):.4f}")
for t in (28, 365, 10000):
    print(f"shrinkage at {t:5d} days (drying from day 3): {keta.ec2_shrinkage(t, fck, rh, h0, 'N', ts=3) * 1e6:8.2f} µm/m")
print(f"age-adjusted modulus (E = 34 GPa, dphi = 2, chi = 0.8): {keta.aaem_modulus(34e9, 2.0) / 1e9:.4f} GPa")
print(f"nonlinear creep factor (|sigma| = 0.6 fcm): {keta.nonlinear_creep_factor(-0.6 * 43e6, 43e6):.4f}")

# Relaxation of a strand stressed to 0.75 fpk (class 2, rho1000 = 2.5 %) and time-dependent loss
fpk = 1860e6
for th in (1000, 5e5):
    r = keta.ec2_relaxation_ratio(th, 0.75 * fpk, fpk, 2, 2.5)
    print(f"EC2 relaxation at {th:8.0f} h: {100 * r:.3f} % of the initial stress")
print(f"BPEL relaxation at 3000 days under 1400 MPa: {keta.bpel_relaxation(3000, 1400e6, fpk, 2.5) / 1e6:.3f} MPa")
dp = keta.ec2_deferred_loss(-3e-4, 195e9, 60e6, 34e9, 2.0, 8e6, 3e-3, 0.5, 0.05, 0.3)
print(f"time-dependent loss (Eq. 5.46): {dp / 1e6:.3f} MPa")

# Coded law: time functions of a code law
law = keta.concrete_law("EC2", fc=fck, rh=rh, h_mm=300, cement="N")
print(f"EC2 law: E(28) = {law.E(28) / 1e9:.3f} GPa, E_ref(28) = {law.E_ref(28) / 1e9:.3f} GPa, "
      f"phi(10000, 28) = {law.phi(10000, 28):.4f}, J(10000, 28) = {law.compliance(10000, 28) * 1e12:.3f} 1/TPa")
t = keta.log_time_grid(28, 0.01, 1e4, 60)
eps = keta.volterra_strain(law, t, [-10e6] * len(t))
print(f"constant stress -10 MPa: final strain {eps[-1] * 1e6:.2f} µm/m "
      f"(= sigma J: {-10e6 * law.compliance(t[-1], 28) * 1e6:.2f})")
R = keta.relaxation_function(law, 28.0, t)
print(f"relaxation under a unit strain imposed at 28 days: R(28) = {R[0] / 1e9:.3f} GPa, "
      f"R(10028) = {R[-1] / 1e9:.3f} GPa")
ch = keta.fit_kelvin_chain(law, 28.0)
print(f"fitted Kelvin chain: {len(ch['tau'])} units, maximum deviation {100 * ch['max_rel_error']:.3f} %")

7.4.5 Affinity coefficients#

Each term of a coded law can be multiplied by an affinity coefficient, passed like the other parameters to set_concrete_law or to keta.concrete_law:

ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
k_modulus—1instantaneous modulus
k_shrinkage_autogenous, k_shrinkage_drying—1autogenous shrinkage, drying shrinkage
k_creep_basic, k_creep_drying—1basic creep, drying creep
k_creep_kinetics—1kinetics of creep
k_*_kinetics—1kinetics of each term (laws of EN 1992-1-1:2027): the age is divided by the coefficient in the time function of the term

These coefficients are used to calibrate a law on measurements or on the requirements of a contract. A misspelt keyword is refused.

7.4.6 Modulus at the loading age in staged construction#

By default, each increment of a staged construction analysis uses the modulus e of the material. The parameter modulus of phased (Chapter 13) gives instead the instantaneous modulus at the age of the concrete of the bar, at the date of the stage: m.phased(phases, modulus=lambda bar, age: law.E(age)).
