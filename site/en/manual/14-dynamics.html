Chapter 14 Dynamics

14.1 Masses#

The mass matrix is consistent: it includes the distributed mass of the bars (ρ·A, with the rotary inertia of the sections), of the shells and of the continuum elements, computed from the density of the materials, and the point masses (add_point_mass, Section 6.12), as well as the nodal mass matrices and the added line masses (Section 6.12.1). A material without density does not contribute to the mass. Loads are not converted into masses: a superstructure mass is declared by a suitable density, by added line masses or by point masses.

14.2 Mode shapes#

Syntax
m.modal(n_modes=6, method='auto', f_max=None, mass_ratio=None, increment=10)  →  Modal
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
n_modes—6number of modes computed (the lowest frequencies)
method—'auto''dense' (full solution, small models), 'lanczos' (Lanczos iterations, large models) or 'auto' (choice according to size)
f_maxHzNoneall the modes of frequency below f_max; their number is first counted by a Sturm sequence
mass_ratio—Noneenough modes for each translation direction carrying mass to reach this fraction of its total mass (0.9: 90 %), the number of modes growing by increment

The modes solve Kφ=ω2Mφ. A check confirms that no mode has been missed below the last frequency returned, including where frequencies are repeated (square or circular sections, rings, symmetric structures): the number of eigenvalues lying below that frequency is counted by a Sturm sequence and compared with the number of modes found. A free structure, without supports, is accepted: its six rigid-body modes come first, at 0 Hz. A RuntimeWarning reports: a Sturm count that finds more eigenvalues than modes obtained (with n_modes as with f_max), eigenpairs rejected for too large a residual, or a mass fraction mass_ratio not reached. A number n_modes larger than the number of free degrees of freedom is reduced to that number without a warning.

The Modal object provides:

ItemResult
frequencies, omegas, periodsfrequencies [Hz], circular frequencies [rad/s], periods [s]
n_modes, node_idsnumber of modes, node identifiers
mode(k)shape of mode k (0 for the first): [(node, (6 components)), …], normalised to a unit maximum amplitude
mode_displacement(k, node)components of mode k at a node
participation()participation factors per mode and per direction (dx … rz)
effective_mass()effective masses per mode and per direction [kg, kg·m²]
total_mass()total mobilisable mass per direction
mass_ratio()ratios effective mass / total mass per mode and per direction
Exampleexamples/manual/ex_modes.py
# Mode shapes: simply supported beam of 20 m (distributed mass), then addition of a point mass.
import math
import keta

L, E, I, rho, A = 20.0, 35e9, 0.5, 2500.0, 1.5

def make_beam(mass=0.0):
    m = keta.Model()
    nd = [m.add_node(L * i / 20, 0, 0) for i in range(21)]
    sec = m.add_section_props(sx=A, iy=I, iz=2.0, ix=0.3)
    mat = m.add_material(e=E, nu=0.2, rho=rho)
    for i in range(20):
        m.add_beam(nd[i], nd[i + 1], sec, mat)
    m.add_support(nd[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True, rz=True)
    m.add_support(nd[-1], dy=True, dz=True, rz=True)
    for x in nd[1:-1]:
        m.add_support(x, dx=True, dy=True, rx=True, rz=True)   # bending in the XZ plane only
    if mass:
        m.add_point_mass(nd[10], mass)
    return m, nd

m, nd = make_beam()
mo = m.modal(n_modes=4)
print(mo)
mu = rho * A
for k, f in enumerate(mo.frequencies, start=1):
    fth = k * k * math.pi / (2 * L * L) * math.sqrt(E * I / mu)
    print(f"mode {k}: f = {f:8.4f} Hz  T = {mo.periods[k - 1]:.4f} s  (Euler-Bernoulli: {fth:8.4f} Hz)")
print("total mass per direction [t]:", [round(x / 1e3, 3) for x in mo.total_mass()[:3]])
for k, r in enumerate(mo.mass_ratio(), start=1):
    print(f"mode {k}: effective mass along Z = {mo.effective_mass()[k - 1][2] / 1e3:8.3f} t ({100 * r[2]:6.2f} %)")
x, phi = mo.mode(0)[10]
print(f"mode shape 1 at node {x} (unit peak amplitude): uz = {phi[2]:.4f}")

m2, nd2 = make_beam(mass=20e3)
f2 = m2.modal(n_modes=1, method="dense").frequencies[0]
print(f"with a mass of 20 t at midspan: f1 = {f2:.4f} Hz")
Output

14.2.1 Substructuring and rotating structures#

Syntax
m.modal_substructured(substructures, masters=[], n_modes=6)  →  dict
m.modal_rotating(omega, axis=(1, 0, 0), point=(0, 0, 0), n_modes=6, stiffening=True,
                 softening=True, lumped=False)  →  Modal

modal_substructured condenses substructures (dict(bars=[…], shells=[…], n_modes=0)) on their boundary nodes (Guyan condensation, supplemented by n_modes fixed-interface modes according to Craig-Bampton); masters designates retained nodes. It returns dict(modal, n_boundary, n_reduced, fixed_interface_hz). modal_rotating computes the modes of a structure rotating at speed ω [rad/s] about an axis (centrifugal prestress and spin softening).

14.3 Damping#

The modal superposition analyses (spectral, harmonic, power spectral density) accept at most one of the following three damping parameters:

ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
zeta—0.02 or 0.05single critical damping ratio for all the modes (default 0.05 for the spectral analysis, 0.02 otherwise)
zetas——list of the damping ratios, mode by mode
rayleigh——pair (α, β) of the Rayleigh damping C = α M + β K; ζi = α / (2 ωi) + β ωi / 2

14.4 Spectral analysis#

Syntax
m.response_spectrum(spectrum, dir=0, zeta=None, zetas=None, rayleigh=None, rule='srss',
                    n_modes=0, table_zeta=0.0, direction=None, duration=20.0)  →  Spectrum
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
spectrums, m/s²—pseudo-acceleration response spectrum: list of (period, Sa), increasing periods, linear interpolation
dir—0direction of the excitation: component 0 to 5 (0: X, 1: Y, 2: Z)
direction—Nonearbitrary direction (vx, vy, vz) of the excitation, replacing dir
rule—'srss'modal combination: 'srss' (square root of the sum of squares), 'cqc' (complete quadratic combination), '10%', 'double_sum' (double sum), 'abs' (sum of absolute values), 'nrl'
durations20duration of the strong motion of the earthquake, read by the 'double_sum' rule
n_modes—0number of modes; 0: all the modes of the model (to be set for a large model)
table_zeta—0damping for which the spectrum is given; if it is non-zero, the spectrum is corrected for the modal damping by the coefficient η of EN 1998-1

The Spectrum object provides frequencies(), omegas(), zetas(), participation(), effective_mass(), total_mass(), combined_disp(node) (combined displacement, positive values), modal_disp(k, node) (contribution of mode k), base_shear() (combined base shear force) and modal_base_shear().

The spectrum is a plain table: a code spectrum is built in Python from its formulas, as in the example below (horizontal design spectrum of EN 1998-1 form). The response spectrum of an accelerogram is obtained by:

Syntax
keta.response_spectrum_of(accel, dt, periods, zeta=0.05)  →  [Sa, …]
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
accelm/s²—ground accelerations at constant time step
dts—time step
periodss—periods of computation
zeta—0.05damping of the oscillator
Exampleexamples/manual/ex_spectre.py
# Response spectrum analysis: 3-storey frame with rigid floors, horizontal design
# spectrum of the EN 1998-1 type (built in Python), SRSS and CQC combinations.
import keta

def design_spectrum(ag, S=1.2, TB=0.15, TC=0.5, TD=2.0, q=1.5, beta=0.2):
    """Design spectrum (m/s2) tabulated between 0 and 4 s."""
    pts = []
    for i in range(401):
        T = 0.01 * i
        if T <= TB:
            sa = ag * S * (2 / 3 + T / TB * (2.5 / q - 2 / 3))
        elif T <= TC:
            sa = ag * S * 2.5 / q
        elif T <= TD:
            sa = max(ag * S * 2.5 / q * TC / T, beta * ag)
        else:
            sa = max(ag * S * 2.5 / q * TC * TD / T**2, beta * ag)
        pts.append((T, sa))
    return pts

m = keta.Model()
concrete = m.add_material(e=30e9, nu=0.2, rho=0.0)             # mass carried by the floors
col = m.add_section_rect(0.4, 0.4)
pts = [[m.add_node(x, 0, 3.0 * k) for x in (0.0, 6.0)] for k in range(4)]
for k in range(3):
    for j in range(2):
        m.add_beam(pts[k][j], pts[k + 1][j], col, concrete)
for j in range(2):
    m.fix(pts[0][j])
for k in range(1, 4):
    m.add_rigid_link(pts[k][0], [pts[k][1]], dy=False, dz=False, rx=False, ry=False, rz=False)  # floor
    for j in range(2):
        m.add_support(pts[k][j], dy=True, rx=True, ry=True, rz=True)   # plane frame XZ, infinitely stiff beams
        m.add_point_mass(pts[k][j], 30e3)

mo = m.modal(n_modes=3)
print("periods [s]:", [round(T, 4) for T in mo.periods])
sp = design_spectrum(ag=2.0)
for rule in ("srss", "cqc"):
    r = m.response_spectrum(spectrum=sp, dir=0, rule=rule, zeta=0.05, n_modes=3)
    print(f"{rule.upper():4}: base shear {r.base_shear() / 1e3:8.2f} kN, "
          f"top displacement {r.combined_disp(pts[3][0])[0] * 1e3:7.3f} mm")
print("modal base shears [kN]:", [round(v / 1e3, 2) for v in r.modal_base_shear()])
print("effective masses [t]:", [round(x / 1e3, 3) for x in r.effective_mass()], "/",
      round(r.total_mass() / 1e3, 3))
Output
periods [s]: [0.4701, 0.1678, 0.1161]
SRSS: base shear   660.38 kN, top displacement  27.342 mm
CQC : base shear   660.84 kN, top displacement  27.336 mm
modal base shears [kN]: [658.14, 53.91, 6.87]
effective masses [t]: [164.534, 13.478, 1.988] / 180.0

The floors are made rigid by rigid links limited to the translation dx: the number of modes is therefore given explicitly. The SRSS and CQC rules give close results here, the periods being well separated.

14.4.1 EN 1998-1 spectra and combination of directions#

Syntax
keta.ec8_spectrum(ag, S=1.0, TB=0.15, TC=0.4, TD=2.0, q=None, zeta=0.05, beta=0.2, plateau=2.5,
                  periods=None)  →  [(T, Sa), …]
keta.combine_directional(fields, rule='srss')  →  [(6 values), …]
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
agm/s²—design ground acceleration
S, TB, TC, TD—, s—soil and spectrum shape parameters (Tables 3.2 and 3.3)
q—Nonebehaviour factor: design spectrum (Eq. 3.13 to 3.16); None: elastic spectrum
zeta—0.05damping; correction \eta = \sqrt{10/(5+100\zeta)} \ge 0.55
plateau—2.5plateau amplification; 3.0 for a vertical spectrum
fields——a list of peak values per direction
rule—'srss''srss' or '30%' (30 % rule of EN 1998-1 §4.3.3.5.2)

The spectrum is returned as a table, which is passed as is to response_spectrum. The number of modes can be fixed by a cut-off frequency, m.modal(f_max=…), or by the mass fraction to be reached in each direction, m.modal(mass_ratio=0.9) (Section 14.2).

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_spectre_ec8.py
# EN 1998-1 spectrum and modes up to a cut-off frequency.
import math
import keta

ag, S, TB, TC, TD = 2.0, 1.2, 0.15, 0.5, 2.0
spectrum = keta.ec8_spectrum(ag, S, TB, TC, TD)                # elastic, 5 %
design = keta.ec8_spectrum(ag, S, TB, TC, TD, q=1.5)           # design
eta = math.sqrt(10 / (5 + 100 * 0.05))
print(f"elastic plateau = {dict(spectrum)[0.3]:.3f} m/s2 (ag S eta 2.5 = {ag * S * eta * 2.5:.3f})")
print(f"design plateau  = {dict(design)[0.3]:.3f} m/s2 (ag S 2.5/q = {ag * S * 2.5 / 1.5:.3f})")

# pier of 12 m clamped at its base, deck mass at its top
m = keta.Model()
n = [m.add_node(0, 0, 12.0 * i / 6) for i in range(7)]
s = m.add_section_rect(b=2.0, h=2.0)
for i in range(6):
    m.add_beam(n[i], n[i + 1], s, m.concrete())
m.fix(n[0])
m.add_point_mass(n[-1], 400e3)
mo = m.modal(f_max=15.0)
print(f"{len(mo.frequencies)} modes below 15 Hz:", [round(f, 3) for f in mo.frequencies])
sp = m.response_spectrum(spectrum, dir=0, zeta=0.05, rule="cqc", n_modes=len(mo.frequencies))
print(f"base shear (X) = {sp.base_shear() / 1e3:.1f} kN")
Output

design plateau  = 4.000 m/s2 (ag S 2.5/q = 4.000)

14.5 Harmonic response#

Syntax
m.harmonic(case=0, freqs=[], zeta=None, zetas=None, rayleigh=None, n_modes=0,
           stations_per_bar=11, case_id=None)  →  Harmonic
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
case_id—Nonenumber of the load case defining the amplitude of the force F(t) = F₀ sin ωt
case—0rank of this case (0: first case by increasing number), read when case_id is not given
freqsHz—excitation frequencies swept
zeta, zetas, rayleigh—0.02damping (Section 14.3)
n_modes—0number of modes of the superposition (0: all)
stations_per_bar—11output stations for the stresses

The Harmonic object provides, for each frequency of index fi: freqs(), amplitude(node, comp) (amplitudes over the whole sweep), amplitude_at(fi, node, comp), real, imag, phase (same arguments), peak(node, comp) (frequency and amplitude of the maximum) and stress_amplitude(fi, bar, k, y, z) (amplitude of the normal stress at the point (y, z) of the output section of index k).

14.6 Random response#

Syntax
m.psd(case=0, freqs=[], zeta=None, zetas=None, rayleigh=None, n_modes=0, stations_per_bar=11,
      psd=None, psd_table=None, case_id=None)  →  Psd
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
case_id—Nonenumber of the case defining the pattern P of the force F(t) = α(t)·P
case—0rank of this case, read when case_id is not given
psd1/HzNoneconstant power spectral density of α (white noise)
psd_tableHz, 1/HzNonetabulated power spectral density [(f, S), …], linearly interpolated

The Psd object provides disp_psd(node, comp), disp_psd_at(fi, node, comp), peak_disp_psd(node, comp), disp_rms(node, comp) (standard deviation, square root of the integral of the density over the sweep), stress_psd, stress_rms(bar, k, y, z) (k: index of the output section), input_psd() and harmonic() (underlying harmonic response).

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_harmonique.py
# Harmonic and random response: beam of 20 m excited at midspan by a sinusoidal
# force of amplitude 10 kN; then power spectral density (white noise).
import math
import keta

m = keta.Model()
nd = [m.add_node(i * 1.0, 0, 0) for i in range(21)]
sec = m.add_section_props(sx=1.5, iy=0.5, iz=2.0, ix=0.3)
mat = m.add_material(e=35e9, nu=0.2, rho=2500.0)
bars = [m.add_beam(nd[i], nd[i + 1], sec, mat) for i in range(20)]
for i, x in enumerate(nd):
    m.add_support(x, dx=True, dy=True, dz=(i in (0, 20)), rx=True, rz=True)
m.add_point_load(nd[10], fz=-10e3, case=1)           # force pattern (case 1 = index 0)

f1 = m.modal(n_modes=1).frequencies[0]
w_stat = m.analyze().case(1).displacement(nd[10])[2]
freqs = [f1 * (0.5 + 0.01 * i) for i in range(101)]
h = m.harmonic(case=0, freqs=freqs, zeta=0.02, n_modes=6)
fp, ap = h.peak(nd[10], 2)
print(f"f1 = {f1:.4f} Hz; static deflection {abs(w_stat) * 1e3:.4f} mm")
print(f"peak: f = {fp:.4f} Hz, amplitude {ap * 1e3:.4f} mm, amplification {ap / abs(w_stat):.3f} "
      f"(1 / 2 zeta = {1 / 0.04:.1f} for a single oscillator)")
i = freqs.index(fp)
print(f"phase at resonance: {math.degrees(h.phase(i, nd[10], 2)):.2f} degrees")
print(f"stress at the bottom fibre at midspan: {h.stress_amplitude(i, bars[10], 0, 0.0, -0.6) / 1e6:.3f} MPa")

# Random excitation F(t) = alpha(t) x pattern, spectral density of alpha = 1 /Hz (white noise 0 to 50 Hz)
fr = [0.1 * k for k in range(1, 501)]
p = m.psd(case=0, freqs=fr, zeta=0.02, n_modes=6, psd_table=[(0.0, 1.0), (50.0, 1.0)])
fpk, spk = p.peak_disp_psd(nd[10], 2)
print(f"PSD of the deflection: peak {spk * 1e6:.4f} mm2/Hz at {fpk:.2f} Hz; standard deviation {p.disp_rms(nd[10], 2) * 1e3:.4f} mm")
print(f"standard deviation of the bottom stress at midspan: {p.stress_rms(bars[10], 0, 0.0, -0.6) / 1e6:.4f} MPa")
Output
f1 = 8.4487 Hz; static deflection 0.0952 mm
peak: f = 8.4487 Hz, amplitude 2.3465 mm, amplification 24.638 (1 / 2 zeta = 25.0 for a single oscillator)
phase at resonance: 90.03 degrees
stress at the bottom fibre at midspan: 1.218 MPa

14.7 Footbridges under pedestrian loading#

The 2006 Setra/AFGC guide (“Footbridges: assessment of vibrational behaviour of footbridges under pedestrian loading”) classifies the footbridge (I to IV according to the traffic), places each natural frequency in a resonance risk range (1 to 4), deduces from it the load cases to be studied and applies, for each mode at risk, a harmonic surface load at the frequency of the mode, whose sign follows the mode shape. The functions below return these quantities as raw values, without verdict.

Syntax
keta.setra_frequency_range(f, direction="vertical")  →  int
keta.setra_load_cases(footbridge_class, frequency_range)  →  list
keta.setra_surface_load(footbridge_class, load_case, direction, f, xi, area)  →  dict
keta.setra_comfort_range(accel, direction="vertical")  →  int
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
direction—"vertical""vertical", "longitudinal" or "lateral"
footbridge_class——class 1 to 4 or "I" to "IV"
load_case——case 1 (sparse or dense crowd), 2 (very dense), 3 (second harmonic)
fHz—natural frequency of the mode
xi——critical damping ratio of the structure
aream²—loaded area S

setra_surface_load returns the density d, the number of pedestrians N = d·S, the number of equivalent pedestrians (10.8√(ξN) for random frequencies and phases, 1.85√N for random phases only), the force F₀ of one pedestrian (280, 140 or 35 N; 70, 35 or 7 N for the second harmonic), the coefficient ψ(f) and the amplitude d·F₀·neq/N·ψ in N/m². The elementary functions are also available: setra_equivalent_pedestrians(n, xi, very_dense=False), setra_psi(f, direction, harmonic=1), setra_crowd_density(footbridge_class, load_case), setra_pedestrian_mass(density) (70 kg per pedestrian, to be added to the deck mass) and the constant SETRA_LOCK_IN_ACCEL (0.10 m/s², lateral lock-in threshold). The static crowd load of Fascicule 61 Titre II (Article 13.2) is given in N/m² by keta.fascicule61_footbridge_load(loaded_length).

Syntax
m.pedestrian_load(modal, mode, q, comp=2, bars=None, case=1, sign_cancelled=False)  →  int
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
modal——result of m.modal(...)
mode——rank of the mode (0: the first)
qN/m—amplitude of the line load (surface load × loaded width)
comp—2global direction of the load (0, 1, 2)
bars—Noneloaded bars (None: all the beams)
case—1number of the load case supplemented
sign_cancelled—FalseTrue: load applied only where the mode shape is positive

The load is q·sign(φ(x)) along each bar, where φ is the component of the mode shape in the direction comp; a bar that contains a node of the mode is split at the zero of the mode shape. The case is then driven at the frequency of the mode by m.harmonic (steady state) or by m.time_history(cases=...) with a time function cos(2πft); keta.fourier_load_factors(a0, a_cos, b_sin, period, dt, nsteps) samples such a periodic function.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_passerelle_pietons.py
# Footbridge under pedestrian action (Sétra/AFGC 2006 guide, example A.5.1.1.1): simply
# supported composite beam of 38.85 m, EI = 210e9 x 0.0292, 1456 kg/m, 2.50 m wide, xi = 0.6 %,
# class III (sparse crowd). Acceleration at resonance of the first vertical mode.
import math
import keta

L, WIDTH, XI = 38.85, 2.5, 0.006
mu = 1456.0 + keta.setra_pedestrian_mass(0.5) * WIDTH        # deck + pedestrians of class III
m = keta.Model()
mat = m.add_material(e=210e9, nu=0.3, rho=mu / 100.0)
sec = m.add_section_props(sx=100.0, iy=0.0292, iz=50.0, ix=50.0)  # fictitious A: negligible rotary inertia
nd = [m.add_node(L * i / 20, 0, 0) for i in range(21)]
for i in range(20):
    m.add_beam(nd[i], nd[i + 1], sec, mat)
m.add_support(nd[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
m.add_support(nd[20], dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)

modes = m.modal(n_modes=4)
f1 = modes.frequencies[0]
freq_range = keta.setra_frequency_range(f1, "vertical")
print(f"f1 = {f1:.3f} Hz, frequency range {freq_range}, load cases {keta.setra_load_cases('III', freq_range)}")
s = keta.setra_surface_load("III", 1, "vertical", f1, XI, L * WIDTH)
print(f"N = {s['pedestrians']:.1f} pedestrians, n_eq = {s['equivalent']:.2f}, psi = {s['psi']:.2f}, "
      f"load {s['amplitude']:.2f} N/m2")
q = s["amplitude"] * WIDTH                                     # distributed load (N/m)
m.pedestrian_load(modes, 0, q, case=1)                         # sign of the mode 1 shape
h = m.harmonic(case_id=1, freqs=[f1], zeta=XI)
acc = (2 * math.pi * f1) ** 2 * h.amplitude(nd[10], 2)[0]
print(f"acceleration at midspan: {acc:.3f} m/s2 (closed form {4 / math.pi * q / mu / (2 * XI):.3f}), "
      f"comfort range {keta.setra_comfort_range(acc)}")
Output
f1 = 2.074 Hz, frequency range 1, load cases [1]
N = 48.6 pedestrians, n_eq = 5.83, psi = 1.00, load 16.81 N/m2
acceleration at midspan: 2.888 m/s2 (closed form 2.888), comfort range 4

The guide finds 16.8 N/m² and 2.89 m/s² (range 4, discomfort). For f₁ it quotes the mean (2.08 Hz) of the frequencies when empty (2.14 Hz) and under one pedestrian per square metre (2.02 Hz); the frequency under the mass of class III is 2.074 Hz. For a single mode, the acceleration at resonance is (1/2ξ)(4/π)q/μ, which the computation recovers.

14.8 Time-history analyses#

14.8.1 Nonlinear links and earthquake#

Syntax
m.nonlinear_time_history(devices, dt, steps, cases=[], ground_dir=None, ground=None,
                         rayleigh_a=0.0, rayleigh_b=0.0, observe=[])  →  dict
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
devices——devices placed on elastic links: [dict(link, comp, law, …, C, alpha_c, v_lin)]; law = 'lrb' (k1, Qd, k2: lead rubber bearing), 'fps' (W, R, mu, dy: friction pendulum), 'bilinear' (k, fy, alpha), 'none'; C, alpha_c: viscous damper F = C·vα
dt, stepss, ——time step and number of steps
cases—[]time-dependent loads: [(case, times, values)], the case multiplied by the time function
ground_dir, ground—Nonedirection (0 to 2) and accelerogram (times, values) of the ground
rayleigh_a, rayleigh_b—0Rayleigh damping coefficients
observe—[]observed (node, component)

The integration is Newmark's (average acceleration) with Newton equilibrium at each step. The result contains t, u (one row per step, one value per observed point), the forces, deformations and work of the devices and the terms of the energy balance (input_work, kinetic, strain, rayleigh_work, device_work).

Note

The list devices may be empty: the computation is then a direct linear time-history analysis. The mass matrix must be non-singular over all the free degrees of freedom: a model whose only masses are point masses, without rotary inertia, is rejected (“singular mass”); give a density to the bars or inertias to the point masses.

14.8.2 Large displacements#

Syntax
m.large_displacement_time_history(case=1, dt=0.01, steps=100, f=None, observe=[], lumped=True,
                                  rayleigh_a=0.0, rayleigh_b=0.0, initial_velocity={}, rho_inf=-1.0,
                                  initial_state=None, rupture_step=None, rupture_bars=[],
                                  rupture_cables=[])  →  dict
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
case—1number of the load case
f—Nonetime function (times, values) multiplying the case; None: 1
lumped—Truelumped masses
initial_velocitym/s, rad/s{}initial velocities {node: (vx, vy, vz, wx, wy, wz)}
rho_inf—−1−1: average acceleration Newmark; from 0 to 1: generalised-α scheme with this spectral radius
initial_state—Nonea m.nonlinear(...) result: the motion starts from this equilibrium
rupture_step—Nonestep at which rupture_bars and rupture_cables disappear

The result contains t, u, iterations and the final displacements final. Elastic catenary cables take part with their tangent stiffness recomputed at each iteration and their mass (weight divided by g, lumped half at each end of each element); a cable structure starts from its static equilibrium (initial_state) rather than from the definition geometry, where it would fall into its sag. A rupture (rupture_step) continues the computation on the structure without the cut bars or cables, from the displacements and velocities reached; a cable that goes slack (zero tension) stops carrying load, like the exact catenary.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_temporel.py
# Time-history analyses: response spectrum of an accelerogram, oscillator on a lead-core
# bearing (nonlinear) and pendulum in large displacements.
import math
import keta

# 1) Response spectrum (pseudo-acceleration) of a damped sinusoidal signal of 2 Hz
dt = 0.005
acc = [3.0 * math.sin(2 * math.pi * 2.0 * k * dt) * math.exp(-0.3 * k * dt) for k in range(2001)]
T = [0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0]
Sa = keta.response_spectrum_of(acc, dt, T, zeta=0.05)
print("Sa [m/s2]:", ", ".join(f"T = {t} s: {s:.3f}" for t, s in zip(T, Sa)))

# 2) Mass of 200 t on a lead-rubber bearing, sinusoidal earthquake of 0.8 Hz
m = keta.Model()
ground, mass = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(0, 0, 0)
m.fix(ground)
m.add_support(mass, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True, ry=True, rz=True)
m.add_point_mass(mass, 2e5)
iso = m.add_elastic_link(ground, mass)
ts = [0.01 * k for k in range(801)]
ag = [3.0 * math.sin(2 * math.pi * 0.8 * t) if t < 5.0 else 0.0 for t in ts]
h = m.nonlinear_time_history([dict(link=iso, comp=0, law="lrb", k1=2e7, Qd=2e5, k2=1e6, C=1e5)],
                             dt=0.01, steps=800, ground_dir=0, ground=(ts, ag), observe=[(mass, 0)])
u = [x[0] for x in h["u"]]
print(f"isolator: max displacement {max(abs(x) for x in u) * 1e3:.2f} mm, "
      f"max force {max(abs(f[0]) for f in h['device_force']) / 1e3:.2f} kN")
balance = h["kinetic"] + h["strain"] + h["rayleigh_work"] + sum(h["device_work"])
print(f"input energy {h['input_work'] / 1e3:.3f} kJ = kinetic + elastic + dissipated {balance / 1e3:.3f} kJ")

# 3) Rigid pendulum of 2 m released horizontally: it passes to the other side at the half period
p = keta.Model()
a, b = p.add_node(0, 0, 0), p.add_node(2, 0, 0)
p.add_beam(a, b, p.add_section_props(1e-2, 1e-6, 1e-6, 2e-6), p.add_material(2e11, 0.3))
p.add_support(a, dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True, rz=True)
p.add_support(b, dy=True, rx=True, rz=True)
p.add_point_mass(b, 100.0)
p.add_point_load(b, fz=-981.0)
hist = p.large_displacement_time_history(1, dt=0.001, steps=1700, observe=[(b, 0)])
umin = min(x[0] for x in hist["u"])
print(f"pendulum: minimum horizontal displacement {umin:.4f} m (expected -2 L = -4 m)")
Output
Sa [m/s2]: T = 0.1 s: 3.055, T = 0.25 s: 4.697, T = 0.5 s: 15.282, T = 1.0 s: 2.346, T = 2.0 s: 0.883
isolator: max displacement 223.07 mm, max force 438.92 kN
input energy 612.365 kJ = kinetic + elastic + dissipated 612.365 kJ
pendulum: minimum horizontal displacement -4.0000 m (expected -2 L = -4 m)

The energy balance of the isolator is verified: the energy input by the earthquake equals the sum of the kinetic, elastic and dissipated energies. The rigid pendulum released horizontally reaches the symmetric position: its end moves horizontally by −2 L.

The modes about the equilibrium of a heavy cable are read on the tangent of the state (section “Saved tangent states” of the stability chapter); they follow the linear theory of Irvine and Caughey: out-of-plane modes ωL/√(H/m) = nπ, antisymmetric in-plane modes 2nπ, first symmetric mode root of tan(ω̄/2) = ω̄/2 − (4/λ²)(ω̄/2)³ with λ² = (mgL/H)² L/(H Le/EA).

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_cable_irvine.py
# Heavy cable of 100 m (40 catenaries, 10 kg/m, EA = 1e9 N), sag close to L/100: modes
# about the equilibrium (tangent of the large-displacement state) against Irvine's theory.
import math
import keta

L, N, MU, EA = 100.0, 40, 10.0, 1e9
d0 = L / 100
H0 = MU * 9.80665 * L * L / (8 * d0)
l0 = L / N * (1 + 8 / 3 * (d0 / L) ** 2 - H0 / EA)        # unstressed length of one element
m = keta.Model()
nd = [m.add_node(L * i / N, 0, 0) for i in range(N + 1)]
for i in range(N):
    m.add_cable(nd[i], nd[i + 1], ea=EA, w=MU * 9.80665, l0=l0)
m.fix(nd[0])
m.fix(nd[N])
for x in nd[1:N]:
    m.add_support(x, rx=True, ry=True, rz=True)
m.name_case(1, "self-weight")                            # empty case: the weight of the cables alone

state = m.nonlinear(case=1, substeps=20, max_iterations=80)
sag = -state.displacement(nd[N // 2])[2]
modes = state.tangent().modal(n_modes=6)
H = MU * 9.80665 * L * L / (8 * sag)                     # horizontal tension (parabola)
mu = MU * N * l0 / L                                      # mass per metre of span
scale = L / math.sqrt(H / mu)
print(f"sag {sag:.4f} m, H = {H / 1e3:.2f} kN")
for i in range(modes.n_modes):
    dy = max(abs(d[1]) for _, d in modes.mode(i))
    dz = max(abs(d[2]) for _, d in modes.mode(i))
    print(f"mode {i + 1}: f = {modes.frequencies[i]:.4f} Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = {modes.omegas[i] * scale:.3f}, "
          f"{'out-of-plane' if dy > dz else 'in-plane'}")
lam2 = (mu * 9.80665 * L / H) ** 2 * L / (H * L * (1 + 8 * (sag / L) ** 2) / EA)
lo, hi = math.pi / 2 + 1e-12, 3 * math.pi / 2 - 1e-12          # tan x = x - (4 / lambda2) x^3
for _ in range(200):
    x = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
    lo, hi = (x, hi) if math.tan(x) - x + 4 / lam2 * x ** 3 < 0 else (lo, x)
print(f"lambda2 = {lam2:.1f}; Irvine: out-of-plane n pi = 3.142, 6.283, 9.425; antisymmetric 2 pi = 6.283; "
      f"symmetric {lo + hi:.3f}")
Output
sag 1.0002 m, H = 122.56 kN
mode 1: f = 0.5534 Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = 3.141, out-of-plane
mode 2: f = 1.1055 Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = 6.274, in-plane
mode 3: f = 1.1059 Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = 6.277, out-of-plane
mode 4: f = 1.2123 Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = 6.881, in-plane
mode 5: f = 1.6567 Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = 9.403, out-of-plane
mode 6: f = 1.7363 Hz, w L / sqrt(H/m) = 9.855, in-plane
lambda2 = 52.2; Irvine: out-of-plane n pi = 3.142, 6.283, 9.425; antisymmetric 2 pi = 6.283; symmetric 6.890

The remaining deviations (at most 2.3e-3 in relative terms) come from the dispersion of the lumped-mass chain: a continuous mode ω̄ becomes 2N sin(ω̄/2N) there, N being the number of elements.

14.8.3 Linear time-history analysis#

Syntax
m.time_history(*, dt, steps, cases=[(case, times, values)], ground=None, harmonic=None, damping=None,
               scheme='average', hht_alpha=0.0, modal=None, lumped=False, observe=[(node, comp)])  →  dict
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
cases—[]each load case multiplied by its piecewise linear time function
ground—None(direction 0, 1 or 2, ground accelerations at each step): relative displacements
harmonic—None(node, component, amplitude, circular frequency)
damping—NoneNone: no damping; modal ratio or dict(per_mode=[…]) (modal superposition only); dict(rayleigh=(α, β)) (both methods)
scheme—'average''average', 'linear' (Newmark) or 'hht'
modal—Nonea m.modal(...) result: modal superposition; None: direct integration
observe—[]degrees of freedom whose history is returned

The returned dictionary contains t, u, v, a (histories per observed degree of freedom; dt and steps are given by name) and the energy balance. Two methods of the model complete the analysis: m.floor_spectrum(modal, node, comp, dir, accel, dt, periods, zeta_floor=0.05, damping=None) computes a floor spectrum (dict(accel, periods, sa)), and m.strain_energy_damping(modal, zeta=None, zeta_links=0.0) the modal damping weighted by the strain energy of materials with different damping (zeta = {material: ratio}, or None for the ratios given to the materials by add_material(damping=)). In addition, keta.high_frequency_filtered_damping(freqs, xi, f_c, d_c) adds dc to the ratios of the modes of frequency above fc (filter of the spurious high frequencies of a direct integration). strain_energy_damping and high_frequency_filtered_damping return ratios per mode, to be passed through dict(per_mode=...) to a modal superposition.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_temporel_lineaire.py
# Linear time-history analysis: force step on a cantilever with a tip mass,
# direct integration then modal superposition; combination of directions.
import math
import keta

E, I, L, M, F = 210e9, 2e-4, 3.0, 2000.0, 10e3
m = keta.Model()
a, b = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 0)
m.add_beam(a, b, m.add_section_props(sx=0.01, iy=I, iz=10 * I, ix=I), m.add_material(e=E, nu=0.3))
m.fix(a)
m.add_point_mass(b, M)
m.add_point_load(b, fz=F, case=1)
k = 3 * E * I / L**3
T = 2 * math.pi * math.sqrt(M / k)
dt, n = T / 200, 400                                     # two periods
step = [(1, [0.0, 10 * T], [1.0, 1.0])]                  # case 1 multiplied by 1 from t = 0
direct = m.time_history(dt=dt, steps=n, cases=step, observe=[(b, 2)])
modal = m.time_history(dt=dt, steps=n, cases=step, modal=m.modal(n_modes=1), observe=[(b, 2)])
for name, h in (("direct", direct), ("modal", modal)):
    print(f"{name}: peak = {max(h['u'][(b, 2)]) * 1e3:.4f} mm  (2F/k = {2 * F / k * 1e3:.4f} mm)")

# (the tenfold lateral inertia makes vertical bending the first mode)
# combination of two seismic directions (peak values per component)
x = [(1.0, 0.2, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)]
y = [(0.3, 0.8, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)]
print("SRSS:", [round(v, 4) for v in keta.combine_directional([x, y], rule="srss")[0][:2]])
print("30 %:", [round(v, 4) for v in keta.combine_directional([x, y], rule="30%")[0][:2]])
Output
direct: peak = 4.2857 mm  (2F/k = 4.2857 mm)
modal: peak = 4.2857 mm  (2F/k = 4.2857 mm)
SRSS: [1.044, 0.8246]
30 %: [1.09, 0.86]

Under a step load, the displacement peak of an undamped oscillator is twice the static deflection: both methods recover it.

14.8.4 Accidental rupture during the history#

Syntax
m.rupture_history(*, dt, steps, rupture_step, bars, cases=[(case, times, values)], damping=None,
                  scheme='average', hht_alpha=0.0, lumped=False, start_static=True,
                  observe=[(node, comp)])  →  dict
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
rupture_step——step at which the bars disappear (t = rupture_step × dt, before the last step)
bars——severed bars (beams or trusses: stay cable, hanger, prop)
cases—[]load cases and their time functions, as for time_history
damping—NoneNone or dict(rayleigh=(α, β)) (direct integration)
start_static—Truestart at the static equilibrium of the intact structure under the loads at t = 0

The computation integrates the intact structure up to the rupture, then the structure without the severed bars from the state reached: displacements and velocities are continuous, the acceleration is recomputed (the force of the severed bar leaves the structure at once). The severed bars take their mass and their own loads with them. The dictionary contains t, u, v, a and the static states static_intact and static_severed at the observed degrees of freedom. A mass that would lose all stiffness in motion is rejected.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_rupture.py
# Accidental rupture: continuous beam of 2 x 10 m (concrete, 0.4 x 0.8 m) supported at its middle by
# a prop; the prop is cut at t = 0 from the equilibrium under 20 kN/m. Midspan deflection.
import keta

m = keta.Model()
mat = m.add_material(e=3.5e10, nu=0.2, rho=2500.0)
beam_sec = m.add_section_rect(0.4, 0.8)
prop_sec = m.add_section_rect(0.3, 0.3)
nd = [m.add_node(i * 1.0, 0, 0) for i in range(21)]
bars = [m.add_beam(nd[i], nd[i + 1], beam_sec, mat) for i in range(20)]
ground = m.add_node(10.0, 0, -5.0)
prop = m.add_truss(ground, nd[10], section=prop_sec, material=mat)
m.add_support(nd[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
m.add_support(nd[20], dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
m.fix(ground)
for b in bars:
    m.add_udl(b, wz=-20e3, case=1)

T1 = 1 / m.modal(n_modes=1).frequencies[0]                 # period of the intact beam
h = m.rupture_history(dt=T1 / 200, steps=1600, rupture_step=0, bars=[prop],
                      cases=[(1, [0.0, 1e9], [1.0, 1.0])], observe=[(nd[10], 2)])
w0, w1 = h["static_intact"][(nd[10], 2)], h["static_severed"][(nd[10], 2)]
peak = min(h["u"][(nd[10], 2)])
print(f"static deflection with the prop {w0 * 1e3:.3f} mm, without the prop {w1 * 1e3:.3f} mm")
print(f"maximum deflection after rupture {peak * 1e3:.3f} mm: dynamic factor {(peak - w0) / (w1 - w0):.3f}")
Output
static deflection with the prop -0.394 mm, without the prop -70.012 mm
maximum deflection after rupture -139.431 mm: dynamic factor 1.997

For an undamped single-degree-of-freedom oscillator, the dynamic factor is 2; it is 1 + exp(−πξ/√(1 − ξ²)) with a damping ratio ξ. The beam approaches it (1.997): its first mode carries almost all the release.
