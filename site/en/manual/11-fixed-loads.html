Chapter 11 Fixed loads

Fixed loads comprise:

  • the self-weight of the structure;
  • the forces and moments applied to the nodes;
  • the concentrated, uniform and linearly varying loads on the bars;
  • the imposed displacements of the rigid supports;
  • the uniform temperature variations and the thermal gradients of the bars;
  • the surface loads distributed on bars, on shells or on continuum elements;
  • the equivalent loads of the prestressing of shells (Chapter 8).

11.1 Load cases#

All the loading methods have a parameter case (default 1) that designates the load case receiving the load. A case is created at its first load; the successive loads of the same case are added together.

Syntax
m.name_case(case, name)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
case——number of the case
name——name of the case (repeated in the representation of the results); by default “LC” followed by the number

The cases are analysed together by m.analyze(); their results are read by r.case(number), and the list of their numbers, in increasing order, is r.case_ids.

11.2 Self-weight#

Syntax
m.add_self_weight(case=1, factor=1.0, g=None)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
case—1load case
factor—1multiplying factor
gm/s²Noneacceleration vector (gx, gy, gz); None: (0, 0, −9.80665)

Applies to the whole structure (bars, shells, continuum elements) the weight of its materials, ρ·A·g per unit length for a bar. The vector applied is factor × g. The weight of an offset bar acts on its offset axis and reaches the nodes through the rigid arms, with the corresponding moment. Point masses (add_point_mass) also have weight: each receives the force mass × factor × g; nodal mass matrices and added line masses (Section 6.12.1) have no weight.

11.4.3 Linearly varying load#

Syntax
m.add_linear_load(bar, w1, w2, x1=0.0, x2=1.0, case=1, frame='global')
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
w1, w2N/m—intensities (wx, wy, wz) at the start and at the end of the loaded zone
x1, x2—0; 1relative abscissae of the start and of the end of the loaded zone
case, frame—1; 'global'as for add_udl

A partial uniform load is obtained with w1 = w2 and chosen abscissae x1, x2; a trapezoidal load with different intensities.

11.4.4 Parabolic load#

Syntax
m.add_parabolic_load(bar, w1, wmid, w2, x1=0.0, x2=1.0, case=1, frame='global', absolute=False)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
w1, wmid, w2N/m—intensities (wx, wy, wz) at the start, at the middle and at the end of the loaded zone; the load follows the parabola that passes through these three values
x1, x2, absolute— or m0; 1; Falseloaded zone, as for add_linear_load
case, frame—1; 'global'as for add_udl

The equivalent nodal forces, the forces and the deflections at the output sections are exact in linear statics. For a parabola that is zero at the ends of a cantilever and equal to q₀ at the middle, the fixed-end moment is q₀L²/3 and the tip deflection 7q₀L⁴/(90EI); on a beam fixed at both ends, the end moments are q₀L²/15. The analyses that carry only linear span loads (second order by stability functions, large displacements, exact bar on soil, warping, influence lines, plasticity of shells and fibres) refuse it and name it.

11.4.5 Axes of distributed loads#

The 'global' axes give a load per metre of bar, whatever its inclination: its resultant is w·L. The 'local' axes give components in the axes of the bar (a load normal to the bar, for example). The 'projected' axes give a load per metre of length projected on a plane perpendicular to the direction of the load: a vertical load on a bar inclined at an angle α to the horizontal has the resultant w·L·cos α; this is the case of snow, of the weight of fill on a vault or of loads defined per unit area in plan.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_charges_repere.py
# Distributed loads on an inclined bar: global, local and projected axes.
import math
import keta

alpha = math.radians(30)
L = 10.0
for frame in ("global", "local", "projected"):
    m = keta.Model()
    a = m.add_node(0, 0, 0)
    b = m.add_node(L * math.cos(alpha), 0, L * math.sin(alpha))
    bar = m.add_beam(a, b, m.add_section_rect(0.3, 0.6), m.concrete())
    m.add_support(a, dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
    m.add_support(b, dy=True, dz=True)
    m.add_udl(bar, wz=-10e3, frame=frame)             # 10 kN/m along -Z (or local -z)
    Fx, Fy, Fz = m.analyze().case(1).sum_reactions()
    print(f"{frame:9}: resultant of the reactions Fx = {round(Fx / 1e3, 3) + 0.0:7.3f} kN, Fz = {Fz / 1e3:7.3f} kN")
print(f"expected: global 10 L = {10 * L:.3f} kN; projected 10 L cos(alpha) = {10 * L * math.cos(alpha):.3f} kN; "
      f"local: 10 L normal to the bar")
Output
global   : resultant of the reactions Fx =   0.000 kN, Fz = 100.000 kN
local    : resultant of the reactions Fx = -50.000 kN, Fz =  86.603 kN
projected: resultant of the reactions Fx =   0.000 kN, Fz =  86.603 kN
expected: global 10 L = 100.000 kN; projected 10 L cos(alpha) = 86.603 kN; local: 10 L normal to the bar

L = 8.0
m = keta.Model()

m.add_support(b, dy=True, dz=True)

for c, name in [(1, "point"), (2, "triangle"), (3, "uniform"), (4, "moment")]:

import keta

L = 10.0

11.5 Imposed support displacements#

Syntax
m.add_support_disp(node, dx=None, dy=None, dz=None, rx=None, ry=None, rz=None, case=1)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
node——supported node
dx, dy, dzmNoneimposed translations (global axes)
rx, ry, rzradNoneimposed rotations
case—1load case
Important

A displacement can be imposed only on a component rigidly restrained by a support. The component must therefore have been declared by fix, pin or add_support.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_tassement.py
# Imposed support displacement: continuous beam on three supports, settlement of 10 mm of the central support.
import keta

L, EI, d = 6.0, 30e9 * 4.0e-3, -0.010
m = keta.Model()
n = [m.add_node(L * i, 0, 0) for i in range(3)]
sec = m.add_section_props(sx=0.2, iy=4.0e-3, iz=1e-3, ix=1e-3)
b = [m.add_beam(n[i], n[i + 1], sec, m.add_material(30e9, 0.2)) for i in range(2)]
m.add_support(n[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
m.add_support(n[1], dy=True, dz=True)
m.add_support(n[2], dy=True, dz=True)
m.add_support_disp(n[1], dz=d, case=1)
case_ = m.analyze().case(1)
M = case_.bar_forces(b[0])[-1][5]
R = dict(case_.reactions())[n[1]][2]
print(f"imposed displacement: {case_.displacement(n[1])[2] * 1e3:.3f} mm")
print(f"moment over the central support: {M / 1e3:.3f} kN.m (-3 EI |d| / L² = {-3 * EI * -d / L**2 / 1e3:.3f} kN.m, bottom fibre in tension)")
print(f"reaction of the central support: {R / 1e3:.3f} kN (-6 EI |d| / L³ = {-6 * EI * -d / L**3 / 1e3:.3f} kN)")
Output
imposed displacement: -10.000 mm
moment over the central support: -100.000 kN.m (-3 EI |d| / L² = -100.000 kN.m, bottom fibre in tension)
reaction of the central support: -33.333 kN (-6 EI |d| / L³ = -33.333 kN)

11.6 Thermal actions#

Syntax
m.add_thermal(bar, dt, gy=0.0, gz=0.0, case=1)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
bar——loaded bar
dtK—uniform temperature variation
gy, gzK/m0temperature gradients along the local axes y and z (dT/dy, dT/dz)
case—1load case

The uniform variation produces a free expansion α·ΔT; a gradient produces a free curvature α·g. For a bar of depth h whose top fibre (z = +h/2) is warmer by Δθ than the bottom fibre, gz = Δθ / h. The material must have a coefficient of thermal expansion (alpha_temp, Chapter 7).

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_temperature.py
# Thermal actions: uniform variation on a bar clamped at both ends, gradient on a cantilever.
import keta

E, alpha, A, h = 210e9, 1.2e-5, 0.02, 0.5
m = keta.Model()
steel = m.add_material(e=E, nu=0.3, alpha_temp=alpha)
sec = m.add_section_props(sx=A, iy=8e-4, iz=2e-4, ix=1e-4)
a, b = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(10, 0, 0)
bar = m.add_beam(a, b, sec, steel)
m.fix(a)
m.fix(b)
m.add_thermal(bar, dt=30.0, case=1)                        # +30 K uniform
N = m.analyze().case(1).bar_forces(bar)[5][1]
print(f"fixed-fixed, +30 K: N = {N / 1e3:.3f} kN (closed form -E A alpha dT = {-E * A * alpha * 30 / 1e3:.3f} kN)")

m = keta.Model()
steel = m.add_material(e=E, nu=0.3, alpha_temp=alpha)
sec = m.add_section_props(sx=A, iy=8e-4, iz=2e-4, ix=1e-4)
a, b = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(10, 0, 0)
bar = m.add_beam(a, b, sec, steel)
m.fix(a)
m.add_thermal(bar, dt=0.0, gz=20.0 / h, case=1)            # 20 K difference between fibres z = -h/2 and +h/2
ux, uy, uz, rx, ry, rz = m.analyze().case(1).displacement(b)
k = alpha * 20.0 / h                                        # free curvature
print(f"cantilever, gradient 40 K/m: uz = {uz * 1e3:.3f} mm, ry = {ry * 1e3:.4f} mrad")
print(f"closed forms: |uz| = k L²/2 = {k * 100 / 2 * 1e3:.3f} mm, |ry| = k L = {k * 10 * 1e3:.4f} mrad")
Output
fixed-fixed, +30 K: N = -1512.000 kN (closed form -E A alpha dT = -1512.000 kN)
cantilever, gradient 40 K/m: uz = -24.000 mm, ry = 4.8000 mrad
closed forms: |uz| = k L²/2 = 24.000 mm, |ry| = k L = 4.8000 mrad

11.7 Imposed strains#

Syntax
m.add_prestrain(bar, strain=0, ky=0, kz=0, case=1, kx=0)
m.add_prestrain_linear(bar, start, end, case=1, kx=(0, 0))
m.add_prestrain_parabolic(bar, start, mid, end, case=1, kx=(0, 0, 0))
m.add_prestrain_point(bar, at, dx=0, ry=0, rz=0, dy=0, dz=0, rx=0, absolute=False, case=1)
m.add_surface_thermal(slab, dt, grad=0.0, case=1)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
strain—0imposed elongation (> 0: elongation)
ky, kz1/m0imposed curvatures about the local axes y and z, opposite to the rotation gradients: ky = −dθy/dx = d²w/dx² (ky > 0 elongates the −z fibre, like a thermal gradient gz = −ky/α) and kz = −dθz/dx = −d²v/dx² (kz > 0 elongates the +y fibre, like gy = kz/α)
kxrad/m0; (0, 0)imposed rate of twist dθx/dx, the free torsion of the bar (uniform, or from the origin to the end for add_prestrain_linear); fixed at both ends, the bar carries the torsional moment −GJ times its mean
start, end——(strain, ky, kz) at the origin and at the end: linear variation
mid——(strain, ky, kz) at the middle of the bar: the parabola passing through start, mid and end (kx: its three rates of twist)
at— or m—position of a concentrated dislocation
dx, dy, dz, rx, ry, rzm, rad0discontinuities of displacement and rotation between the two faces of the cut
slab, dt, grad—, K, K/m—shells of the group slab: uniform variation and gradient through the thickness

An imposed strain is a free strain ε0​,κ0​: on a statically determinate structure it produces displacements without forces, on a statically indeterminate structure forces without having to go through an equivalent temperature. It represents a differential shrinkage, a fabrication camber, an erection misfit. The parabolic field reproduces the creep curvature φM/EI of a bar under distributed load, whose moment is parabolic: on a statically indeterminate structure with uniform creep, it redistributes nothing and the deflection is multiplied by 1+φ.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_deformations_imposees.py
# Imposed strains: uniform curvature, shortening, linear variation, dislocation.
import keta

E, A, I, L = 30e9, 0.2, 4e-3, 5.0
m = keta.Model()
a, b = m.add_node(0, 0, 0), m.add_node(L, 0, 0)
p = m.add_beam(a, b, m.add_section_props(sx=A, iy=I, iz=I, ix=I), m.add_material(e=E, nu=0.2))
m.fix(a)
k = 2e-4
m.add_prestrain(p, ky=k, case=1)                        # uniform curvature
m.add_prestrain(p, strain=-3e-4, case=2)                # uniform shortening
m.add_prestrain_linear(p, (0, 0, 0), (0, k, 0), case=3)  # curvature from 0 to k
m.add_prestrain_point(p, 0.5, ry=1e-3, case=4)          # 1 mrad kink at mid-length
r = m.analyze()
d = [r.case(c).displacement(b) for c in (1, 2, 3, 4)]
print(f"uniform curvature:  rotation {d[0][4] * 1e3:8.4f} mrad (kL = {k * L * 1e3:.4f}), "
      f"deflection {d[0][2] * 1e3:8.4f} mm (kL2/2 = {k * L * L / 2 * 1e3:.4f})")
print(f"shortening:         ux = {d[1][0] * 1e3:8.4f} mm (eps L = {-3e-4 * L * 1e3:.4f})")
print(f"linear curvature:   rotation {d[2][4] * 1e3:8.4f} mrad (kL/2 = {k * L / 2 * 1e3:.4f})")
print(f"kink at mid-length: deflection {d[3][2] * 1e3:8.4f} mm (1e-3 L/2 = {1e-3 * L / 2 * 1e3:.4f})")

# same strains on a bar clamped at both ends: forces, no displacement
m.fix(b)
st = m.analyze().case(2).bar_forces(p)[5]
print(f"restrained shortening: N = {st[1] / 1e3:.1f} kN (-E A eps = {E * A * 3e-4 / 1e3:.1f})")
Output
uniform curvature:  rotation  -1.0000 mrad (kL = 1.0000), deflection   2.5000 mm (kL2/2 = 2.5000)
shortening:         ux =  -1.5000 mm (eps L = -1.5000)
linear curvature:   rotation  -0.5000 mrad (kL/2 = 0.5000)
kink at mid-length: deflection   2.5000 mm (1e-3 L/2 = 2.5000)
restrained shortening: N = 1800.0 kN (-E A eps = 1800.0)

11.8 Surface loads on bars#

Syntax
m.add_area_load(polygon, qx=0.0, qy=0.0, qz=0.0, mode='two_way', span=(1.0, 0.0, 0.0), case=1)  →  int
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
polygonm—vertices (x, y, z) of a convex plane panel bounded by bars
qx, qy, qzPa0surface load (global vector)
mode—'two_way''two_way': distribution to the nearest edge (45° lines); 'one_way': strips parallel to span, carried by the bars at the ends of the strips
span—(1, 0, 0)span direction of the 'one_way' mode

The load of the panel is converted into linear loads on the bars of its boundary (tributary area). The method returns the number of bar loads created.

11.9 Loads on shells#

Syntax
m.add_surface_pressure(slab, qx=0.0, qy=0.0, qz=0.0, frame='global', case=1)
m.add_polygon_pressure(polygon, qx=0.0, qy=0.0, qz=0.0, holes=[], slab=0, case=1)
m.add_hydrostatic(gamma, surface_point, slab=0, side=1.0, down=(0, 0, -1), case=1)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
slab—— / 0loaded group of shells (0: all the shells)
qx, qy, qzPa0pressure; for add_surface_pressure in 'normal' axes, qz is the pressure along the normal of each facet
frame—'global''global' (vector per unit area), 'normal' (along the normal), 'projected' (per unit area in plan)
polygon, holesm—boundary of the loaded polygon and boundaries of the openings, in plan; the pressure is given per unit projected area
gammaN/m³—unit weight of the fluid
surface_pointm—a point of the free surface
side—1+1: pressure along the normal of the facets, −1: in the opposite direction
down—(0, 0, −1)direction of depth

The hydrostatic pressure is γ times the depth below the free surface, zero above; the facets cut by the free surface are integrated over their wetted part.

# Area loads: load distribution zone on a panel of beams, polygonal
# pressure and hydrostatic pressure on shells.
import keta

# 1) Floor of 6 m x 4 m bordered by four beams: 5 kN/m2 distributed to the beams
for mode in ("two_way", "one_way"):
    m = keta.Model()
    c = [m.add_node(x, y, 0) for x, y in ((0, 0), (6, 0), (6, 4), (0, 4))]
    sec, mat = m.add_section("IPE300"), m.steel()
    beams = [m.add_beam(c[i], c[(i + 1) % 4], sec, mat) for i in range(4)]
    for nd in c:
        m.pin(nd)
    nb = m.add_area_load([(0, 0, 0), (6, 0, 0), (6, 4, 0), (0, 4, 0)], qz=-5e3, mode=mode, span=(0, 1, 0))
    case_ = m.analyze().case(1)
    M = [abs(case_.bar_forces(p)[5][5]) / 1e3 for p in beams]
    print(f"{mode:8}: {nb} bar loads, total = {case_.sum_reactions()[2] / 1e3:.2f} kN, "
          f"midspan M of the beams = {', '.join(f'{x:.2f}' for x in M)} kN.m")

# 2) Vertical wall of 3 m x 2.5 m (shells), clamped at the base, retaining 2.5 m of water
m = keta.Model()
concrete = m.add_material(e=30e9, nu=0.2)
g = [[m.add_node(0.5 * i, 0.0, 0.5 * j) for i in range(7)] for j in range(6)]
for j in range(5):
    for i in range(6):
        m.add_shell(g[j][i], g[j][i + 1], g[j + 1][i + 1], 0.25, concrete, slab=1)
        m.add_shell(g[j][i], g[j + 1][i + 1], g[j + 1][i], 0.25, concrete, slab=1)
for nd in g[0]:
    m.fix(nd)
m.add_hydrostatic(gamma=9810.0, surface_point=(0.0, 0.0, 2.5), slab=1, case=1)
Fy = m.analyze().case(1).sum_reactions()[1]
print(f"hydrostatic thrust: |Fy| = {abs(Fy) / 1e3:.3f} kN (gamma h² b / 2 = {9810 * 2.5**2 * 3 / 2 / 1e3:.3f} kN)")

# 3) Pressure on a polygon with an opening, applied in plan on a horizontal slab
m = keta.Model()
concrete = m.add_material(e=30e9, nu=0.2)
g = [[m.add_node(1.0 * i, 1.0 * j, 0.0) for i in range(7)] for j in range(5)]
for j in range(4):
    for i in range(6):
        m.add_shell(g[j][i], g[j][i + 1], g[j + 1][i + 1], 0.2, concrete, slab=1)
        m.add_shell(g[j][i], g[j + 1][i + 1], g[j + 1][i], 0.2, concrete, slab=1)
for nd in g[0] + g[-1]:
    m.pin(nd)
outline = [(0.5, 0.5, 0), (5.5, 0.5, 0), (5.5, 3.5, 0), (0.5, 3.5, 0)]
opening = [(2.0, 1.5, 0), (3.0, 1.5, 0), (3.0, 2.5, 0), (2.0, 2.5, 0)]
m.add_polygon_pressure(outline, qz=-4e3, holes=[opening], case=1)
Fz = m.analyze().case(1).sum_reactions()[2]
print(f"polygonal pressure: Fz = {Fz / 1e3:.3f} kN (4 kN/m2 x (15 - 1) m2 = 56.000 kN)")

11.10 Loads on continuum elements#

Syntax
m.add_continuum_traction(element, face, traction, case=1)
m.add_continuum_pressure(element, face, p, case=1)
m.add_continuum_thermal(element, T, T0=0.0, case=1)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
element——continuum element
face——side of a plane element (from vertex face to the next) or face of a hexahedron (0: bottom, 1: top, then η = −1, ξ = +1, η = +1, ξ = −1)
tractionPa—vector (tx, ty, tz) of the force per unit area (per radian for an axisymmetric element)
pPa—pressure, positive when it pushes towards the inside of the element
TK—nodal temperatures {node: T}, for example from conduction (Chapter 18)
T0K0reference temperature

The equivalent nodal forces are computed by integration consistent with the shape functions. The example of Section 6.15 applies a face traction.
