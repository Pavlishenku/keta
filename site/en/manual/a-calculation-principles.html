Appendix A Calculation principles

This appendix summarises the methods used by the kernel. The manual keta — Calculation Principles (docs/keta_calculation_principles.pdf) sets them out in detail, with their equations, their assumptions and their limits.

A.1 Beam element#

The beam is a straight two-node element with six degrees of freedom per node. Its stiffness is that of Timoshenko theory: in bending about z, for example, the shear parameter Φ = 12 E Iz / (G Avy L²) corrects the Euler-Bernoulli matrix; a zero shear area (“rigid” section) gives Φ = 0. Torsion is uniform (Saint-Venant, G J), except in warping analyses (Section 16.8). Hinges are introduced by static condensation of the released components; offsets by a rigid-body transformation between the nodes and the ends of the bar.

Span loads are transformed into nodal forces through their fixed-end forces. After solution, the internal forces at the output sections are recomputed by integrating the equilibrium of the bar from its end forces: they are exact between the nodes, whatever the fineness of the subdivision.

A.2 Assembly and solution#

The global stiffness matrix is assembled in sparse form; restrained degrees of freedom and imposed displacements are eliminated directly, rigid links and linear relations by master-slave reduction. The system is solved by a sparse LDLᵀ factorisation (supernodal above 10,000 unknowns) after reordering of the unknowns. All the load cases of a static analysis are solved with the same factorisation. Reactions are computed from the forces of the elements connected to the supported nodes.

A.3 Elastic foundation#

In linear statics, a frame bar without end release or shear area resting on a Winkler foundation receives the analytical solution of the beam on elastic foundation, which does not depend on the mesh. In the other cases (Timoshenko bar or bar with releases, modal, buckling or nonlinear analyses), the foundation adds to the stiffness of the bar the consistent matrix ∫ Nᵀ k N dx built with the cubic bending shape functions, which converges to the same solution as the bar is subdivided (Section 6.10). The zone-based calculation (pse) uses, with the option exact, the exact solution of the equation EI w⁗ + k w = q on each zone.

A.4 Shells and continuum elements#

The shell facet combines the Allman membrane (in-plane rotations, stabilisation of the single spurious mode) and Mindlin-Reissner bending through the MITC3 element, free of shear locking for thin shells. The continuum elements are isoparametric, integrated by Gauss rules, exact for straight-sided triangles and tetrahedra (2 × 2 reduced integration for the Q8).

A.5 Second order and large displacements#

The second-order analysis adds to the stiffness the consistent geometric matrix of the axial forces (K + Kg(N)) u = F, solved by iterations until N is stable. The large-displacement analysis uses a corotational formulation: each element is followed in a frame that accompanies its rigid-body motion, the local response remaining linear (small strains). Equilibrium is obtained by Newton's method; the arc-length method adds a constraint equation on the norm of the displacement increment to pass limit points. The number of negative pivots of the tangent stiffness signals the critical points.

A.6 Buckling#

Linear buckling solves the generalised eigenvalue problem (K + λ Kg) φ = 0, Kg being built with the axial forces of the reference case. The smallest positive values are obtained by Lanczos iterations, or by a full solution for small models.

A.7 Dynamics#

The mode shapes solve K φ = ω² M φ (consistent mass matrix). The participation factor of mode i in direction d is Γi,d = φiᵀ M rd / (φiᵀ M φi) and its effective mass Γi,d² φiᵀ M φi. Response spectrum analysis combines the maximum modal responses by the chosen rule; the complete quadratic combination uses the Der Kiureghian correlation coefficients. The harmonic and random responses are obtained by modal superposition with the defined modal damping. Time-history analyses integrate the equations of motion by the average-acceleration Newmark scheme (or HHT-α; generalised α in large displacements), with Newton equilibrium at each step when they are nonlinear.

A.8 Cables#

The cable is the Irvine elastic catenary: for an unstressed length L₀, an axial stiffness EA and a weight w, the horizontal and vertical projections of the chord are expressed in closed form as functions of the components H and V of the tension at one end. The corresponding 2 × 2 flexibility is inverted to obtain the tangent stiffness used in the equilibrium iterations coupled with the frame.

A.9 Influence lines and surfaces#

The influence line of a response R = cᵀ u is obtained by the adjoint method: K v = c is solved once, then the ordinate of the line at a point is the work of the unit load in the field v (taking account of the fixed-end forces for points located inside the bars). The placement functions sweep the positions where the line changes slope and choose the loaded zones according to the sign of the line.

A.10 Staged construction, creep and prestressing#

Staged construction accumulates increments computed on the structure active at each instant. Between two times t₁ and t₂, the creep strain increment of a fibre that has undergone the stress increments Δσj at ages tj is, with the exact method:

Δεfl = Σj Δσj · [φ(t₂, tj) − φ(t₁, tj)] / Eref(tj)

to which the shrinkage increment is added. This strain is imposed on the active structure, whose response redistributes the internal forces. The interval is divided into logarithmic sub-steps to follow the variation of the stresses. Prestressing is introduced through its equivalent loads; the elastic and time-dependent losses (shrinkage, creep at the level of the tendon, relaxation) are computed at each increment and reintroduced into the structure.

A.11 Fire analysis#

The temperature in the section is obtained by finite volumes of transient heat conduction (grid of square cells, implicit Euler scheme), with the temperature-dependent conductivity, specific heat and density of the concrete (EN 1992-1-2) and convection and radiation conditions on the exposed faces. For each bar, the strain plane that balances its internal forces is sought on the hot fibre section; the resulting secant stiffnesses and thermal strains are reintroduced into the frame, until the displacements stabilise.
