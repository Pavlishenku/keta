Chapter 3 Units, axes and sign conventions

3.1 Units#

keta works exclusively in the International System. All data are supplied and all results are returned in the following units, without implicit conversion:

QuantityUnitExamples
length, coordinate, displacementmthickness 0.25 m; deflection −0.0124 m
angle, rotationradbeta=math.pi / 2; math.radians(30)
forceN10 kN is written 10e3
momentN·m40 kN·m is written 40e3
distributed load per unit lengthN/m25 kN/m is written 25e3
pressure, stress, modulusPaE = 210 GPa is written 210e9; 5 kN/m² is written 5e3
densitykg/m³concrete 2500
mass, mass moment of inertiakg, kg·m²point mass of 20 t: 20e3
temperature, gradientK (or °C for fire), K/mΔT = 30 K
times; days for staged construction and creep; minutes for firestage date 28 (days)
frequencyHz; angular frequency in rad/s
spring stiffnessN/m; N·m/rad
subgrade reaction modulus (elastic foundation)N/m per m (= Pa)soil modulus × width

The acceleration of gravity is g = 9.80665 m/s². The self-weight is computed from the density of the materials; a zero density gives a material without mass and without weight.

Note

Python scientific notation (3.5e-3, 210e9) avoids errors in the power of ten; the _ separators are also accepted (10_000).

3.2 Global axes#

The global coordinate system is a right-handed orthonormal system (O, X, Y, Z). The Z axis is vertical, pointing upwards: gravity acts along −Z. Node coordinates, nodal loads, nodal displacements and support reactions are expressed in this system.

A plane structure is described in a plane of the global system, by restraining the out-of-plane degrees of freedom (Section 3.4); there is no separate plane analysis option.

3.3 Local axes of bars#

Each bar carries a local coordinate system (x, y, z):

  • the x axis lies along the bar, from the origin node n1 to the end node n2;
  • for a non-vertical bar, the y axis is horizontal (y = Z ∧ x, normalised) and the z axis, lying in the vertical plane that contains the bar, points upwards (z = x ∧ y). For a bar parallel to the X axis of the global system, the local and global axes coincide;
  • for a vertical bar (component of x along Z greater than 0.99999 in absolute value), the y axis is the global Y axis and z = x ∧ y. For a column pointing upwards, z = −X;
  • the angle beta (parameter of add_beam) then rotates the y and z axes about x, in the positive (right-handed) sense.

The y and z axes are the principal axes of the section: Iy is the bending second moment of area about y (bending in the xz plane), Iz the second moment of area about z (bending in the xy plane). For a rectangular section add_section_rect(b, h), the width b is measured along y and the depth h along z: a horizontal rectangular beam is therefore bent vertically through its strong-axis second moment of area b h³/12.

Important

The rule for choosing the local axes changes in the vicinity of the vertical. A nearly vertical bar (slightly inclined by a geometric imperfection, for example) receives a horizontal y axis perpendicular to its projection, whereas a vertical bar receives y = Y. In a column in which some bars are vertical and others very slightly inclined, the second moments of area Iy and Iz can then be swapped from one bar to the next. For a column with an unsymmetrical section whose geometry is perturbed along Y, the section is oriented by beta so that the bending under study occurs along X (example in Chapter 16).

3.4 Degrees of freedom#

Each node has six degrees of freedom, expressed in the global system and identified by a component index from 0 to 5:

This order (dx, dy, dz, rx, ry, rz) is that of all the tuples of displacements, reactions and nodal loads, and of the index comp of the functions that target a component (influence lines, adjustments, time-history analyses, etc.).

Unused degrees of freedom are restrained automatically: rotations of nodes that carry only truss bars, rotations and out-of-plane translation of the nodes of plane continuum elements. For a plane bar structure, the out-of-plane degrees of freedom are restrained explicitly by add_support (for example dy, rx and rz for a structure in the XZ plane).

3.5 Internal forces and stresses#

The internal forces at the output stations of a bar are expressed in its local axes. They are the forces exerted on the part of the bar located on the origin side by the part located on the end side (forces on the face with normal +x):

  • N, axial force, positive in tension;
  • Vy, Vz, shear forces, components along y and z of the force on the +x face (Vz = dMy/dx);
  • T, torsional moment about x;
  • My, bending moment about y, positive when it puts the fibre z > 0 in tension;
  • Mz, bending moment about z, positive when it compresses the fibre y > 0.

The normal stress at a point (y, z) of the section is:

σ = N / A + My · z / Iy − Mz · y / Iz

For a horizontal beam loaded downwards (−Z), whose local z axis is vertical upwards, the span moment is negative (bottom fibre in tension) and the moment over an intermediate support is positive (top fibre in tension).

Note

The end forces end_forces (12 components) are, by contrast, the forces and moments exerted by the nodes on the bar, in the local axes and in the order (Fx, Fy, Fz, Mx, My, Mz) at the origin node then at the end node. At the origin, they are of opposite sign to the internal forces defined above; at the end, they are of the same sign.

3.6 Reactions and loads#

Nodal loads and support reactions are expressed in the global system, positive in the direction of the axes. A reaction is the force exerted by the support on the structure: for a beam carrying a downward load, the vertical reaction is positive. The sum of the reactions balances the sum of the applied loads.

Distributed loads on bars are given in the global axes (default), in the local axes of the bar or in projection (Chapter 11). Imposed support displacements are expressed in the global system.
