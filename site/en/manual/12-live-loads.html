Chapter 12 Live loads

12.1 Principle#

Live loads (vehicles, convoys, distributed traffic loads, crowd) are moving: their most unfavourable position depends on the quantity studied. keta treats them by influence lines and influence surfaces. For a given response (displacement, reaction, force at a section), the influence line η(s) is the value of the response when a unit load, of given direction, is at the abscissa s of the running path. The value of a response under a set of loads Pi at the abscissae si and of distributed loads q(s) is then:

R = Σ Pi · η(si) + ∫ q(s) · η(s) ds

The placement functions seek the position (and, for distributed loads, the extent) that makes R maximum or minimum. All the lines are computed by the adjoint method: a single factorisation of the structure gives the complete line.

The loads of the code models are supplied in the form of convoys, densities or wheel sets (Sections 12.4 to 12.8); an arbitrary convoy is described directly in Python.

12.2 Influence lines#

Syntax
m.influence_line(bars, response, dir=(0, 0, -1), per_bar=11)  →  InfluenceLine
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
bars——running path: ordered list of contiguous bars; the abscissa s starts at the origin of the first bar
response——response studied, dictionary (Table 12.1)
dir—(0, 0, −1)direction of the moving unit load (global axes); by default vertical downwards
per_bar—11number of calculation points per bar
Table 12.1 — Responses studied
DictionaryResponse
dict(kind='displacement', node=n, comp=c)displacement of node n, component c (0 to 5)
dict(kind='reaction', node=n, comp=c)reaction of the support of node n
dict(kind='bar_force', bar=b, x=x, comp=c)force in bar b at the distance x [m] from its origin; c = 0 to 5 for N, Vy, Vz, T, My, Mz
dict(kind='bar_displacement', bar=b, x=x, comp=c)displacement of bar b at the distance x

The InfluenceLine object returned has the following attributes and methods:

ItemRole
s, etaabscissae [m] and ordinates (response per newton) of the points computed
at(s)ordinate at an arbitrary point (zero outside the path)
integral(a, b)integral of η over [a, b]
integral_pos(), integral_neg()areas of the positive and negative zones
zones(sign=1)zones of one sign: list of (start, end, area)
point_extreme()(max, min) under a single unit load
InfluenceLine.from_samples(s, eta)piecewise linear line defined point by point (known line, check)
Note

With the default direction (downward load) and the conventions of Chapter 3, the line of the midspan moment of a beam is negative (span moment that puts the bottom fibre in tension) and the line of a vertical reaction is positive. The extremes to be retained are therefore read according to the sign: the “min” of a span moment is the usual positive moment.

12.3 Convoys and distributed loads#

A convoy is a dictionary dict(axles=[(offset, load), …], segments=[(start, length, total), …]): point axles located by their offset [m] relative to the first one, and uniform distributed loads defined by their start, their length and their total load [N]. The functions of the code models return this format; it can also be written directly.

Syntax
keta.convoy_extreme(line, convoy, steps=500)  →  (max, min, s_max, s_min)
keta.udl_extreme(line, q)  →  (q·A⁺, q·A⁻, 0, 0)
keta.multi_vehicle_extreme(line, vehicles, udl=0.0, steps=200)  →  (max, min, positions_max, positions_min)
keta.intensity_udl_extreme(line, density, sign=1)  →  (value, loaded length, zones)
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
line——influence line
convoy——convoy (dictionary above), run in one direction only; for the other direction, change the sign of the offsets
steps—500number of positions tried
qN/m—free distributed load, applied over all the zones of the same sign
vehicles——list of vehicles dict(axles, occ_back, occ_front, rem_back, rem_front): axles, rear and front clearances [m], and lengths over which the concomitant distributed load is removed
udlN/m0concomitant distributed load, removed under the vehicles
density——Python function q(L) of the loaded length L [m] → density [N/m]
sign—1sign of the loaded zones (+1 or −1)

convoy_extreme slides the convoy along the line (axles outside the path have no effect) and returns the extremes and the corresponding position of the reference of the convoy (the point of zero offset). multi_vehicle_extreme places several vehicles without overlap and gives the optimum on the grid of positions. intensity_udl_extreme treats loads whose intensity depends on the loaded length: it selects the most unfavourable subset of zones.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_ligne_influence.py
# Influence lines: continuous bridge with three spans 25 + 30 + 25 m, moment over a pier
# and moment at the middle of the central span; extremes under point load, distributed load and vehicles.
import keta

spans = [25.0, 30.0, 25.0]
m = keta.Model()
xs = [0.0]
for L in spans:
    xs += [xs[-1] + L * i / 10 for i in range(1, 11)]
n = [m.add_node(x, 0, 0) for x in xs]
sec = m.add_section_props(sx=6.0, iy=4.0, iz=40.0, ix=5.0)
b = [m.add_beam(n[i], n[i + 1], sec, m.concrete()) for i in range(len(n) - 1)]
m.add_support(n[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
for i in (10, 20, 30):
    m.add_support(n[i], dy=True, dz=True)

# My at the end of bar 10 (over pier 1) and at the middle of the central span (end of bar 15)
line_pier = m.influence_line(b, dict(kind="bar_force", bar=b[9], x=2.5, comp=4))
line_mid = m.influence_line(b, dict(kind="bar_force", bar=b[14], x=3.0, comp=4))
print(f"line: {len(line_pier.s)} abscissae from {line_pier.s[0]:.1f} to {line_pier.s[-1]:.1f} m")
for name, line in (("M over pier", line_pier), ("M midspan", line_mid)):
    mx, mn = line.point_extreme()
    print(f"{name:12}: unit load -> max {mx:7.3f}, min {mn:7.3f} m; "
          f"areas + {line.integral_pos():8.3f} / - {line.integral_neg():8.3f} m2")
    print(f"{'':12}  positive zones: " + ", ".join(f"[{a:.2f}; {c:.2f}]" for a, c, _ in line.zones(1)))
    print(f"{'':12}  ordinate at s = 40 m: {line.at(40.0):.4f} m")

# Moment at the middle of the central span: vehicles and distributed loads (characteristic values, N.m)
q = 9e3 * 3.0                                    # 9 kN/m2 on a lane of 3 m
cases = [("LM1 tandem lane 1", keta.convoy_extreme(line_mid, keta.lm1_tandem(1), 2000)),
         ("distributed 27 kN/m", keta.udl_extreme(line_mid, q)),
         ("LM71", keta.convoy_extreme(line_mid, keta.rail_lm71(), 2000))]
for name, (mx, mn, rmx, rmn) in cases:
    print(f"{name:19}: max = {mx / 1e3:9.1f} kN.m, min = {mn / 1e3:9.1f} kN.m")

keta.fasc61_bc()  →  convoy

keta.fasc61_mc120()  →  convoy

ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
loaded_lengthm—loaded length
bridge_class——class of the bridge (1, 2 or 3)
loaded_lanes——number of loaded lanes
element_lengthm—length of the element for the dynamic amplification factor
permanent_loadN—permanent load of the element
max_axle_loadN—maximum load of the axles that can be placed on it

fasc61_a_lane_load gives the load per metre of a lane of given width, a2 · max(a1·A(l), 400 − 0.2·l kg/m²) × width, a1 being that of one loaded lane; combined with intensity_udl_extreme, it leads to the most unfavourable loaded length. The convoys Bc (two 30 t lorries one behind the other), Bt (one tandem of two 16 t axles) and Mc120 (110 t tracked vehicle on 6.1 m) are given for one line of vehicles, without the bc or bt factor and without dynamic amplification: these factors apply to the result.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_convois.py
# Road live loads on an influence line: LM1 load model per lane,
# A(l), Bc and Mc120 systems and Courbon's transverse distribution.
import keta

# Simply supported span of 30 m: midspan moment (exact triangular line, sampled)
L = 30.0
m = keta.Model()
n = [m.add_node(L * i / 12, 0, 0) for i in range(13)]
b = [m.add_beam(n[i], n[i + 1], m.add_section_props(5.0, 3.0, 30.0, 4.0), m.concrete()) for i in range(12)]
m.add_support(n[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
m.add_support(n[-1], dy=True, dz=True)
line = m.influence_line(b, dict(kind="bar_force", bar=b[5], x=2.5, comp=4), per_bar=21)
print(f"maximum ordinate |eta| = {abs(line.point_extreme()[1]):.4f} m (L/4 = {L / 4:.4f} m)")

# EN 1991-2, LM1: lanes of 3 m on a carriageway of 9 m
nl, width, rest = keta.en_lane_division(9.0)
print(f"carriageway of 9 m: {nl} lanes of {width} m, remaining area {rest} m")
for lane in (1, 2, 3):
    res = keta.lm1_lane_extreme(line, lane, width)
    print(f"lane {lane}: tandem {keta.lm1_tandem(lane)['axles'][0][1] / 1e3:.0f} kN/axle, "
          f"UDL {keta.lm1_udl(lane, width) / 1e3:.1f} kN/m -> M = {min(res[:2]) / 1e3:9.1f} kN.m")

# Fascicule 61 Titre II: A(l) on the most unfavourable loaded length, Bc, Mc120
density = lambda Lc: keta.fasc61_a_lane_load(Lc, 1, 3.5) * 2   # 2 lanes, first-class bridge [N/m]
val, Lc, zones = keta.intensity_udl_extreme(line, density, sign=-1)
print(f"A(l): loaded length {Lc:.2f} m, A(30) = {keta.fasc61_a_of_l(30.0):.1f} N/m2 -> M = {val / 1e3:.1f} kN.m")
G, S = 30 * 150e3, 2 * 60 * 9.80665e3                            # self-weight and maximum Bc load
delta = keta.fasc61_delta(L, G, S)
mx, mn, rmx, rmn = keta.convoy_extreme(line, keta.fasc61_bc(), 3000)
print(f"Bc (one line of trucks): M = {mn / 1e3:.1f} kN.m, head of the convoy at s = {rmn:.2f} m; "
      f"dynamic amplification delta = {delta:.4f}")
mx, mn, rmx, rmn = keta.convoy_extreme(line, keta.fasc61_mc120(), 3000)
print(f"Mc120: M = {mn / 1e3:.1f} kN.m")

# Courbon's transverse distribution: 4 girders, unit load at y = 3 m
K = keta.courbon_coefficients([-4.5, -1.5, 1.5, 4.5], y=3.0)
print("Courbon coefficients:", [round(k, 4) for k in K])

12.6 Transverse distribution#

Syntax
keta.courbon_coefficients(girder_y, weights=[], y)  →  [K1, K2, …]
keta.separable_surface(line, ky, k)  →  InfluenceSurface
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
girder_ym—transverse positions of the girders
weights—[]relative second moments of area of the girders (empty: identical girders)
ym—transverse position of the unit load
line——longitudinal influence line

Courbon's method (infinitely stiff cross-beams) gives the share of a load carried by each girder. separable_surface builds an influence surface from an influence line and a transverse distribution law, for a simplified grillage analysis.

12.7 Influence surfaces#

Syntax
m.influence_surface(nodes, nx, ny, response, dir=(0, 0, -1))  →  InfluenceSurface
m.influence_surface_cloud(nodes, response, dir=(0, 0, -1))  →  CloudSurface
m.lm1_surface(nodes, nx, ny, response, y0, y1, minimise=False, step_y=0.05, factors='class1')  →  dict
ParameterUnitDefaultMeaning
nodes——deck nodes; for a grid, list ordered by successive rows (x increasing then y increasing), nx × ny nodes
nx, ny——numbers of grid nodes along x and y
response——response studied (Table 12.1, without 'bar_displacement')
dir—(0, 0, −1)direction of the unit load
y0, y1m—edges of the carriageway (y ordinates)
minimise—Falsesearch for the minimum instead of the maximum
step_ym0.05transverse sweep step of the lanes

The influence surface on a grid is interpolated bilinearly between the nodes; the one on a cloud of nodes (skew deck, fan-shaped deck) is linear on a Delaunay triangulation of the positions in plan. Their placement methods are:

Method (InfluenceSurface)Result
x, y, etacoordinates of the grid and ordinates by rows
at(x, y), integral(), integral_pos(), integral_neg(), integral_box(x0, x1, y0, y1)ordinate, total integrals, by sign, over a rectangle

patch_extreme(a, b, p, steps_x=60, steps_y=60)rectangle a × b loaded at p, moved over the grid
wheel_set_extreme(wheels, steps_x=60, steps_y=60), wheel_set_value(wheels, rx, ry)wheel set [(dx, dy, P)] moved over the grid; value at one position
vehicle_extreme(wheels, y0, y1, both_directions=True, minimise=False)exact extreme of a vehicle whose wheels remain within [y0, y1]: dict(value, x, y, reversed)
load_group(group, y0, y1, minimise=False, factors='class1', footways=[], vehicle=[], clear=0.0, step_y=0.05, concomitant=[])group of loads of EN 1991-2: 'gr1a' (LM1 and footways at 3 kN/m²), 'gr4' (crowd 5 kN/m²), 'gr5' (special vehicle and frequent LM1); dict(value, lm1, footways, vehicle, vehicle_at, concomitant)

CloudSurface has at, integral, integral_pos, integral_neg, wheels_value(wheels, rx, ry) and wheels_extreme(wheels, steps_x, steps_y). lm1_surface places the complete model 1 (lanes, tandems and distributed loads on the unfavourable zones) and returns dict(value, remaining, lanes), each lane being described by its rank, its edge y_start, the position ts_x of its tandem and its loads.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_surface_influence.py
# Influence surfaces: grillage of 24 m x 9 m (5 main girders, cross-beams),
# deflection and reaction; full LM1, load groups, special vehicle and wheel set.
import keta

nx, ny, Lx, Ly = 13, 7, 24.0, 9.0
m = keta.Model()
concrete = m.concrete()
g = [[m.add_node(Lx * i / (nx - 1), Ly * j / (ny - 1), 0.0) for i in range(nx)] for j in range(ny)]
girder = m.add_section_props(sx=0.9, iy=0.35, iz=0.05, ix=0.02)     # longitudinal girders
xbeam = m.add_section_props(sx=0.3, iy=0.02, iz=0.01, ix=0.005)     # cross-beams / slab
for j in range(ny):
    for i in range(nx - 1):
        m.add_beam(g[j][i], g[j][i + 1], girder if j % 3 == 0 else xbeam, concrete)
for i in range(nx):
    for j in range(ny - 1):
        m.add_beam(g[j][i], g[j + 1][i], xbeam, concrete)
for j in range(ny):
    m.add_support(g[j][0], dx=(j == 0), dy=(j == 0), dz=True)
    m.add_support(g[j][-1], dz=True)
nodes = [nd for row in g for nd in row]                        # successive rows (order x then y)
centre = g[ny // 2][nx // 2]

srf = m.influence_surface(nodes, nx, ny, dict(kind="displacement", node=centre, comp=2))
print(f"grid {len(srf.x)} x {len(srf.y)}; ordinate at the centre {srf.at(12.0, 4.5) * 1e9:.4f} mm/MN")
print(f"deflection under 1 kN/m2: everywhere {srf.integral() * 1e6:.4f} mm, unfavourable zones "
      f"{srf.integral_neg() * 1e6:.4f} mm, rectangle [8;16]x[3;6] {srf.integral_box(8, 16, 3, 6) * 1e6:.4f} mm")
mx, mn = srf.point_extreme()
print(f"unit point load: min {mn * 1e9:.4f} mm/MN")
u = srf.udl_extreme(5e3)
print(f"distributed load of 5 kN/m2 on the unfavourable zones: {u['min'] * 1e3:.4f} mm")
lm = m.lm1_surface(nodes, nx, ny, dict(kind="displacement", node=centre, comp=2), 0.0, 9.0, minimise=True)
print(f"LM1: extreme deflection {lm['value'] * 1e3:.3f} mm; lanes:")
for v in lm["lanes"]:
    print(f"   lane {v['rank']}: edge y = {v['y_start']:.2f} m, tandem at x = {v['ts_x']:.2f} m")
gr1a = srf.load_group("gr1a", 0.0, 9.0, minimise=True)
gr4 = srf.load_group("gr4", 0.0, 9.0, minimise=True)
print(f"group gr1a: {gr1a['value'] * 1e3:.3f} mm; group gr4 (crowd): {gr4['value'] * 1e3:.3f} mm")
wheels = keta.annex_a_vehicle("1800/200", [(-1.0, 0.5), (1.0, 0.5)])  # 2 lines of wheels at +/- 1 m
v = srf.vehicle_extreme(wheels, y0=0.0, y1=9.0, minimise=True)
print(f"special vehicle 1800/200 ({len(wheels)} wheels): {v['value'] * 1e3:.3f} mm at x = {v['x']:.2f}, y = {v['y']:.2f}")
lm2 = keta.lm2_wheels()
print(f"LM2 ({lm2}): {srf.wheel_set_extreme(lm2, 48, 36)['min'] * 1e3:.3f} mm")

# Support reaction on an arbitrary cloud of nodes (triangulation of the positions in plan)
cloud = m.influence_surface_cloud(nodes, dict(kind="reaction", node=g[0][0], comp=2))
print(f"surface on a cloud: corner reaction for 100 kN at (2, 1) m = {cloud.wheels_value([(0, 0, 100e3)], 2.0, 1.0) / 1e3:.3f} kN")

These functions give the load models LM71, SW/0, SW/2 and “unloaded train”, the HSLM-B train (n_axles forces of 170 kN spaced at spacing; the number of forces and the spacing are read on Figures 6.12 and 6.13 of EN 1991-2 according to the span), the dynamic factors Φ2 and Φ3, the traction, braking, nosing and centrifugal forces, the eccentric distribution of the loads on the rails, the distribution of axle loads through the ballast, the service trains of Annex D and their mixes, the lower limit of the first natural frequency and the fatigue dynamic factor. The dynamic behaviour under high-speed trains and the track-bridge interaction are treated by:

rail_speed_sweep computes, by superposition on the first n_modes modes (exact modal forces of the moving axles, each oscillator advanced step by step under a load linear over the step; Newmark scheme for sprung axles), the envelopes of the displacements and accelerations of the observed points for each speed, the static envelope, the dynamic amplification factor and the critical speed. The result is a dictionary points (one entry per speed: speed, u_max, u_min, a_max), static_max, static_min, dynamic_factor, critical_speed. track_bridge_uic774 applies the method of UIC leaflet 774-3: thermal state, then successive positions of the train, and returns for each state (thermal, positions, envelope) the extreme stresses of the rail, the forces on the bearings and the relative displacements.

12.9 Fatigue#

Syntax
keta.convoy_history(line, convoy, exact=True, steps=500)  →  (positions, effects)
keta.turning_points(series)  →  [values]
keta.rainflow(series)  →  [(range, mean, count), …]

convoy_history gives the history of the effect of a convoy crossing the influence line. With exact=True, the effect is computed at each position where an axle or an end of a distributed load passes over a point of the line: for a piecewise linear line (from_samples) and axles only, the effect is linear between these positions, which therefore contain its extremes; for a line computed on the model (cubic between its points) or a convoy with distributed loads, 8 intermediate positions are added per interval. With exact=False, the effect is computed at steps + 1 regular positions. turning_points extracts the turning points of a series and rainflow performs the cycle counting on it by the rainflow method (ASTM E1049): each cycle is counted as 1 or 0.5. The damage is then computed in Python by Miner's rule with the desired resistance curve.

Exampleexamples/manual/ex_fatigue.py
# Fatigue: history of the midspan moment of a continuous beam 2 x 20 m under the passage
# of the FLM3 vehicle, rainflow counting and Miner damage (curve of slope m = 3).
import keta

m = keta.Model()
n = [m.add_node(2.0 * i, 0, 0) for i in range(21)]
b = [m.add_beam(n[i], n[i + 1], m.add_section_props(0.5, 0.08, 0.5, 0.05), m.steel()) for i in range(20)]
m.add_support(n[0], dx=True, dy=True, dz=True, rx=True)
m.add_support(n[10], dy=True, dz=True)
m.add_support(n[20], dy=True, dz=True)
line = m.influence_line(b, dict(kind="bar_force", bar=b[4], x=2.0, comp=4))  # My at x = 10 m

flm3 = keta.flm3_vehicle()
print("FLM3:", [(d, round(p / 1e3)) for d, p in flm3["axles"]], "(offset m, kN)")
r, M = keta.convoy_history(line, flm3)
print(f"history: {len(M)} positions, M from {min(M) / 1e3:.1f} to {max(M) / 1e3:.1f} kN.m")
tp = keta.turning_points(M)
print("turning points [kN.m]:", [round(x / 1e3, 1) for x in tp])
cycles = keta.rainflow(M)
for range_, mean, count in cycles:
    print(f"   cycle: range {range_ / 1e3:8.2f} kN.m, mean {mean / 1e3:8.2f} kN.m, count {count}")

W = 0.08 / 0.5                                   # section modulus I / v [m3]
dsc, NC = 71e6, 2e6                              # detail category 71 MPa at 2e6 cycles
D = sum(nb * ((e / W) / dsc) ** 3 / NC for e, _, nb in cycles)
print(f"damage of one passage: {D:.3e}; allowable passages: {1 / D:.3e}")
