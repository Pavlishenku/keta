Chapter 5 Combinations and envelopes

A structure is never verified under a single load case. Self-weight, superimposed dead loads, support settlements, traffic, temperature and wind act together, each with its design value, and the engineer looks, at each section, for the most unfavourable effect of their combination. This chapter describes how keta forms these combinations from the computed load cases: linear combinations, the EN 1990 combinations that the kernel can generate, the search for the extremum of a response among all admissible combinations, then envelopes, which keep at each point the bounds of a family of states, and concomitant forces, which restore the whole state behind each bound. It ends with the output sections, the points of the bars where these results are evaluated. Moving loads, whose extreme effect is read on an influence line, are the subject of chapters 11 and 12; combinations of modal responses, of chapter 15.

5.1 Assumptions#

  • the results combined are those of linear analyses: the superposition principle applies, and the response to λ1​F1​+λ2​F2​ is λ1​R1​+λ2​R2​;
  • each load case is computed once, on the same structure (same supports, same active bars): all the results combined have the same shape, node by node and section by section;
  • a nonlinear result (unilateral supports, pseudo-plastic soil, links with a nonlinear law, cables) cannot be superposed: keta marks it as such and refuses to combine it;
  • the partial factors γ and the combination factors ψ are input data: the predefined sets take the values of the French National Annex for bridges, and each value can be replaced;
  • envelopes and combinations are formed at the output sections of the bars, at the nodes, at the supports and at the centre of the shell facets; nothing is interpolated between these points.

5.2 Linear combination of cases#

A combination is a list of (factor, case) pairs. Its result is the weighted sum of the results of the cases, field by field:

Rc​=i∑​ci​Ri​(5.1)

for the nodal displacements, the support reactions (including the reactions of skew supports and the forces of linear relations), the end forces of the bars, the internal forces, deflections and rotations at the output sections, the soil pressures, the forces and deformations of elastic links and the stress resultants of shells. Since the internal forces at an output section are exact for each case (chapter 4), they remain so for the combination.

Only the tensor components of a shell are superposed: nx​,ny​,nxy​,mx​,my​,mxy​,qx​,qy​. The principal moments of the combination are recomputed from the combined moments,

m1,2​=2mx​+my​​±(2mx​−my​​)2+mxy2​​(5.2)

and are not themselves combined, which would be wrong as soon as the principal directions of the cases differ. The same applies to the principal membrane forces.

The calculation refuses a combination that refers to a case that does not exist, to results of different shapes, or to a result that cannot be superposed. A model with unilateral supports or supports with a force cap is computed case by case (chapter 6) and its combinations are refused: each combination must then be computed as a case, with all its loads.

The “+” sign means “to be combined with”: each permanent action takes its unfavourable value γG,sup​ or its favourable value γG,inf​, and a favourable variable action is omitted. Prestress has no category of its own in these combinations: it is described as a permanent action, with its own factors.

5.3.2 Generation of combinations#

The keta generator adds explicit linear combinations to the model, each variable action becoming the leading action in turn. The permanent actions all take the same pair of factors (γG,sup​,γG,inf​), by default (1.35,1.0): the generator does not set one favourable permanent action against another unfavourable one. Each variable action carries its factors γQ​, ψ0​, ψ1​, ψ2​; four sets are predefined.

  • the “snow” set is that of snow on a bridge (table A2.1, ψ0​=0.8).

Accidental and seismic combinations are not generated: they are written as linear combinations. The combination of the directions of an earthquake (30 % rule of EN 1998-1) is described in chapter 15.

5.3.3 Extremum of a response#

Generating every explicit combination soon becomes impractical: with exclusive traffic groups, settlement variants and permanent actions that are favourable or unfavourable separately, their number grows as the product of the choices. keta therefore turns the problem round. For a given scalar response (a moment at a section, a reaction), the effect ea​ of each action a with a unit factor is known. The maximum (or the minimum) of

E=a∑​fa​ea​,fa​∈Fa​(state,variant,L)(5.6)

is sought over all admissible factors fa​, the set Fa​ depending on the limit state, on the variant (6.10, 6.10a or 6.10b) and on the leading action L.

An action can be declared unable to be the leading action.

Example: two permanent actions with effects 1.0 and 0.5, a settlement with effect 0.2, two exclusive traffic groups (load model 1 with effect 1.0, special vehicle with effect 0.8, both with γQ​=1.35 and ψ0​=0.75), temperature (0.3; ψ0​=0.6) and wind (0.4; ψ0​=0.6). Enumerating all admissible combinations gives the same value.

Applied field by field (each component of displacement, of reaction, of end force, of internal force and deflection at the output sections, each shell component), the same calculation gives the envelope of the EN 1990 combinations. Each bound refers to the combination that produces it; the distinct combinations thus found are stored once each, with their terms. Two components of the same section may come from different combinations: they are bounds, not a state.

5.4 Envelopes#

5.4.1 Component-by-component envelope#

The envelope of a set of results R(1),…,R(n) (cases, combinations, states) keeps, for each scalar component k, its two bounds and the index of the source that reaches them:

Rkmax​=imax​Rk(i)​,Rkmin​=imin​Rk(i)​,ikmax​=argimax​Rk(i)​(5.8)

The components enveloped are the six displacements of each node, the six components of each reaction, the twelve end forces of each bar, the six internal forces, the three deflections and the three rotations of each output section, and twelve quantities per shell facet (nx​,ny​,nxy​,mx​,my​,mxy​,qx​,qy​,m1​,m2​,n1​,n2​). The principal values are enveloped as independent quantities; their angles have no meaning on a bound and are set to zero. In case of a tie, the first source prevails. A non-finite value stops the calculation, as do sources of different shapes.

An envelope bound is not a state of equilibrium: the maximum moment and the maximum shear force at a given section generally come from different sources. For a verification that combines several internal forces (bending with axial force, interaction of shear and torsion), the concomitant state is needed.

5.4.2 Concomitant forces#

A driving quantity is chosen, an internal force at an output section, a reaction component or a nodal displacement, and keta looks for the source that makes it extreme:

i∗=argimax​p(R(i)),Rconc=R(i∗)(5.9)

The state restored is the whole result of this source: all the displacements, all the reactions, all the internal forces at all the sections. It is an actual state of the structure, which satisfies equilibrium. 5.4.3 Envelope of factored cases#

A variant assigns to each case an interval of factors [cimin​,cimax​], for example [1.0,1.35] for a permanent action or [0,1.5] for a variable action. Each case is then taken alone, with the factor from its interval that makes it most unfavourable, and the envelope is the most unfavourable of the sources:

Rkmax​=imax​ max(cimin​Rk(i)​, cimax​Rk(i)​)(5.10)

This is not a combination: the cases are not added. For the effect of simultaneous actions, use a combination, the extremum of (5.7) or the envelope with bounded factors described next.

5.4.4 Envelope of combinations with bounded factors#

The general case is that of sources acting together, each with a factor free within its interval. At a given point, the driving quantity p is linear in these factors; its maximum over the box of factors is attained at a corner, chosen source by source:

pmax=ci​∈[cimin​,cimax​]max​i∑​ci​pi​=i∑​max(cimin​pi​, cimax​pi​)(5.11)

The factor ci∗​ retained for each source being known, keta rebuilds the whole state at the same corner,

Rmax=i∑​ci∗​R(i)(5.12)

which is a linear combination in the sense of (5.1), hence a consistent state: the concomitant forces at a section never mix independent extremes. The minimum is treated in the same way with its own corner. A reaction component can be enveloped at the same time as the driving quantity, with its own corner.

A source may also be a set of possible states, for example the two bounds of an envelope already computed. It then contributes through the most unfavourable of its alternatives, never through their sum: this is how an envelope of envelopes is formed. In a simple envelope over alternatives, an alternative whose value is zero at the section does not compete, unless all of them are zero; at reactions, all of them compete.

5.4.5 Driving quantities#

The driving quantity of an envelope with bounded factors is one of the following.

  • An internal force at an output section, N,Vy​,Vz​,T,My​ or Mz​, or a nodal displacement.
  • A normal stress at an extreme fibre of the cross-section,
    σ=AN​+Iy​My​z​−Iz​Mz​y​(5.13)
    evaluated at y=vy​ or −wy​, z=vz​ or −wz​, or at the four corners (±,±). The distances to the extreme fibres come from the stress points of the cross-section; failing those, from the elastic section moduli (vz​=wz​=Iy​/Wel,y​). A cross-section that has neither is refused.
  • A generalised stress, a linear combination of the six internal forces plus a constant,
    Σ(x)=cN​N+cVy​​Vy​+cVz​​Vz​+cT​T+cMy​​My​+cMz​​Mz​+c0​(5.14)
    whose coefficients are given per bar, constant, linear between the start and the end, or parabolic through the start, the middle and the end:
    c(ξ)=2(cstart​−2cmid​+cend​)ξ2+(−3cstart​+4cmid​−cend​)ξ+cstart​,ξ=x/L(5.15)
    The constant c0​ applies to load cases, not to the effects of moving loads read on an influence line. The coefficients may be written in the keta convention or in the compression-positive convention (N, My​, Mz​ with their signs changed).
  • A soil pressure px​,py​,pz​ or a soil moment mx​, on bars resting on an elastic foundation (chapter 6).

The envelope can be evaluated at one section, or at all the output sections of all the bars; the bars where the quantity does not exist (no soil, no generalised stress defined) are then skipped. Envelopes over the steps of staged construction, with their own factors for prestress, are described in chapter 10.

5.5 Output sections#

The internal forces, deflections and rotations of a bar are returned at a finite number of sections, the output sections. Unless otherwise specified, each bar receives eleven, equally spaced, ends included (chapter 4). Arbitrary abscissae can also be given, bar by bar; a bar that receives them loses its regular grid, the others keep theirs. The value at each section is exact (chapter 4); envelopes and combinations are formed at these sections and are not interpolated between them. A support section, the point of application of a point load or a section where the reinforcement changes must therefore be declared if their extremes are wanted.

These abscissae are described in blocks. In this description, every bar carries by default a section at each end. An ordinary block starts again from these default sections and adds its own to them (earlier additions are forgotten); an “additional” block is added to what exists; an “only” block replaces everything, default sections included. Each line of a block targets a list of bars (or all of them) and gives absolute or relative abscissae, or else a step over an interval [xa​,xb​] (the whole bar by default). An absolute step h places the sections xa​+kh and adds the end xb​ if the step does not fall on it. A relative step must be the inverse of an integer of the form

h=n1​,n=2a5b(5.16)

so that the relative abscissae are terminating decimals: 1/2, 1/4, 1/5, 1/10 are accepted, 1/3 or 1/7 are refused. It must also divide the interval into a whole number of steps. Two sections less than 10−9max(L,1 m) apart are merged. Each section carries an indication of the family of results it serves (internal forces and stresses, or displacements and soil pressures); keta always returns both families at each section, and the indication only serves to restrict the grid if requested.

The abscissae are measured from the start of the bar along its axis, that is from one offset end to the other (chapter 3):

L=∥(xj​+ej​)−(xi​+ei​)∥(5.17)

so that a relative abscissa falls exactly where the recovery of internal forces expects it. An abscissa outside [0,L], an empty list or a step longer than the interval is refused.

5.6 Scope#

The combinations and envelopes of this chapter apply to linear results computed on the same structure. The results of nonlinear analyses are not combined: there, each combination is a load case. The EN 1990 combination generator covers the fundamental ultimate limit states (6.10, or 6.10a and 6.10b) and the three serviceability combinations; it applies a single pair of factors to all the permanent actions and has the particular features noted above (same ψ for the tandem and the distributed load of traffic). The exact extremum of a response is free of these limits: each permanent action in it is favourable or unfavourable independently, and the factor ξ affects only the unfavourable value. Accidental and seismic combinations are not generated. Envelopes see only the declared output sections.
