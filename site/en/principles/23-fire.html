Chapter 23 Fire

This chapter deals with the fire resistance of a reinforced concrete or steel frame. The problem is posed in two stages, as in the advanced calculation methods of EN 1992-1-2 and EN 1993-1-2. First a thermal problem: under a nominal fire curve, heat enters the section through its exposed faces, and the temperature field in the section is sought at each instant. Then a mechanical problem: at the temperature of each fibre, concrete and steel lose strength and stiffness and expand; the equilibrium state of the frame is sought, loaded by the actions concomitant with the fire, with its hot bars. The absence of equilibrium signals failure.

keta also treats the heating of steel members by the simplified method of EN 1993-1-2, with a uniform temperature over the section. The fibre laws at ambient temperature and the integration of sections are those of chapters 18 and 21; heat conduction in continuum elements is described in chapter 25.

23.1 Assumptions#

  • conduction is two-dimensional: the section is prismatic and heat does not travel along the bar (the framework of Annex B of EN 1992-1-2);
  • the thermal problem is uncoupled from the mechanical problem: cracking and deformations do not modify the conduction;
  • the water in the concrete enters only through the peak in specific heat of EN 1992-1-2 §3.3.2; there is neither moisture transport nor a spalling model. The spalled thickness is removed by hand from the geometry of the section;
  • the thermal and mechanical properties are those of EN 1992-1-2 (concrete, reinforcement) and of EN 1993-1-2 (structural steel);
  • each fibre of the section is thermo-elastic with a secant modulus taken on its law at its temperature; transient creep is present only through the law of EN 1992-1-2, which includes it implicitly;
  • the frame is in small displacements; each exposed bar receives a single strain plane, obtained from its mean internal forces: an exposed span must be divided into several bars;
  • prestressing steel is not treated in fire;
  • all lengths of the thermal analysis are in metres.

23.2 Fire exposure#

23.2.1 Temperature-time curves#

The fire is described by the gas temperature θg​(t), in °C, as a function of time t in minutes. keta offers the nominal curves of EN 1991-1-2 §3.2, the increased hydrocarbon curve, a constant temperature and a curve given point by point:

ISO 834:external:hydrocarbon:increased hydrocarbon:​θg​=20+345log10​(8t+1)θg​=20+660(1−0.687e−0.32t−0.313e−3.8t)θg​=20+1080(1−0.325e−0.167t−0.675e−2.5t)θg​=20+1280(1−0.325e−0.167t−0.675e−2.5t)​(23.1)

A curve given as a table (t,θg​) is interpolated linearly and held constant beyond its bounds. For conduction in sections, the possible exposures are the ISO 834 curve, the two hydrocarbon curves, a constant gas temperature, a table of gas temperature, a table of surface temperature (imposed directly) and the adiabatic face; an unexposed face is adiabatic. The external curve is used only for the heating of steel members.

23.2.2 Heat flux received by a surface#

The net heat flux received by a surface at temperature θm​ is the sum of a convective exchange and a radiative exchange (EN 1991-1-2 §3.1):

h˙net​=αc​(θg​−θm​)+εm​σ[(θg​+273)4−(θm​+273)4](23.2)

with σ=5.67⋅10−8 W/m²K⁴ and the emissivity εm​=0.7 by default. The coefficient of heat transfer by convection is 25 W/m²K for the ISO 834 curve and the external curve, 50 W/m²K for the hydrocarbon curves (EN 1991-1-2 §3.2) and, in sections, for curves given as tables (25 W/m²K by default for a table applied to a steel member). For a constant gas temperature, it is 4 W/m²K as long as the surface remains below 50 °C (unexposed face), 25 W/m²K above. It can also be imposed, constant or as a function of the surface temperature.

23.3 Heating of a steel member#

A steel member has a high conductivity and a small thickness: its temperature is taken as uniform over the section. EN 1993-1-2 §4.2.5 gives the temperature increase during a step Δt. For an unprotected member with section factor Am​/V and shadow factor ksh​:

The specific heat of steel ca​(θ) is that of EN 1993-1-2 §3.4.1.2, with its peak at 735 °C, and ρa​=7850 kg/m³. The gas temperature is taken at the end of the step; the default step is 5 s. After 30 minutes of ISO 834 fire, an unprotected member with section factor Am​/V=200 m⁻¹ reaches 828.8 °C, a value returned by a separate step-by-step integration of the same formulas.

The critical temperature of a member whose degree of utilisation in fire is μ0​ is that of EN 1993-1-2 §4.2.4, expression (4.22), valid for 0.013≤μ0​≤1:

23.4 Conduction in the section#

23.4.1 Heat equation#

In a concrete section, the temperature θ(y,z,t) satisfies the nonlinear Fourier equation

ρ(θ)c(θ)∂t∂θ​=∇⋅(λ(θ)∇θ)(23.6)

with, on an exposed face, the flux (23.2) and, on an adiabatic face, zero flux. The initial temperature is uniform (20 °C by default) or given point by point.

23.4.2 Thermal properties of concrete#

The properties are those of EN 1992-1-2 §3.3, for 20≤θ≤1200 °C. The thermal conductivity lies between an upper limit and a lower limit; keta takes a combination of the two, λ=aλinf​+(1−a)λsup​, with a=1 (lower limit, recommended by the French National Annex for normal concretes) by default and a=0 for high-strength concretes:

λsup​=2−0.2451100θ​+0.0107(100θ​)2,λinf​=1.36−0.136100θ​+0.0057(100θ​)2(23.7)

The specific heat of dry concrete is 900 J/kgK up to 100 °C, increases linearly to 1000 J/kgK at 200 °C and then 1100 J/kgK at 400 °C, and remains constant beyond. The evaporation of water adds a plateau between 100 and 115 °C, of value cpeak​ = 900, 1470 or 2020 J/kgK for a moisture content of 0, 1.5 or 3 % by weight (interpolated between these values), which decreases linearly to the dry curve at 200 °C. The density decreases through the loss of water:

The conductivity, the specific heat and the density can also be given as constants, or as tables as functions of temperature. Finally, the volumetric enthalpy H(θ) can replace the product ρc: it represents without approximation a latent heat concentrated over a narrow temperature interval.

23.4.3 Mesh#

The section is divided into square cells of side h (1 cm by default), on a Cartesian grid. Three geometries are possible:

  • a slab, treated in one dimension through its thickness: a single column of cells, adiabatic lateral faces; only its top and bottom faces can be exposed;
  • a rectangle, in two dimensions, with four faces that can be exposed separately;
  • an arbitrary polygonal outline, traversed in either direction, rasterised: a cell belongs to the section if its centre lies inside the polygon. Each cell boundary face takes the exposure of the side of the polygon closest to its midpoint. An outline not parallel to the axes is therefore represented as a staircase.

For the slab and the rectangle, the step is adjusted so that a whole number of cells covers each dimension exactly; for a dimension b and a requested mesh size h,

nb​=max(1, round(b/h)),hb​=nb​b​(23.9)

and, for an outline, the grid covers the bounding rectangle with ⌈b/h⌉ cells of side h. keta refuses a mesh coarser than one third of the smallest dimension of the section (the width of the bounding rectangle for an outline), a mesh size of more than 1 m and dimensions of more than 100 m, which almost always reveal an input in millimetres.

23.4.4 Finite volumes and boundary conditions#

Equation (23.6) is discretised by cell-centred finite volumes; in the interior, the scheme is that of the five-point Laplacian. The flux between two neighbouring cells a and b crosses two half-cells in series, hence the conductance per unit length of bar

Kab​=d(λa​+λb​)2λa​λb​​ℓ(23.10)

where d is the distance between the centres and ℓ the length of the common face. On an exposed face, heat crosses in series the air film, the protection if any (resistance Rp​ in m²K/W) and the half-cell. Radiation is written exactly as a linear exchange coefficient,

hrad​=εσ(Tg2​+Ts2​)(Tg​+Ts​),U=[αc​+hrad​1​+Rp​+λh/2​]−1(23.11)

(Tg​, Ts​ absolute temperatures of the gas and of the surface, with 273.15 K), so that the flux towards the centre of the boundary cell is U(θg​−θi​). The surface temperature θs​ that enters hrad​ is the root of the equation obtained by equating the surface flux and the flux through the protection and the half-cell; it is obtained by fixed-point iteration (at most 25 iterations, tolerance 10−8 °C) before the step is solved. An imposed surface temperature acts through the resistance of the protection and the half-cell alone.

23.4.5 Time integration#

The time scheme is the implicit Euler scheme, unconditionally stable. The properties λ, ρ and c are taken at the temperature of the previous step: each step is then a symmetric positive definite linear system,

(ΔtρcA​+K(θn​))θn+1​=ΔtρcA​θn​+f(tn+1​)(23.12)

whose sparsity pattern is the same throughout the fire; the symbolic factorisation is done once. The scheme is first-order in time; the step is a choice of accuracy, not of stability, and is 30 s by default. When the enthalpy is given, each step solves A[H(θn+1​)−H(θn​)]/Δt+Kθn+1​=f by Newton's method with the tangent capacity dH/dθ and an exact line search: this residual is the gradient of a convex functional, the iteration converges whatever the latent heat, and the energy balance of the step is satisfied to within the iteration tolerance (relative correction 10−10).

The results are the temperature fields at the requested instants and at the end of the fire, with the mean temperature and the maximum temperature of the section.

23.5 Mechanical properties at elevated temperature#

23.5.1 Concrete#

The stress-strain law of concrete at temperature θ is that of EN 1992-1-2 §3.2.2.1. In absolute value (compression is negative in keta):

σ(ε)=⎩⎨⎧​εc1,θ​[2+(ε/εc1,θ​)3]3εfc,θ​​fc,θ​εcu1,θ​−εc1,θ​εcu1,θ​−ε​​ε≤εc1,θ​εc1,θ​<ε<εcu1,θ​​fc,θ​=kc​(θ)fck​(23.13)

The descending branch is the linear branch permitted by the standard. Beyond εcu1,θ​ the stress is zero. The factor kc​(θ) and the strains εc1,θ​, εcu1,θ​ are interpolated linearly in Table 3.1 of EN 1992-1-2, in the column for siliceous or calcareous aggregates. The initial modulus is 3fc,θ​/(2εc1,θ​). Concrete has no tensile strength (cracked analysis). On request, the initial modulus is kept in tension, without a strength limit.

23.5.2 Steels#

For structural steel (EN 1993-1-2 §3.2, Table 3.1) and for reinforcing steel (EN 1992-1-2 §3.2.3, Tables 3.2a for class N, hot rolled or cold worked, and 3.2b for class X), three factors reduce the effective yield strength, the proportional limit and the modulus: fy,θ​=ky​fy​, fp,θ​=kp​fy​, Eθ​=kE​E. 23.6 Hot section#

At a given instant of the fire, the section becomes a set of fibres: each cell of the thermal mesh is a concrete fibre, at its temperature; each reinforcing bar is a steel fibre. A bar takes the temperature of the hottest cell covered by its disc of diameter ϕ (a cell is covered if its point nearest to the centre of the bar is closer than ϕ/2). This choice is on the safe side: with the 1 cm mesh, a 16 mm bar whose axis is 15 mm from the face takes the temperature of the surface cell.

For a strain plane ε(y,z)=ε0​+κy​(z−zr​)−κz​(y−yr​), the mechanical strain of fibre i is εe,i​=εi​−εth,i​, its stress σi​=fi​(εe,i​) by its law at its temperature, and its secant modulus Ei​=σi​/εe,i​. A negative (descending branch) or undefined modulus is replaced by zero: the fibre stops carrying load and the load is transferred to the others. The stiffnesses of the hot section and its thermal resultants are taken about the centre of stiffness (yc​,zc​)=(∑Ei​Ai​yi​,∑Ei​Ai​zi​)/EA, which the heating moves away from the cold centroid:

EANth​​=i∑​Ei​Ai​,=i∑​Ei​Ai​εth,i​,​EIy​Mth,y​​=i∑​Ei​Ai​(zi​−zc​)2,=i∑​Ei​Ai​εth,i​(zi​−zc​),​EIz​Mth,z​​=i∑​Ei​Ai​(yi​−yc​)2=−i∑​Ei​Ai​εth,i​(yi​−yc​)​(23.16)

The equivalent expansion ε0​=Nth​/EA and the thermal curvatures κth​=Mth​/EI are those that a free bar would take; a restrained bar develops the force −EAε0​, the classical restrained-expansion force. The centre of stiffness can also be held at its cold position. If EA or one of the bending stiffnesses becomes zero, the section has lost all stiffness and the calculation stops on this finding of failure. For a slab, meshed as a single column, the transverse bending stiffness comes only from the reinforcement distributed over the width.

23.7 Equilibrium of the hot frame#

23.7.1 Secant iteration#

The mechanical calculation seeks the equilibrium of the frame under the load case concomitant with the fire, at a given instant (the fire duration), with its exposed bars in their hot state. The thermal problem is first solved once for each exposed bar. Then, at each iteration:

  1. each exposed bar receives an equivalent section: A=EA/E, Iy​=EIy​/E, Iz​=EIz​/E, where E is the modulus of its material; the shear areas and the torsion constant are reduced in the ratio EAhot​/EAcold​, for want of an integration of shear over the hot section; the density is corrected so that the self-weight remains that of the cold section;
  2. the load case is supplemented, for each exposed bar, by the imposed strain (ε0​,κth,y​,κth,z​) (chapter 3);
  3. the frame is analysed by linear statics; for each bar its mean internal forces (N,My​,Mz​) over its length (trapezoidal mean over 11 sections) are taken, transferred to the centre of stiffness, and a new strain plane is deduced
ε0​=EAN+Nth​​,κy​=EIy​My​+Mth,y​​,κz​=EIz​Mz​+Mth,z​​(23.17)

which gives new secant moduli. The process is repeated until the relative change in the nodal displacements, in the infinity norm, is below 10−3 (adjustable tolerance), within a limit of 500 iterations. The fixed point sought is that of the secant method: at convergence, the fibre stresses, taken with the secant moduli of the strain plane, have as resultants the internal forces of each bar, and the frame is in equilibrium under its loads, to within the tolerance.

The shift of the centre of stiffness creates an eccentricity of the axial force with respect to the line of the nodes. keta accounts for it by an additional imposed curvature (−zc​N/EIy​,yc​N/EIz​), with the axial force of the previous iteration; it can also carry this shift over into the offset of the bar.

23.7.2 Tangent corrections and relaxation#

Between two analyses of the frame, the strain plane of each section is refined, with the internal forces frozen, by a few Newton iterations (four by default) on the section, with the 3×3 tangent matrix

kt​=i∑​Et,i​Ai​​1⋅sym.​zi​−zr​(zi​−zr​)2⋅​−(yi​−yr​)−(zi​−zr​)(yi​−yr​)(yi​−yr​)2​​(23.18)

and a line search (the step is halved, at most twelve times, as long as the residual does not decrease): the laws have plateaus, and a full Newton step often overshoots the solution. These corrections accelerate convergence without changing the secant fixed point.

On a section that cracks, the secant iteration can oscillate between two states. When the residual no longer decreases (it stays above 0.99 times its value two iterations earlier), the update of the strain plane is under-relaxed by a factor ω, halved at each new stagnation down to 1/16; the convergence criterion then applies to the change divided by ω. If the stagnation persists twenty-five times at the minimum factor, the tangent corrections are abandoned and the iteration resumes as pure secant. A calculation that converges without stagnating is never relaxed.

Important

Lack of convergence is not a numerical defect to be worked around: it means that the frame has no equilibrium state under these loads at this fire duration. keta then stops with a failure message and supplies no approximate state.

As an option, the materials are degraded without the thermal strains being applied: this gives the effect of the loss of stiffness and strength alone. The results are the displacements, the reactions and the internal forces of the hot frame and, for each exposed bar, its section state (stiffnesses, centre of stiffness, thermal resultants, maximum and mean concrete temperatures, maximum reinforcement temperature).

23.8 Capacity of a hot section#

For a steel section at uniform temperature θ, keta gives the ratio Mfi​/M20​ of the resistant moments hot and cold. The fibres of the section whose law is bilinear or elastic receive the elliptical law (23.14) of structural steel at θ; the other fibres keep their law. The two moments are read from the moment-curvature curve at zero axial force (chapter 18) at the curvature

κ=zmax​0.2​(23.19)

(zmax​ distance of the furthest fibre), that is about ten times the yield strain of 2 % at the extreme fibre: the section is fully plastified. Prestressing steel and initial fibre strains are not admitted in this calculation. The capacity of a reinforced concrete section under a non-uniform temperature field is not computed through this ratio; it is read from the equilibrium of the hot frame of the previous section.

23.9 Scope#

The scope of the fire calculation and its limits are as follows:

  • frame bars only, in plane or spatial modelling; grillage modelling is refused, as is an exposed bar resting on an elastic foundation;
  • in plane modelling, the section is a slab or a rectangle;
  • secant method: the result is an equilibrium state at one fire duration, not a load-displacement history; no unloading, no plastic memory, no explicit creep at elevated temperature;
  • one strain plane per bar, taken from its mean internal forces;
  • no spalling, no moisture transport, no conduction along the bar;
  • no prestressing steel in fire: neither the factors of EN 1992-1-2 Table 3.3, nor expansion, nor tendons in the hot section;
  • polygonal outline represented as a staircase by the grid;
  • shear and torsional stiffnesses reduced in the ratio of the axial stiffnesses, not integrated over the hot section.
