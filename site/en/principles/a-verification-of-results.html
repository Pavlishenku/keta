Appendix A Verification of results

A result of keta is said to be verified when it has been compared with a value that does not come from keta. This value is of three kinds: a closed form of mechanics (a strength-of-materials formula, a solution of elasticity, a series or an integral worked out by hand); a published value (a worked example from a reference book, a table of a standard, a benchmark problem from the literature such as those of MacNeal and Harder); a reference solution obtained by an independent route, for example the step-by-step integration of a formula carried out separately or the limit of a sequence of increasingly fine meshes. The chapters give examples along the way: the catenary of chapter 7, the resistant moment of a rectangle in chapter 21, the deflection of a simply supported plate in chapter 24, the Lamé cylinder in chapter 25. The accepted difference depends on the comparison: rounding when keta must reproduce a formula, the expected discretisation error when it approximates a continuous solution, the accuracy of the source when the value is read from a publication.

Verification establishes that the calculation is correct in the cases compared; it does not prove that it is correct in all others, and the rarest combinations of options are the least tested. It bears on the solution of the problem posed, not on the choice of that problem: it does not say whether the model represents the structure (supports, stiffnesses, staged construction, material laws, loads), nor whether the assumptions stated at the head of each chapter suit the case in hand. The scopes and the refusals described in the chapters bound what has been verified; outside these bounds, a result has not been verified.

keta computes; it does not validate a structure. The engineer who uses its results in a design remains responsible for them: the engineer chooses the model, the assumptions, the partial factors and the code checks, and checks the results by an order of magnitude, by the global equilibrium of loads and reactions, by a simplified model or by examining the deformed shapes. An unexplained difference between these checks and keta must be resolved before any use of the result, and deserves to be reported.
