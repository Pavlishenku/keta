Chapter 7 Cables

This chapter deals with heavy cables stretched between two nodes of the frame: stays, main cables and backstays of suspension bridges, ties, cable nets. The problem is the static equilibrium of the assembly formed by the frame and its cables, under the loads of a case. A cable is not a bar: it works in tension only, sags under its own weight and stiffens as its tension increases. Its stiffness therefore depends on the state being sought, and the problem is no longer linear.

Prestressing tendons, which lie inside the concrete and transfer forces to it by friction and deviation, are the subject of chapter 8. The dynamic behaviour of cables is dealt with in chapter 16, and their role in large-displacement analysis in chapter 17.

7.1 Assumptions#

The cable is represented by a single elastic catenary between its two attachment nodes, without subdivision into elements. The assumptions are those of Irvine's theory:

  • the cable is perfectly flexible: it has neither bending nor torsional stiffness, and carries no compression;
  • the material is linearly elastic, with a constant axial rigidity EA;
  • the only distributed load is the self-weight, uniform, w per unit unstressed length; the other loads are applied at the nodes;
  • under its weight, the cable stays in the vertical plane that contains its chord; a vertical chord is not allowed;
  • the strains of the cable remain small (the elongation is limited to a few per cent); its displacements, on the other hand, may be large: the geometry of the cable is recomputed at each iteration from the position of its ends.

The frame that carries the cables remains, in the coupled analysis of this chapter, under small displacements. The large-displacement analysis of the whole (chapter 17) uses the same catenaries.

7.2 Equations of the elastic catenary#

The origin is placed at end 1 of the cable. In the vertical plane of the chord, ℓ denotes the horizontal projection of the chord and h its vertical projection, positive upwards from end 1 to end 2. The cable has an unstressed length L0​, and a point of the cable is located by its unstressed arc length s, from 0 to L0​. No horizontal load acts between the ends: the horizontal component H of the tension is constant. The vertical component increases with the weight carried:

V(s)=V1​+ws,V2​=V(L0​)=V1​+wL0​,T(s)=H2+V(s)2​(7.1)

With the conventions of the figure, (H,V1​) and −(H,V2​) are the forces that the cable exerts on its nodes 1 and 2. A cable that descends from end 1 has V1​<0; the low point, if there is one, lies at s=−V1​/w.

The tangent to the cable is carried by the tension, dz/dx=V(s)/H, and the element ds elongates by Tds/EA (Hooke's law). Integrating dx=(H/T)(1+T/EA)ds and dz=(V/T)(1+T/EA)ds from 0 to L0​ gives the two projections in closed form:

ℓ=EAHL0​​+wH​[arsinhHV2​​−arsinhHV1​​](7.2)
h=EAL0​​(V1​+2wL0​​)+wT2​−T1​​(7.3)

The first term of each equation is the elastic elongation, the second the geometry of the inextensible catenary. The tension is greatest at the higher end: on an inextensible catenary, T=T1​+w(z−z1​).

A zero weight makes these expressions indeterminate. keta then replaces w by a tiny weight, 10−9EA/L02​, with no measurable effect on the result; the form finding of suspension bridges (section 7.8) uses a form of the same integrals that admits a zero weight.

7.2.1 Solution for given end positions#

When the positions of the two ends are known, (7.2) and (7.3) are two equations in (H,V1​). keta solves them by Newton's method. The Newton matrix is the flexibility of the cable, the derivative of the projections with respect to the forces:

F=​∂H∂ℓ​∂H∂h​​∂V1​∂ℓ​∂V1​∂h​​​,F11​F12​F22​​=w1​[arsinhHV2​​−arsinhHV1​​]−w1​(T2​V2​​−T1​V1​​)+EAL0​​=F21​=wH​(T2​1​−T1​1​)=w1​(T2​V2​​−T1​V1​​)+EAL0​​​(7.4)

Each iteration corrects the forces by

[ΔHΔV1​​]=−F−1[ℓ(H,V1​)−ℓtarget​h(H,V1​)−htarget​​](7.5)

the step being halved as long as H would become negative. The starting point depends on the state of the cable. A cable longer than its chord c starts from a parabola of sag f=3ℓ(L0​−c)/8​, that is H=wℓ2/(8f). A cable shorter than its chord starts from a tension directed along the chord, T0​=EA(c−L0​)/L0​. The iterations stop when the error on both projections is below 10−13 times the largest of the lengths ℓ, ∣h∣, L0​, or below the round-off noise of the integrals, which grows with the tension.

7.3 Tangent stiffness of the cable#

The inverse of the flexibility (7.4) is the tangent stiffness of the cable in its plane:

Kplane​=F−1=F11​F22​−F122​1​[F22​−F12​​−F12​F11​​]=[Kxx​Kxz​​Kxz​Kzz​​](7.6)

It relates a small relative displacement of the ends, horizontal and vertical in the plane of the cable, to the change in the forces (H,V1​). Out of its plane, a cable displaced laterally by an amount δ swings about its chord like a pendulum: the horizontal component of the tension produces a restoring force Hδ/ℓ. With eh​ the horizontal unit vector of the chord, ez​ the vertical and n=eh​×ez​ the horizontal normal to the plane of the cable, the stiffness of one node relative to the other is

k=Kxx​eh​ehT​+Kxz​(eh​ezT​+ez​ehT​)+Kzz​ez​ezT​+ℓH​nnT(7.7)

and the matrix of the cable, on the translations of its two nodes, is that of a two-node element:

Kc​=[k−k​−kk​](7.8)

The cable contributes no rotational stiffness. A node that carries only cables must therefore have its rotations restrained by a support or by another bar.

7.4 Unstressed length#

The unstressed length L0​ defines the cable. It may be given directly, or determined in the initial geometry of the model from a tension. In both cases, it is computed once, in the undeformed geometry, and does not change afterwards, whatever the displacements of the frame.

7.4.1 Imposed horizontal tension#

The root sought is that of

H(L0​)−Htarget​=0(7.9)

where H(L0​) is the horizontal tension of the catenary of length L0​ stretched between the two ends, obtained by the solution of the previous section. H decreases as L0​ increases: the root is unique. keta brackets it starting from [c/2, 3c/2], widened if needed, then narrows it by bisection until ∣H−Htarget​∣<10−10Htarget​.

7.4.2 Tension imposed at one end#

The tension at one end is not monotonic in L0​:

T2​(L0​)=H(L0​)2+(V1​(L0​)+wL0​)2​(7.10)

When a taut cable is lengthened, its tension first decreases, like that of a tie; then the cable takes on sag, its weight increases with its length, and the tension rises again. The same tension may therefore correspond to two lengths, one on the taut branch, the other on the slack branch (figure 7.2).

keta keeps the root on the taut branch, the only one that suits a stay. The search starts from L0​=c/(1+0.045), that is an elongation of 4.5 %, and proceeds in steps of 5 % until the root is bracketed, followed by a bisection down to 10−12Ttarget​. A tension higher than that of this starting point would require an elongation beyond the elastic range and is rejected; a tension below the minimum of the curve has no root on the taut branch and is rejected too. The tension may be prescribed at either end.

7.5 Equilibrium of the frame and cables#

The unknown is the vector u of the displacements of all the nodes. Each cable is evaluated at the displaced position of its two nodes: its catenary is solved, which gives the forces q(u) that it exerts on them. Equilibrium reads

r(u)=λF+q(u)−Ku=0(7.11)

where K is the linear stiffness of the frame, F the loads of the case and λ the fraction applied. The self-weight of the cables is always present, in full, since it is contained in q.

7.5.1 Newton's method#

At each iteration, the system solved is

(K+c∑​Kc​(u))Δu=r(u)(7.12)

where the sum runs over the matrices (7.8) of the cables in the current state. The step is followed by a line search: the update kept is u+sΔu with s=1,21​,41​,…, the first value for which

∥r(u+sΔu)∥∞​<∥r(u)∥∞​(7.13)

A step for which a cable finds no catenary (chord become vertical, ends further apart than the admissible tension allows) is rejected in the same way. After thirty unsuccessful halvings, the analysis stops with an error. The matrix of each iteration has the same sparsity pattern as the previous one: the reordering and the symbolic analysis of the factorisation (chapter 4) are done once.

7.5.2 Convergence criterion#

The analysis stops when each component of the residual satisfies

∣ri​∣−8εj∑​∣Kij​uj​∣≤10−10max(∥λF∥∞​, ∥Ku∥∞​, cmax​Tc​)(7.14)

with ε the machine precision. The tension of the cables enters the scale because a cable net without external load has a zero residual at equilibrium, while its cables are in tension. The term subtracted on the left is the round-off error of the product Ku: on a very stiff member, a pylon for example, the terms Kij​uj​ are large and cancel out, and the residual cannot fall below their round-off error. Without this term, Newton's method would iterate on noise.

7.5.3 Load increments#

The loads of the case may be applied in n increments,

r(um​)=λm​F+q(um​)−Kum​=0,λm​=nm​,m=1,…,n(7.15)

each increment starting from the equilibrium um−1​ of the previous one. This is useful when the final state is far from the initial state (a cable very slack at the start, a large displacement of a saddle). The number of iterations is limited to sixty per increment.

7.5.4 Results#

The displacements, internal forces and stresses of the frame are recovered as in linear statics (chapter 4), from u. For each cable, keta gives the horizontal tension H and the largest tension max(T1​,T2​). The support reactions include the pull of the cables anchored at supported nodes:

R=Ku−q(u)−F(7.16)
Note

The results of an analysis with cables are not superposable: each combination must be analysed as a case, with all its loads. Imposed support displacements are not allowed in this analysis.

7.6 Ernst equivalent stiffness#

The linear analyses (statics, eigenmodes, response spectrum) cannot handle a catenary. Each cable is then replaced by a straight bar with the same chord, whose stiffness accounts for the sag: this is Ernst's method. A cable of chord length Lc​ and horizontal projection ℓ, under a mean tension T along the chord, has the axial stiffness

k1​=EAL0​​+12T3(wℓ)2Lc​​,T=ℓHLc​​(7.17)

The first term is the elastic flexibility, the second the flexibility that comes from the sag. Written with the stress σ=T/A and the unit weight γ=w/A, this result is the classical equivalent modulus

Eeq​=1+12σ3(γℓ)2E​E​(7.18)

7.6.1 Range of the approximation#

The approximation improves as the cable becomes tauter. Its relative error with respect to the exact stiffness of the catenary is close to χ2/5, with the sag parameter

χ=HwLc​cos2α​,cosα=Lc​ℓ​(7.19)

where α is the inclination of the chord. keta flags the cables for which χ>0.1, that is an error above about 0.2 %: the catenary must then be kept. The sag term is often found written as w2Lc3​/(12H3). This form is exact for a horizontal chord, but for an inclined chord it multiplies the sag term by 1/cos5α, that is 2.05 at 30° and 32 at 60°: the stiffness is then underestimated, all the more as the sag weighs in the flexibility of the cable.

7.6.2 Substitute model#

From a state computed with the catenaries, keta builds a model in which each cable becomes a pin-ended bar of stiffness k, placed on the geometry of the model. Its modulus and density are chosen to reproduce the stiffness and the mass of the cable:

E∗=AkLc​​,ρ∗=gALc​wL0​​(7.20)

The tension of the state is retained on each bar. A modal analysis about this state accounts for it through the geometric stiffness (chapter 17), which gives the stays their vibrating-string stiffness T/Lc​. The bars remain on the initial geometry: if the computed state has displaced the anchorages by u, the chords rotate by about u/Lc​, which gives the order of magnitude of the error made.

7.7 Tension adjustment#

Adjusting the stays of a bridge means finding the installation tensions that give, under permanent loads, a chosen deck profile, a vertical pylon or chosen reactions. keta sets out this problem in two ways.

7.7.1 Linear adjustment#

The unknowns xj​ are load case coefficients, generally one unit pretension case per stay: an imposed shortening ε0​=−1/EA, which tensions the stay by 1 N if it is free to shorten. Each target result ri​ (displacement, reaction, internal force in a bar or a link) is an affine function of the unknowns:

r=b+Ax(7.21)

where b comes from the base cases and column j of A from case j, all obtained by a single solution with several right-hand sides. The objectives are equalities, bounds or weighted least-squares terms; the unknowns may be bounded and penalised. The problem

xmin​ i∈fitted∑​wi​(ai​x+bi​−ti​)2+j∑​qj​(xj​−xjref​)2subject toai​x+bi​=ti​,  ai​x+bi​≶ti​,  xj−​≤xj​≤xj+​(7.22)

is a convex quadratic programme, solved exactly by the dual method of Goldfarb and Idnani. With as many equalities as unknowns, it amounts to solving a square system; with more unknowns, the penalties qj​ select the solution; with more objectives, these are fitted in the least-squares sense. If the objective is not strictly convex on the unknowns that the equalities leave free, the analysis stops with an error rather than choose a solution.

7.7.2 Nonlinear adjustment#

The unknowns are the unstressed lengths of the cables and, if needed, the length defects of bars of the frame (a bar fabricated too short by δ receives the imposed strain −δ/L). The results r(x) are those of the coupled analysis of section 7.5, or of the large-displacement analysis of the whole. keta linearises them by central differences,

Jij​=2hj​ri​(x+hj​ej​)−ri​(x−hj​ej​)​,hj​=10−6∣L0,j​∣(7.23)

solves problem (7.22) for the step Δx with A=J, repeats the coupled analysis at x+Δx, and halves the step as long as the error on the equalities increases. For a length defect, the difference step is 10−7 times the length of the bar. The analysis stops when each equality is satisfied to within 10−9 and, if there are fitted or bounded objectives, when the step itself has fallen below this tolerance; the error is measured relative to the largest of the target, obtained and initial values; for a zero target displacement, the scale is the diagonal of the box enclosing the model. When there are as many equalities as unknowns, keta computes the singular values of their Jacobian. If the ratio of the smallest to the largest is below 10−10, two objectives are dependent: the analysis stops, naming the objectives and the cables involved.

7.8 Suspension bridges#

7.8.1 Form finding of the main cable#

The main cable is the funicular of the loads transmitted to it by the hangers. Between two hangers, it is an elastic catenary of constant horizontal tension. At the node of a hanger k, which carries the downward load Pk​, the tension of the cable jumps:

Vk+1​=Vk​+Pk​,Hk+1​=Hk​−Pk​−Δzk​Δxk​​(7.24)

where (Δxk​,Δzk​) runs from the cable node to the attachment point of the hanger on the deck (Δzk​<0). For a vertical hanger, Δxk​=0 and the horizontal tension is the same over the whole span. An inclined hanger transmits a force directed along its axis, which makes H vary from one segment to the next; the force in the hanger is

Sk​=Pk​−Δzk​Δxk2​+Δzk2​​​(7.25)

The abscissae of the cable nodes are given. The unknowns are the two components (H,V0​) of the tension in the first segment. From them, the computation marches from saddle to saddle. For each segment k, the unstressed length L0,k​ that gives the required horizontal projection is sought, and the difference in level follows:

ℓk​(L0,k​)=xk+1​−xk​,zk+1​=zk​+hk​(L0,k​)(7.26)

The first equation has a unique solution, since ℓk​ increases with L0,k​ (dℓ/ds=H/T2​+H/EA>0); it is solved by a safeguarded Newton method. The jumps (7.24) are then applied. Two conditions close the problem:

r1​=zN​(H,V0​)−zright saddle​=0,r2​=f(H,V0​)−ftarget​=0  (or H−Htarget​=0)(7.27)

where f is the sag, measured at the horizontal midpoint between the two saddles, from the chord joining the saddles. These two equations are solved by Newton's method, with a Jacobian by central differences. Without self-weight of the cable and with vertical hangers, the classical result is recovered: under a uniform load q over a span L,

H=8fqL2​(7.28)

The catenary integrals are written here in a form that does not subtract two neighbouring hyperbolic sine arguments, which allows a zero weight and avoids losing significant digits on short segments.

7.8.2 Side spans and saddles#

The cable may extend beyond a saddle to an anchorage. Two saddle behaviours bracket reality. On a fixed saddle, the cable is clamped to the saddle: the horizontal tensions on the two sides are independent, and their difference goes down the pylon as a horizontal force at its head,

Qhead​=Hspan​−Hbackstay​(7.29)

the backstay being installed under a chosen horizontal tension (by default, that of the adjacent span, which balances the saddle). On a free saddle, the cable slides without friction: the tension is the same on both sides, as over a pulley, and the unstressed length of the backstay is computed to obtain it:

Hspan2​+Vspan2​​=Hbackstay2​+Vbackstay2​​at the saddle(7.30)

This behaviour is a condition of the permanent state: under live loads, the cable no longer slides on the saddle. A saddle with finite friction, which satisfies ∣ln(T1​/T2​)∣≤μθ for a wrap angle θ, lies between these two cases, and it is enough to compute both.

7.8.3 Flexible pylons#

When the pylons are flexible, the saddles move under the permanent load, and the funicular must be sought at their displaced position. keta alternates the form finding, with the saddles placed at un​, and the analysis of the whole, which gives the displacement G(un​) of the pylon heads. The residual rn​=G(un​)−un​ is the error in the position of the saddles. The iteration is accelerated by Aitken relaxation (Irons and Tuck):

θn​=−θn−1​∥rn​−rn−1​∥2rn−1​⋅(rn​−rn−1​)​,un+1​=un​+θn​rn​(7.31)

with θ0​=1 and θn​ bounded to [0.05, 20]. Without acceleration, the iteration converges as kcable​/(kcable​+kpylon​) at each step, a factor close to 1 when a taut backstay connects the pylon head elastically to the ground. The analysis stops when the saddles move by less than 10−8 m.

7.8.4 Hanger lengths#

A hanger that carries the tension T is longer than its unstressed length: installed at its geometric length, it would let the deck drop. For a hanger of geometric length L, rigidity EA and weight w projected on its axis, the unstressed length is the root of

L=L0​(1+EAT​)+2EAwL02​​⟹L0​=a+a2+2wL/EA​2L​,a=1+EAT​(7.32)

written in this form to avoid a subtraction. keta introduces it in the permanent case as a length defect, that is the imposed strain

ε0​=−L0​L−L0​​(7.33)

so that each hanger carries its form-finding tension and the deck hangs at its design level. This is exact when the hanger loads are the reactions transmitted to them by the deck, that is for a deck hinged at each hanger; a continuous deck retains the deflection due to the redistribution between its supports.

7.8.5 Scope and verification#

The form finding handles one cable plane at a time: a bridge with two cable planes is analysed plane by plane, with the loads of each. The hangers are vertical or inclined in the plane of the cable, and always in tension: a hanger whose attachment point does not lie below the cable node is rejected. The shape found is checked by solving the permanent case on the assembled model: the same horizontal tension and the same hanger forces must be recovered, together with a deck moment that is negligible compared with that of the simply supported span,

∣Mdeck​∣≪8qL2​(7.34)

since the deck is suspended and does not carry the span.
