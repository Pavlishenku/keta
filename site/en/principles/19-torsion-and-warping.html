Chapter 19 Torsion and warping

A twisted beam rotates about its axis; its cross-section, in general, does not remain plane: it warps. If nothing prevents this warping, the torsion is uniform and the torque is carried by the Saint-Venant shear stresses alone. If warping is restrained, by a fixed end, by a neighbouring section that warps less or by the variation of the torque along the bar, normal stresses appear, the torsional stiffness increases and part of the torque is carried by bending of the walls. For a box girder or a solid section, this effect is small and localised. For an open thin-walled section (I section, channel, twin-web girder), it is of first order: it governs the rotation of a twisted cantilever and the critical lateral-torsional buckling load. This chapter describes the Saint-Venant torsion of the standard bar element, then Vlasov non-uniform torsion, carried by an element with seven degrees of freedom per node, in first order, in second order, in buckling and in large displacements. The computation of the section constants (J, Iw​, shear centre) is the subject of chapter 20; lateral-torsional buckling is stated in chapter 17.

19.1 Assumptions#

  • the material is linearly elastic and isotropic, with moduli E and G;
  • the cross-section does not deform in its plane: neither distortion nor local buckling of the walls;
  • non-uniform torsion follows Vlasov's theory of open thin-walled sections: warping is proportional to the rate of twist θ′, and the shear strain of the middle surface of the walls is neglected;
  • the section is doubly symmetric: its shear centre is its centroid, and there is no Wagner asymmetry term;
  • the bars are straight; at a node where warping is free, the bars that meet are collinear;
  • in first and second order, the displacements are small; the large-displacement analysis accepts large rotations with small strains.

19.2 Saint-Venant torsion#

In uniform torsion, the rotation θ(x) varies linearly and the torque is proportional to the rate of twist:

T=GJθ′(19.1)

J is the Saint-Venant torsion constant of the section. keta's bar element with six degrees of freedom per node (chapter 3) knows only this torsion: its torsion matrix is (GJ/L)[1,−1;−1,1] on the rotations θx​ of its ends, and warping has no degree of freedom in it. The orders of magnitude differ greatly with the shape of the section:

sectiontorsion constant
open thin-walled (walls bk​×tk​)J=∑k​bk​tk3​/3
single-cell box (area Am​ enclosed by the midline)J=4Am2​/∮ds/t (Bredt)
arbitrary sectionSaint-Venant warping function, by finite elements (chapter 20)

For an IPE 300, J=20.1 cm4, four hundred times less than the bending second moment of area Iy​: the uniform torsion of an open section is very flexible, and restrained warping accounts for a large part of the stiffness.

19.3 Non-uniform torsion#

19.3.1 Warping and bimoment#

In Vlasov's theory, a point on the midline of a wall, located by its sectorial coordinate ω(s) referred to the shear centre, moves out of the plane of the section by u=−ωθ′. If θ′ varies along the bar, this displacement creates an axial strain and hence a normal stress, whose resultant is a force of a new kind, the bimoment:

σw​=−Eωθ′′=Iw​Bω​,B=∫A​σw​ωdA=−EIw​θ′′,Iw​=∫A​ω2dA(19.2)

Iw​ is the warping constant. The stresses σw​ are self-equilibrating: they give neither axial force nor bending moment. For an I section, they are easily read: the rotation θ moves the two flanges laterally in opposite directions, by ±hs​θ/2, where hs​=h−tf​ is the distance between their mid-planes; each flange bends in its plane under a moment Mf​, equal and opposite to that of the other, and the bimoment is the product B=Mf​hs​. The warping constant is then the one keta computes for an I section,

Iw​=24tf​b3(h−tf​)2​(19.3)

that is, 125.9⋅103 cm6 (1.259⋅10−7 m6) for an IPE 300.

19.3.2 Vlasov equation#

The torque is then shared between a Saint-Venant part, carried by the shear of the walls, and a warping part, carried by the shear forces that bending of the flanges induces in their plane:

T=Tsv​+Tw​,Tsv​=GJθ′,Tw​=−EIw​θ′′′=B′(19.4)

and the equilibrium of a segment under a distributed torque mx​ gives the Vlasov equation:

EIw​θ′′′′−GJθ′′=mx​(19.5)

The end conditions apply to θ (rotation restrained or torque imposed) and to θ′ (warping restrained, θ′=0, or free, B=0). The fork support of bridges and building beams restrains the torsional rotation and leaves warping free.

The homogeneous solution contains coshkx and sinhkx, with

k=EIw​GJ​​(19.6)

The length 1/k measures the distance over which a restrained warping is felt; beyond it, the torsion becomes uniform again. For an IPE 300, 1/k=1.28 m. For a cantilever of length L, fixed (rotation and warping restrained) and loaded at its tip by a torque T, the equation gives

θ(L)=GJT​(L−ktanhkL​),Tsv​(x)=T(1−coshkLcoshk(L−x)​),B(x)=−kT​coshkLsinhk(L−x)​(19.7)

At the fixed end, all the torque is carried by warping (Tsv​=0) and the bimoment is −Ttanh(kL)/k; at the tip, the torque is almost entirely Saint-Venant torque. keta's computation reproduces these three expressions to machine precision, at every node of a cantilever divided into elements, and with a single element for the tip rotation.

19.4 Seven-degree-of-freedom element#

19.4.1 Warping degree of freedom#

keta adds a seventh degree of freedom at each node, the warping:

unode​=(ux​,uy​,uz​,θx​,θy​,θz​,ψ),ψ=θx′​(19.8)

This analysis is distinct from the standard linear analysis: it takes the bar model as it is, with its supports and loads, and solves it with seven degrees of freedom per node. A node may have its warping restrained; otherwise, ψ is free. Since ψ is a scalar, independent of the direction of the bar, it expresses the continuity of warping between two collinear bars; between two bars at an angle, this continuity has no meaning, and keta requires the warping of the node to be restrained. A node reached by no bar with a constant Iw​>0 has no warping degree of freedom: its bars work in Saint-Venant torsion. The warping constant of each bar is that of its section, or a value given for the analysis.

19.4.2 Exact torsion block#

The torsion block of the element, on (θi​,ψi​,θj​,ψj​), is built from the exact solution of (19.5). keta writes the homogeneous solution in the basis {1,x,F2​(x),F3​(x)} of the transfer functions of the beam (hyperbolic functions, or trigonometric ones when the effective torsional stiffness becomes negative in second order, series expansions when it is close to zero). If H relates the coefficients in this basis to the end values (θ,θ′) and D to the end forces (T,B), the stiffness matrix is

kt​=DH−1(19.9)

(symmetrised). A single element per bar is therefore exact in pure torsion. The fixed-end forces of linearly varying distributed torques and of point torques are computed in the same basis, from a particular solution of (19.5): they too are exact. A transverse load applied at the distance (ey​,ez​) from the shear centre also creates the distributed torque ey​qz​−ez​qy​. The axial and bending blocks are those of keta's element (Timoshenko in first order). When ∣Gt​∣L2/(EIw​)>400, that is kL>20, the hyperbolic functions lose their precision through cancellation: keta requires the bar to be subdivided.

19.4.3 Results and refusals#

The analysis returns, for each node, seven displacements (including ψ, in rad/m); for each bar, the twelve end forces and the two bimoments, with the convention B=−EIw​θ′′; and, at each end, the sharing of the torque:

MT1​=GJψ,MT3​=Nr02​ψ,MT2​=MT​−MT1​−MT3​(19.10)

where MT1​ is the Saint-Venant torque, MT3​ the Wagner torque (second order only, see below) and MT2​ the warping torque. The warping normal stresses at a point of the section follow from them through (19.2), with the sectorial coordinate of chapter 20. The analysis does not give the distribution of the secondary shear stresses in the walls, nor the forces at intermediate sections of a bar: the bar must be subdivided to read the sharing of the torque along it.

The analysis refuses bars with released ends, with rigid arms, on a Winkler foundation or of variable section, truss bars and cables, imposed support displacements, coupled elastic supports and unilateral supports, shells and continuum elements, and monosymmetric sections (Wagner coefficient βx​=0).

19.5 Second order and buckling#

19.5.1 Second-order energy#

Under axial force and bending moments, the thin-walled bar couples bending and torsion. The second-order energy of the doubly symmetric bar (Trahair, Flexural-Torsional Buckling of Structures, 1993, chapter 3) is

U2​=∫0L​[2N​(v′2+w′2+r02​θ′2)+My​θv′′+Mz​θw′′]dx+21​∑(F⋅e)θ2,r02​=AIy​+Iz​​(19.11)

The first term is the geometric stiffness of the axial force, Wagner term included; the second, the coupling by which a bending moment twists a beam that moves laterally; the last, the effect of the height at which the loads are applied: a downward load applied above the shear centre (F⋅e<0) is destabilising, a load applied below it is stabilising. The signs of the moments are keta's (My​>0 puts the +z fibre in tension).

In the second-order analysis, the axial force enters the uncoupled blocks without approximation: bending takes the stiffness of the beam-column (stability functions, chapter 17, Euler-Bernoulli), and torsion the effective stiffness

Gt​=GJ+Nr02​(19.12)

in the exact solution of EIw​θ′′′′−Gt​θ′′=mx​. Tension stiffens torsion, compression softens it. The coupling terms due to the moments and to the load heights are integrated with the cubic Hermite shape functions of v, w and θ, by a five-point Gauss rule (exact for the parabolic moment of a uniform load). This coupling is a discretisation, which converges as h2 to h4; the uncoupled blocks are exact. The analysis starts from the first-order state, rebuilds the blocks with the forces of the previous iteration and stops when the relative change in the displacements is below 10−10, or at the rounding floor of the solution (64ε times the ratio of the extreme pivots, capped at 10−6), in at most 50 iterations. An indefinite tangent means that the load exceeds the critical state: the analysis stops. For a twisted cantilever under axial force, the tip angle follows (19.7) with GJ replaced by Gt​, to machine precision.

19.5.2 Flexural-torsional buckling#

Linear flexural-torsional buckling starts from the first-order state of the case and solves

(K+λKg​)φ=0(19.13)

where Kg​ is the matrix of (19.11) built on N, My​, Mz​ and the load heights of this state. The modes have seven components per node. The same analysis gives the lateral-torsional buckling of a beam in bending and the torsional buckling of a column. For a column on fork supports, the torsional critical load is

Ncr,T​=r02​1​(GJ+L2π2EIw​​)(19.14)

which keta recovers to within 2⋅10−7 with 32 elements, alongside the flexural modes. For a beam on fork supports under uniform moment, the critical moment tends to the closed form of chapter 17 (difference of 2⋅10−7 with 16 elements for a 6 m IPE 300). Under uniform load, the result accounts for the height of application; for the same IPE 300, it is within 0.5 % of the approximate formula with factors C1​=1.127 and C2​=0.454, whether the load is at the shear centre, on the top flange or on the bottom flange (the factors C1​, C2​ are themselves rounded).

Flexural-torsional buckling is distinct from the linear buckling of chapter 17: the latter uses the six-degree-of-freedom element, which carries the Wagner term in Saint-Venant torsion but ignores warping and the coupling through the bending moments. For an open section, it therefore underestimates the torsional stiffness (no EIw​), which lowers the torsional critical load of a column, and it ignores the lateral-torsional buckling of a beam in bending.

19.6 Large displacements with warping#

The large-displacement analysis with warping gives the corotational bar of chapter 17 the seventh degree of freedom. The energy of the element is

W=21​dTKˉd+21​tTKtw​t,t=(θˉx,i​,ψi​,θˉx,j​,ψj​),Ktw​=EIw​∫0L​N′′TN′′dx+GJ∫0L​N′TN′dx(19.15)

where the first term is the corotational energy of the axial force and of bending, and the second the Vlasov energy, written on the deformational torsional rotations θˉx​ (with the rigid-body rotation of the frame removed) and on the warpings, with the cubic Hermite interpolation N of the torsional rotation. This block is polynomial, and not exact as in small displacements: it converges with subdivision. The forces and the tangent are the exact gradient and Hessian of W, by automatic differentiation; the load is applied in increments, with Newton's method in each increment. The bending-torsion coupling comes from the rotation of the frame and converges with refinement; there is no Wagner term, which restricts the analysis to bars without large axial force.

The analysis accepts bars with rigid arms (a load height is represented there by an arm) and nodal forces, which keep their direction. It refuses nodal moments, span loads, imposed displacements, releases, Winkler foundations, variable sections, cables, elastic supports, shells, links and monosymmetric sections. Warping is restrained at the designated nodes and at the nodes reached by no bar with a constant Iw​>0.

19.7 Scope#

The table summarises what keta covers in torsion.

coverednot covered
Saint-Venant torsion in all the bar analyseswarping in the standard six-degree-of-freedom bar element
Vlasov non-uniform torsion, doubly symmetric sections, straight barsmonosymmetric or asymmetric sections (eccentric shear centre, coefficient βx​): refused
exact solution of the element in pure torsion, distributed and point torquesbox girders: Benscoter reduction of the warping of closed sections, absent
second order, Wagner term, load heightdistortion of the section and buckling of the walls
lateral-torsional and torsional bucklingdistribution of the secondary shear stresses in the walls
large displacements with warping under nodal forcescontinuity of warping between non-collinear bars (the node must be restrained)
computation of J, Iw​ and the shear centre of a section (chapter 20)nonlinear torsion of the material (fibres remain in elastic Saint-Venant torsion, chapter 18)

For a bridge box girder, Saint-Venant torsion is dominant and restrained warping is a local effect, which Vlasov's theory of open sections would overestimate. keta has no specific treatment of closed sections and does not recognise them: the non-uniform torsion analysis assumes an open section, and it is up to the user not to use it for a box girder, or not to give it a constant Iw​.
