Chapter 25 Continuum elements

This chapter deals with continuum finite elements: plane elements in plane stress or plane strain, axisymmetric elements and solid elements. They are used where beam and shell theory no longer applies: end block and diffusion zone of a concentrated force, anchorage block, abutment and ground, thick member, tank of revolution. The problem posed is that of linear elasticity, or of plasticity for metals and soils, in small strains. The chapter describes the elements (shape functions, integration), the loads they receive, their connection to bars and shells, the nonlinear laws and their integration, heat conduction in these elements and the meshing tools.

Shells are described in chapter 24; heat conduction in the section of a bar exposed to fire, in chapter 23.

25.1 Assumptions#

  • small displacements and small strains, except for the elastic large-displacement option of the nonlinear analysis;
  • isoparametric elements: the geometry and the displacements are interpolated by the same shape functions;
  • plane and axisymmetric elements lie in the global XY plane; for axisymmetry, r=X is the radius and Y the axis, and all quantities (forces, masses, stiffnesses) are counted per radian: a load over the circumference is 2π times the load per radian;
  • the nodes of continuum elements carry only translations;
  • isotropic elastic material, defined by E and ν; von Mises, Drucker-Prager and Mohr-Coulomb plastic laws, and creep, in the nonlinear analysis;
  • no mesher for continuum elements: the elements are given node by node.

25.2.2 Isoparametric mapping#

The position and the displacement at a point with natural coordinates ξ are x=∑i​Ni​(ξ)xi​ and u=∑i​Ni​(ξ)ui​. The Cartesian derivatives are obtained through the Jacobian matrix:

J=∂ξ∂x​=i∑​∂ξ∂Ni​​xiT​,∂x∂Ni​​=J−T∂ξ∂Ni​​,dV=detJdξ(25.3)

An element whose Jacobian is negative or zero at an integration point (nodes in the wrong order, element too distorted) is refused. A plane element whose nodes do not all lie in one plane Z = constant is refused, as is an axisymmetric element that contains a point of zero or negative radius at an integration point.

25.2.3 Strains and elastic laws#

The strain is ε=Bue​. In plane problems, it has three components (εx​,εy​,γxy​); in axisymmetry, four, with the circumferential strain due to the radial displacement:

ε=(∂r∂ur​​, ∂z∂uz​​, rur​​, ∂z∂ur​​+∂r∂uz​​)(25.4)

in solids, six components (εx​,εy​,εz​,γxy​,γyz​,γzx​). The elasticity matrices are those of isotropic elasticity: in plane stress, D=1−ν2E​[1ν0​ν10​00(1−ν)/2​]; in plane strain, in axisymmetry and in solids, with c=E/[(1+ν)(1−2ν)],

Dii​=c(1−ν),Dij​=cν (i=j, normal components),Dγγ​=c21−2ν​(25.5)

Plane elements have a thickness t (in plane strain, an arbitrary thickness, generally 1 m).

25.2.4 Stiffness, mass and integration rules#

The stiffness and the consistent mass of the element are

Ke​=∫V​BTDBdV,Me​=∫V​ρNTNdV,dV=tdA (plane),rdrdz (axisymmetry)(25.6)

integrated by the Gauss rules of the table. On a straight-sided element, the rules of the triangles and of the tetrahedron are exact for the stiffness; axisymmetry, which introduces r and 1/r, takes one order more (3 points for T3, 7 Dunavant points for T6). The Q8 is integrated by the reduced 2×2 rule, which avoids locking and gives better stresses; the H20 by the full 3×3×3 rule, because the 2×2×2 rule leaves spurious modes in a column of a single element. The mass rules are exact for affine elements.

The stresses are returned at any natural point of the element, generally at the Gauss points or at the nodes: (σx​,σy​,τxy​,σz​) in plane problems, with σz​=ν(σx​+σy​) in plane strain and 0 in plane stress; (σr​,σz​,σθ​,τrz​) in axisymmetry; the six components in solids. Nodal averaging of the values of neighbouring elements is left to the user.

The thick cylinder under internal pressure p (radii a and b=2a, plane strain) has the Lamé solution σr,θ​=b2−a2pa2​(1∓r2b2​). With four axisymmetric Q8 elements through the thickness, keta returns its nodal displacements to within 10−13; the stresses, continuous within each element, show small jumps between elements, which disappear with mesh refinement (circumferential stress at the inner wall: 1.6889 instead of 1.6667 times p at the nodal point, with four elements; 1.6684 with sixteen).

25.3 Loads#

A traction t (force per unit area, global vector) on a side or a face gives the consistent nodal forces

fi​=∫Γ​Ni​tdΓ(25.7)

where dΓ is tdℓ in plane problems, rdℓ in axisymmetry (force per radian), dA in solids. A side runs from vertex k to vertex k+1, mid-side node included; the faces of a hexahedron are numbered bottom, top, then η=−1, ξ=+1, η=+1, ξ=−1. A traction can vary linearly along a side. A pressure acts along the local inward normal, exactly, even on a curved side. Self-weight gives the forces Me​g, with the consistent mass.

A nodal temperature field T gives an initial strain ε0​=α(T−T0​) on the normal components, and the nodal forces

f=∫V​BTDε0​dV,ε0​=αΔT(1,1,0) (plane stress),(1+ν)αΔT(1,1,0) (plane strain)(25.8)

(in plane strain, the factor 1+ν expresses the restrained expansion through the thickness; in axisymmetry, the circumferential component is included).

Important

These forces are nodal loads: the returned stresses are DBu, without subtracting Dε0​. For a thermal case, the true stress is D(Bu−ε0​): a plate free to expand under ΔT=100 K is returned with EαΔT/(1−ν) instead of zero. The correction is left to the user.

25.4 Conduction and seepage#

The same elements solve a scalar field problem: temperature (heat conduction) or hydraulic head (groundwater flow), which obey the same equation

∇⋅(k(u)∇u)+s=c∂t∂u​,ki​(u)=ki​+k1​u(25.9)

with a conductivity that is orthotropic along the global axes and linear in u, and a volumetric capacity c. The boundary conditions are values imposed at the nodes, incoming fluxes and exchanges h(u−uenv​) on the sides or faces, point sources and conductances between two nodes or between a node and the surrounding medium. In steady state, keta solves K(u)u=f by Newton's method with the exact tangent (including the term in k1​), to rounding; in transient state, by the implicit Euler scheme with the consistent capacity matrix and a Newton iteration at each step. The fluxes supplied by the imposed nodes (the inflow into an excavation, the heat extracted) are returned as reactions. The temperature field obtained can be used as a thermal loading (25.8).

25.5 Connection to bars and shells#

Continuum elements are assembled with bars, shells, springs and links in a single stiffness matrix, through their common nodes. Their nodes carry only the translations they use (ux​, uy​ in plane problems and in axisymmetry, all three in solids); the other degrees of freedom of a node that belongs only to continuum elements are restrained automatically, like those of a truss node. A bar or a shell that arrives at a node of a continuum element transmits its forces to it, but not its moments: the node has no rotational stiffness, and the bar is in effect hinged there. To fix a bar into a solid block, it must be connected by a rigid link to several nodes of the element. For the same reason, a plane element connects only to the translations in its plane. The table below summarises the active degrees of freedom according to the elements that meet at a node.

elements meeting at the nodeactive degrees of freedom
plane or axisymmetric elements onlyux​, uy​
solid elements onlyux​, uy​, uz​
continuum elements and a bar or a shellall six, the rotations having only the stiffness of the bar or the shell

Linear static, modal and dynamic analyses take continuum elements; buckling, second order, large displacements of bars, staged construction and strain-energy-weighted damping refuse them.

25.6 Nonlinear laws#

25.6.1 Von Mises plasticity#

In plane strain, in axisymmetry and in solids, each Gauss point carries the three-dimensional state (six components): plastic strain εp​ and equivalent plastic strain εˉp​. The elastic predictor is σtr=D(ε−εp​), with deviator str. With the von Mises criterion with linear isotropic hardening ∥s∥≤2/3​(σy​+Hεˉp​), the radial return of Simo and Hughes is exact in one step:

Δγ=2G+32​H∥str∥−2/3​(σy​+Hεˉp​)​,σ=σtr−2GΔγn,εˉp​+=2/3​Δγ(25.10)

with n=str/∥str∥. The consistent tangent matrix

Dep​=K1⊗1+2GβP−2Gγˉ​n⊗n,β=1−∥str∥2GΔγ​,γˉ​=1+H/(3G)1​−(1−β)(25.11)

(P deviatoric projector, K bulk modulus) gives quadratic convergence of the equilibrium at each increment. Plane stress is refused for a plastic material: its return is a different algorithm (that of the shells, chapter 24).

A thick cylinder (b/a=2) in plane strain, perfectly plastic, has the limit pressure plim​=3​2​σy​ln(b/a)=0.80038σy​. With sixteen axisymmetric Q8 elements, the last load step converged by keta is 0.800375σy​, the next one having no equilibrium.

25.6.2 Creep#

A von Mises material can also creep, according to a time-hardening power law, possibly thermally activated (Norton-Arrhenius law):

εc​=Aqntme−Q/T(25.12)

where q is the von Mises stress, t the time since the start of the calculation and T the absolute temperature at the Gauss point, interpolated from the nodal temperatures and constant in time. The flow is deviatoric, along the von Mises normal. It is integrated implicitly together with plasticity: over a step [t0​,t1​], Δεc​=Aq1n​(t1m​−t0m​)e−Q/T, where q1​ is the end-of-step stress, the root of an increasing scalar equation solved by a Newton method safeguarded by bisection. The scheme is unconditionally stable, exact under constant stress and first-order under varying stress. With σy​=0, the material is purely viscous.

25.6.3 Drucker-Prager and Mohr-Coulomb#

For soils (tension positive), keta offers two frictional criteria, with constant cohesion or with linear hardening of the cohesion c=c0​+Hεˉp​, with an angle of friction φ and an angle of dilatancy ψ≤φ (non-associated plastic potential if ψ<φ). The Mohr-Coulomb criterion, in principal stresses σ1​≥σ2​≥σ3​, and the Drucker-Prager criterion are written

(σ1​−σ3​)+(σ1​+σ3​)sinφ=2ccosφ,J2​​+ηp−ξc=0(25.13)

The Drucker-Prager cone is matched to the Mohr-Coulomb hexagon in three ways: by its compressive meridian (outer cone), by its tensile meridian (inner cone), or so as to give the same limit load in plane strain:

η=3​(3∓sinφ)6sinφ​,ξ=3​(3∓sinφ)6cosφ​orη=9+12tan2φ​3tanφ​,ξ=9+12tan2φ​3​(25.14)

The Drucker-Prager return is in closed form on the smooth part of the cone, Δγ=f/(G+Kηηˉ​+ξ2H) (ηˉ​ computed with ψ), or on the apex. The Mohr-Coulomb return is performed in principal stress space, following de Souza Neto, Perić and Owen: return to the main plane, otherwise to the right or left edge (two active planes), otherwise to the apex; each is a linear system that is exact for linear hardening, and the first that gives positive multipliers and respects the order of the principal stresses is retained. The consistent tangent comprises, in addition to the principal part, the terms of rotation of the principal directions (σi​−σj​)/(εi​−εj​). It is not symmetric when ψ=φ: the global system is then solved by a sparse LU factorisation. In a triaxial compression test (φ=30°, c = 10 kPa, proportional loading, lateral stress one fifth of the axial stress), the limit load found by keta coincides with that of (25.13) to within 10−5 for both criteria (outer cone for Drucker-Prager); this case passes through the Mohr-Coulomb edge (σ1​=σ2​).

25.6.4 Global algorithm#

The load case is multiplied by a sequence of factors λk​; each interval is divided into equal substeps (4 by default), and each substep is solved by Newton's method from the accepted state of the previous step:

Kt​(ui​)Δu=λF−fint​(ui​),∥λF−fint​∥≤10−10max(∥λF∥,∥rsupports​∥)(25.15)

(at most 60 iterations; convergence is also accepted when the correction falls below 10−13 times the displacement, the limit of rounding for stiff materials). The imposed support displacements of the case are multiplied by the factor. The Gauss point states are accepted only at convergence of the substep. A substep without equilibrium (beyond the limit load) stops the calculation with an error or, on request, is halved from the last converged state, up to six times, and the calculation returns the factors reached. Each converged factor supplies the displacements, the reactions and, at the end, the stresses and the equivalent plastic strain at the Gauss points. A time can be associated with each factor; it is required as soon as a material creeps (a factor repeated at a later time is a creep hold).

As an option, the analysis is carried out in large displacements, in a total Lagrangian formulation: the strain is the Green-Lagrange strain E=21​(H+HT+HTH), H=∂u/∂X, the material is Saint-Venant-Kirchhoff (S=DE), and the tangent includes the geometric stiffness of the Piola-Kirchhoff stresses. This option is restricted to elastic plane and solid elements; the loads keep their direction.

The nonlinear analysis takes only models made of continuum elements: bars, shells, links and kinematic relations are refused there.

25.7 Meshing#

keta does not generate meshes of continuum elements: the nodes and the elements are supplied by the user, in the numbering order described above. keta's plane mesher is used for slabs (chapter 24) and for arbitrary sections (chapter 20). It is a constrained Delaunay triangulation: a triangle is accepted if no other vertex lies strictly inside its circumscribed circle, a criterion written, for a triangle abc traversed anticlockwise and a point d, as

​xa​−xd​xb​−xd​xc​−xd​​ya​−yd​yb​−yd​yc​−yd​​(xa​−xd​)2+(ya​−yd​)2(xb​−xd​)2+(yb​−yd​)2(xc​−xd​)2+(yc​−yd​)2​​>0⟺d lies inside the circle(25.16)

except across an imposed side (outline, hole, internal bar), which cannot be flipped. Ruppert's refinement then gives the requested size and minimum angle (chapter 24). The result is deterministic: the same data give the same nodes and the same triangles, in the same order.

25.8 Scope#

  • no mesher for continuum elements; no 10-node tetrahedral element, nor prismatic element;
  • stresses returned without subtracting the thermal strains; nodal averaging left to the user;
  • connection to bars and shells through the translations only;
  • continuum elements refused by buckling, second order, large displacements of bars and staged construction;
  • nonlinear analysis restricted to models made of continuum elements only; plasticity refused in plane stress; frictional laws in small displacements; large displacements in elasticity only.
