Chapter 24 Shells

This chapter deals with shell elements: slabs, deck slabs, walls, box-girder walls, curved shells. A shell is a thin solid, whose thickness t is small compared with its other dimensions; it carries load both in membrane action (forces in its plane) and in bending (moments and shear forces). keta represents it by flat three-node triangular facets, which share with the bars the six degrees of freedom of their nodes: a slab connects directly to its beams, its piers and its cables. The chapter describes the element (membrane, bending, enriched facets), its assembly with the bars and the meshing of slabs, the loads it receives, the prestress that passes through it, the internal forces it returns and its plastic behaviour.

Plane and solid continuum elements are described in chapter 25. Shells take part in static, modal, dynamic, buckling and second-order analyses (chapters 4, 15 and 17), in staged construction (chapter 10) and in influence surfaces (chapter 11).

24.1 Assumptions#

  • each facet is flat, with constant thickness (or linear over the facet for an enriched facet); a curved surface is approximated by facets;
  • bending follows Mindlin-Reissner theory: normals remain straight but not necessarily normal to the deformed surface, which gives transverse shear; the shear correction factor is 5/6;
  • the material is linear elastic, isotropic by default, in plane stress (σz​=0);
  • small strains and displacements within the element; large displacements are treated by the corotational analyses (chapter 17), which carry each facet in a frame that follows its rotation;
  • the geometric stiffness contains only the term of the membrane forces acting on the slope of the deflection;
  • the internal forces are returned at the centroid of each facet.

24.2 The facet#

24.2.1 Local axes and degrees of freedom#

Each node carries three translations (u,v,w) and three rotations (θx​,θy​,θz​) in the global axes, giving an 18×18 stiffness matrix per facet. The local axes are built from the order of nodes 1, 2, 3: the z axis is the normalised normal (p2​−p1​)×(p3​−p1​); the x axis is the projection of the global X axis onto the plane of the facet (of the Y axis if the normal is parallel to X, or of a direction given for the slab, for example that of a reinforcement layout); y=z×x. With the rotation R whose rows are the local axes, repeated over the six triplets of the facet in T,

K=TTKℓ​T,T=diag(R,R,R,R,R,R)(24.1)

In the local axes, the stiffness Kℓ​ is the sum of a membrane block on (u,v,θz​) and a bending block on (w,θx​,θy​), uncoupled for a homogeneous facet. A degenerate triangle (area less than 10−12 times the square of its longest side) is refused.

24.2.2 Allman membrane#

A constant-strain membrane triangle has no stiffness in rotation about its normal; assembled into non-coplanar facets or connected to a bar, it leaves mechanisms or forces the addition of a fictitious spring. keta uses the Allman triangle, which gives a true stiffness to the rotation θz​. Following Cook, it is written as the six-node quadratic triangle whose mid-side displacements are tied to the degrees of freedom of the vertices. On side i→j, with midpoint m:

um​=2ui​+uj​​+8(yj​−yi​)(θj​−θi​)​,vm​=2vi​+vj​​−8(xj​−xi​)(θj​−θi​)​(24.2)

The side therefore takes a parabolic normal displacement of amplitude L(θj​−θi​)/8 at its midpoint. The strain ε=Bm​dm​ is linear over the facet, and the membrane stiffness Km​=∫tBmT​Dm​Bm​dA, of degree 2, is integrated exactly by the three mid-side points (weights A/3), with Dm​=1−ν2E​​1ν0​ν10​00(1−ν)/2​​.

One zero-energy mode remains: the three rotations θz​ equal, translations zero. It is stabilised by a penalty on the difference between the mean rotation of the vertices and the rotation of the linear field of the vertices (Hughes and Brezzi):

Estab​=21​γGtA(θˉz​−ω)2,θˉz​=3θ1​+θ2​+θ3​​,ω=21​(∂x∂v​−∂y∂u​),γ=1(24.3)

This energy is zero for a rigid-body motion and for any constant-strain state whose nodal rotations are the rotation of the continuum: the patch test remains satisfied, and no returned internal force depends on it. It is the only fictitious stiffness of the element. The choice γ=1 (modulus G) makes twisted shells converge monotonically, where the rotation mode couples with the bending of neighbouring non-coplanar facets.

24.2.3 Mindlin-Reissner bending#

The rotations θx​, θy​ are the components of the rotation vector of the normal. A fibre at height z moves in the plane by (zθy​,−zθx​), hence the curvatures and the transverse shear strains

κx​=∂x∂θy​​,κy​=−∂y∂θx​​,κxy​=∂y∂θy​​−∂x∂θx​​,γxz​=∂x∂w​+θy​,γyz​=∂y∂w​−θx​(24.4)

and the forces per unit width

m=12t3​Dm​κ,q=65​Gtγ(24.5)

With linear rotations and deflection, transverse shear interpolated directly locks the element as the thickness decreases: a thin plate becomes infinitely stiff. keta uses the MITC3 element of Lee and Bathe: the covariant shear strains ert​, est​, computed from the displacements, are sampled at points A=(21​,0), B=(0,21​) and C=(21​,21​) of the reference triangle, and replaced by the assumed field

e~rt​=ertA​+cs,e~st​=estB​−cr,c=ertC​−estC​−ertA​+estB​(24.6)

constant along each side. The curvature is constant over the facet: bending is integrated exactly at one point; shear, of degree 2, by the three mid-side points.

The Mindlin reference is the Kirchhoff deflection of the Navier series, 0.0040624qa4/D, increased by the shear deflection M/(65​Gt) (relation of Wang, Reddy and Lee between simply supported Kirchhoff and Mindlin plates), where M=(mx​+my​)/(1+ν) is the sum of the Kirchhoff moments at the centre: 0.0040645qa4/D. With the “hard” support, keta's deflection differs from it by less than 2⋅10−4 in relative value at n=128. The “soft” support, where only the deflection is fixed, gives a shear boundary layer along the edges and a slightly more flexible plate: this is a fact of Mindlin theory, not a defect of the element, but the choice of support matters for thick plates.

24.2.4 Enriched facets#

A facet can carry additional data. As soon as it carries one, all its integrals are computed by the 7-point Dunavant rule, exact to degree 5:

  • a different thickness at each vertex, linear over the facet (haunches, deck slabs of variable thickness): membrane in t(ξ), bending in t(ξ)3/12;
  • an offset e of the mid-surface relative to the plane of the nodes, along the normal, treated as a rigid arm: um​=u+eθy​, vm​=v−eθx​;
  • an orthotropic material (moduli Ex​, Ey​, νxy​, Gxy​, Gxz​, Gyz​, axes rotated by a given angle), bending being that of the homogeneous plate t3Q/12;
  • a laminate: stacked orthotropic plies, with given thicknesses and orientations, according to classical lamination theory,
[nm​]=[AB​BD​][εκ​],(A,B,D)=k∑​Qˉ​k​(zk+1​−zk​, 2zk+12​−zk2​​, 3zk+13​−zk3​​)(24.7)
  • with a transverse shear ∑k​tk​Gk​ (oriented) and the factor 5/6; the ply thicknesses must add up to that of the facet;
  • a Winkler elastic foundation normal to the facet, of stiffness k (N/m³), which adds kA(1+δij​)/12 on the nodal deflections, the exact stiffness for a linear deflection.

24.2.5 Mass and geometric stiffness#

The consistent mass comprises the translational mass ρtA, distributed according to the consistent matrix of the linear triangle 12A​[211​121​112​] in each direction, and the rotary inertia ρt3/12 on θx​ and θy​. The rotation θz​ has no mass; as it has a stiffness, it is condensed by the modal analysis and creates no spurious mode. The lumped mass puts a third of the masses of the facet on each node: ρtA/3 on each translation and ρt3A/36 on θx​ and θy​.

Under membrane forces (nx​,ny​,nxy​) of a prestressed state, the geometric stiffness is the von Kármán term

Kg​=∫A​∇wT[nx​nxy​​nxy​ny​​]∇wdA(24.8)

exact at one point since ∇w is constant over the facet. It returns the plate buckling coefficient k=4 of the simply supported plate in compression and the second-order amplification 1/(1−P/Pcr​). The in-plane and rotational couplings of the initial state are not represented.

24.3 Assembly with the bars#

Since the facet has six degrees of freedom per node, including a true stiffness in θz​, a bar that arrives at a shell node connects to it without artifice, whatever its orientation. A beam offset below a slab (rigid arm, chapter 3) whose line of nodes lies in the plane of the slab acts compositely with it, provided that the beam and the slab share their nodes over their common length.

24.3.1 Meshing of slabs#

A slab is described by its outline, its holes, its thickness, its material and a target mesh size h. Its conversion into facets follows deterministic steps (same model, same mesh, same numbers):

  1. the plane of the slab passes through the first vertex of the outline, with the Newell normal of the outline; all vertices must lie within the merge tolerance of it (10−6 m by default);
  2. the frame bars whose two nodes lie in the plane and whose midpoint is strictly inside the slab are detected; two such bars that intersect elsewhere than at a common node are refused; a bar lying on the edge is not integrated;
  3. the existing nodes inside the slab (pier heads) become imposed vertices of the mesh;
  4. the domain is triangulated by a constrained Delaunay triangulation (Bowyer and Watson insertion, recovery of the imposed sides by edge flips), then refined by Ruppert's algorithm: the sides are first divided to length h, then every triangle that is too large or too flat receives a new vertex at its circumcentre;
  5. the mesh nodes are merged with the nearest existing nodes within the tolerance;
  6. the detected bars are split at the mesh vertices lying on their axis, keeping their material, their section, their offset (interpolated) and their loads (redistributed without changing their resultant).

A triangle is too large or too flat if

R>3​h​orℓmin​R​>2sinθmin​1​(24.9)

where R is the radius of the circumscribed circle, ℓmin​ the shortest side and θmin​ the target minimum angle (30° by default, between 20 and 34°). Termination of the algorithm is proven up to 20.7°; beyond, it is observed. A refinement that does not finish is an error or, if accepted, an explicit warning.

24.4 Loads#

24.4.1 Pressures#

A uniform pressure on a slab is expressed in three ways: a force vector per unit of true area in the global axes; a scalar pressure along the local normal of each facet; a force vector per unit of area projected onto the horizontal plane (snow, carriageway loads), which gives a facet inclined at β the force qAcosβ and nothing to a vertical facet. In all three cases, the force of the facet is shared in three equal thirds among its nodes, which is the consistent vector of a uniform load on a linear triangle; the consistent nodal moments are neglected. Self-weight follows the same rule; for a linear thickness, node i receives ρgA(2ti​+tj​+tk​)/12.

A pressure applied over an arbitrary polygon (a wheel contact area, a storage area), possibly with holes, is projected along a given direction onto the facets. For each facet, the area Ac​ of the intersection of the polygon and the projected triangle (Sutherland and Hodgman clipping) and its centroid c are computed exactly; the force qAc​ is distributed according to the barycentric coordinates of c, which is exact for a uniform pressure over part of the triangle:

fi​=q∫Ac​​Ni​dA=qAc​Ni​(c)(24.10)

The hydrostatic pressure p=γd, where d is the depth below a water level, zero above it, is applied along the normal with its consistent nodal forces, the facet being cut at the water line.

When the slab is not modelled, a surface load on a panel bounded by bars is transferred directly to these bars by the geometric rules described in chapter 3.

24.4.2 Thermal effects#

A uniform temperature change ΔT and a gradient ∂T/∂z through the thickness enter as initial strains, ε0​=αΔT(1,1,0) and κ0​=α∂z​T(1,1,0). The equivalent nodal forces are

f=tABmT​Dm​ε0​+ABbT​Db​κ0​(24.11)

exact since Bm​ is linear and Bb​ constant. The returned internal forces subtract these initial strains: a slab free to expand has no internal forces.

24.5 Prestressing in shells#

A prestressing tendon in a slab (post-tensioned slab, deck slab) is a polyline at its actual position in the thickness. It can be built from a plan layout and a longitudinal profile: between two extreme points (high and low points, with zero slope), two parabolas join at a given fraction of the interval; between an extreme point and a free point, a parabola with its vertex at the extreme point; between two free points, a straight line. Each interval is divided into 16 chords by default. The force along the tendon is

P(s)=P0​e−μα(s)−ks(24.12)

from the stressed end, α(s) being the cumulative angular deviation; a tendon stressed at both ends takes at each point the larger of the two values. The equivalent forces on the concrete are the force Pt at the live anchorage (and −Pt at the other anchorage), the deviation force Pafter​tafter​−Pbefore​tbefore​ at each vertex, and the friction force per unit length P′(s)t along each segment, integrated by 8 Gauss points on each piece of segment contained in a facet. Each force is transferred to the facet that contains the orthogonal projection of its point of application, with the moment r×F of its eccentricity relative to the mid-surface, and shared among the nodes by the linear shape functions. The system of forces is exactly self-equilibrated. In staged construction, the steel area, the modulus and the relaxation of the tendon are used to compute its time-dependent losses (chapters 8 and 10).

24.6 Supports and edge springs#

A line support is a support applied to each node lying within a tolerance of a segment. A spring distributed along a line of nodes (slab edge on an elastic support, semi-rigid joint between two slabs) has a stiffness per unit length k (6×6), expressed in the axes of each segment (x along the segment, z towards a given direction). It is lumped at the nodes: each node receives half of each of its two segments,

Knode​=adjacent segments∑​RT2kL​R(24.13)

in the form of elastic links to the ground or to the facing line of nodes, so that all the analyses that take links (including their nonlinear laws) take them. This lumping is exact for a uniform relative displacement along the line. A hinge line is built by doubling the nodes and tying their translations. Because the edge of an Allman facet bends with the rotations θz​ of its ends (24.2), keta can tie these rotations along a line of nodes (the normal of the facets must then be a global axis), so that a supported or loaded edge remains straight between its nodes.

24.7 Returned internal forces#

For each facet and each case, the forces per unit width are computed at the centroid, in the local axes, by subtracting the initial strains:

n=tDm​(ε−ε0​),m=Db​(κ−κ0​),q=65​Gtγ~​(24.14)

that is nx​, ny​, nxy​ (N/m), mx​, my​, mxy​ (N·m/m) and qx​, qy​ (N/m). The moment mx​ is the one that produces the stresses σx​; it is positive when it puts the +z face in tension. The principal values and their direction are computed by

m1,2​=2mx​+my​​±(2mx​−my​​)2+mxy2​​,tan2φ=mx​−my​2mxy​​(24.15)

(likewise for n). Principal values are never combined: in a combination or an envelope, the components are combined, then the principal values recomputed. keta does not smooth the internal forces at the nodes: the value at the centre of the facet is the most accurate value of the element, and the mesh must be fine enough where the internal forces vary quickly. The reinforcement design moments (Wood and Armer) are deduced from the combined (mx​,my​,mxy​), according to the directions of the reinforcement (chapter 21).

24.8 Plasticity of shells#

24.8.1 Layered law#

For a collapse analysis of a wall or a steel plate, keta integrates an elastic-plastic law through the thickness of each facet. At each in-plane integration point (the three mid-side points, or the seven Dunavant points of a variable-thickness facet) and at each height z, the strain is the Kirchhoff-Love strain ε(z)=ε0​+zκ and the transverse shear is distributed according to f(z)=45​(1−4z2/t2), a profile whose energy returns the factor 5/6. The criterion is von Mises in plane stress on (σx​,σy​,τxy​,τxz​,τyz​), with linear isotropic hardening:

f=21​σTPσ−31​R(α)2≤0,R=fy​+Hα(24.16)

where P is the plane-stress deviatoric projector and α the cumulative plastic strain. The return to the yield surface is the implicit Euler scheme of Simo and Taylor: since C and P have the same eigenvectors, the stress is written in closed form

σ(Δγ)=(C−1+ΔγP)−1C−1σtrial​(24.17)

and the consistency condition is a single equation in Δγ, solved by Newton's method to rounding. The consistent tangent matrix

Cep​=Ξ−σTPΞPσ+β(ΞPσ)(ΞPσ)T​,Ξ=(C−1+ΔγP)−1,β=1−32​HΔγ32​HσTPσ​(24.18)

gives quadratic convergence of the global equilibrium. Hill's orthotropic criterion (ratios of the yield strengths along the material axes) is also available, with the same algorithm; laminates and orthotropic facets integrate each ply separately. The thickness is integrated by a composite 4-point Gauss-Lobatto rule over 40 layers by default, exact to degree 5 per layer and whose end points are the faces: the surface stresses are read, not extrapolated. The stabilisation of the rotation θz​ remains elastic.

24.8.2 Analysis#

The load of the case is applied in 20 equal increments by default; each increment is solved by Newton's method with the consistent tangent (24.18):

Kt​(ui​)Δu=λk​F−fint​(ui​),λk​=20k​,∥λk​F−fint​∥≤10−10∥λk​F∥(24.19)

with at most 50 iterations per increment. The bars of the model remain elastic, unless they are given fibre sections or plastic hinges (chapter 18). As an option, the facets and the bars are treated in large displacements, in a corotational frame, with small strains. A load that exceeds the limit load finds no equilibrium: the calculation stops with an explicit error or, on request, divides the increment up to six times and returns the converged steps, which brackets the limit load. Cables are not admitted in this calculation.

24.9 Scope#

  • flat facets: a curved shell is approximated by facets, and its accuracy depends on the mesh;
  • internal forces at the centre of the facets, without smoothing at the nodes;
  • geometric stiffness limited to the membrane term acting on the slope of the deflection;
  • pressures distributed in thirds to the nodes, without consistent nodal moments;
  • automatic meshing of flat slabs only; a bar whose chord leaves the domain (concave outline, hole) makes the meshing fail; a bar on the edge is not integrated into the mesh;
  • plasticity of shells by the von Mises or Hill criterion: no cracked-concrete law in shells; cables refused in the plastic calculation.
