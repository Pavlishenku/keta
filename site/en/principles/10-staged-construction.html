Chapter 10 Staged construction

A concrete bridge is never loaded in its final state all at once. Its spans are cast or erected one after another, on falsework or props that are later removed, its tendons are stressed while the structure is still statically determinate, its balanced cantilevers are closed, and all the while the concrete creeps and shrinks. The final internal forces depend on this history: a load applied to a cantilever does not redistribute as if the span had been continuous from the outset, and creep slowly takes back part of the difference. This chapter describes how keta follows staged construction: the way it accumulates the increments of each stage, the operations a stage may contain, the treatment of creep, prestressing and cables from stage to stage, the tools specific to certain construction methods (form traveller, incremental launching) and the factored states it derives. The time-dependent laws are those of chapter 9, and the prestress losses those of chapter 8.

10.1 Assumptions#

  • each increment is computed linearly, under small displacements, on the substructure active at that moment (active bars, supports and links), except in second-order stages and for catenary cables;
  • an activated element is born stress-free in the position then occupied by its nodes;
  • states are superposed: the state at a stage is the sum of the increments since the origin;
  • time enters only through creep, shrinkage and relaxation, between the dates of the stages; the operations of a given stage are simultaneous as far as time is concerned;
  • time-dependent effects are decided by the materials (a concrete given a law creeps and shrinks) and by the tendons (a tendon given relaxation data relaxes), never by a flag in the staging plan;
  • shells are active from the first stage, cast at date zero; only bars are activated stage by stage.

10.2 Incremental superposition#

A staged analysis is a sequence of dated stages. Each operation of a stage produces a load increment ΔFk​ that is solved on the stiffness KAk​​ of the substructure Ak​ active at that moment, then added component by component to the cumulative state:

KAk​​Δuk​=ΔFk​,Sn​=k≤n∑​ΔSk​(10.1)

where S stands equally for the displacements, the reactions, and the internal forces at the ends and at the stations of the bars. There is no global solution: the forces “locked in” a structure whose static system has changed come from the fact that each increment saw only the structure of its own moment. Inactive bars carry zero internal forces. keta keeps one factorisation of the stiffness per distinct active substructure, so that a plan returning to a configuration already seen does not refactorise it; the key is only a hint, the assembled stiffness being compared before any reuse.

Within each stage, the operations are carried out in the following order, which is that of a construction site:

StepOperations
1relaxation of the strands still on their bed during the elapsed interval
2activations: added supports, bars, links
3creep and shrinkage over the elapsed interval, time-dependent losses of the tendons
4casting of parts of composite sections
5deactivations of bars and links, removal of supports
6changes of section, insertion of hinges, closures
7catenary cables: installation, adjustments, rupture
8grouting of ungrouted tendons
9support modifications, then jacking
10load cases of the stage
11stressing and destressing, group after group

After each increment, the bonded tendons already anchored undergo the corresponding elastic loss (chapter 8, rule K=1), which is itself an increment.

10.3 Activation and deactivation#

10.3.1 Activated bars#

An activated bar enters the stiffness of the subsequent increments without initial stress. Its internal forces are those of the increments it receives from its activation onwards:

Sbar​(t)=k:tk​≥tactivation​∑​ΔSbar,k​(10.2)

A bar that no activation list names is active from the start, cast at date zero. By default, the displacements at the stations of an activated bar count only the subsequent increments. Two options change this starting point without creating any internal force:

  • the bar is born straight on the displaced positions of its nodes: its stations start from the chord of the end translations, and the displacements printed are then totals, continuous with those of the nodes;
  • the bar extends the deformed structure: if only one of its nodes already belongs to the structure, the other takes the rigid-body motion of that node, uj​=ui​+θi​×(xj​−xi​), θj​=θi​. This is the case of a segment placed at the tip of a cantilever that has already rotated.

10.3.2 Concrete age and modulus#

The age a of a bar at the time of its activation may be given: the bar is then deemed to have been cast a days earlier. A negative age describes deferred casting: the bar is in place but neither creeps nor shrinks before it is cast. This age governs creep, shrinkage and, on request, the modulus: the stiffness of the increments of stage k, of date tk​, may use the modulus of the concrete law at the age reached,

tcast​=tactivation​−a,Ek​=E(tk​−tcast​)(10.3)

evaluated once per stage and per bar. By default, the modulus is that of the material.

10.3.3 Deactivation#

A deactivated bar exerted on its nodes the forces −f, opposite to its cumulative end forces f. Its removal therefore applies to the remaining structure the increment

ΔF=+fbar​(10.4)

(release of the locked-in forces). Its internal forces and its creep memory are erased: if it is reactivated later, it is born again stress-free. The deactivation of a bar on elastic soil is rejected. An elastic link is deactivated in the same way; a rigid link cannot be. Links activated during the staged analysis are also born free of force.

10.4 Supports#

10.4.1 Addition, removal and modification#

An added support restrains the displacements from the position reached: it carries a reaction only for the subsequent increments. A removed support releases its cumulative reaction R:

ΔF=−Rsupport​(10.5)

In a beam of two 6 m spans under 25 kN/m, the reaction 5qL/4=187.5 kN of the central support, removed afterwards, is thus transferred to the 12 m span and the end supports. A modified support takes on new conditions: a released component releases its reaction, and a newly restrained component is placed on the deformed structure, without reaction.

10.4.2 Jacking#

Jacking brings the total displacement of the restrained components of a support back to zero: the increment is an imposed displacement

Δusupport​=−usupportcumulative​(10.6)

on each non-zero restrained component. An arbitrary relative support displacement is applied by an imposed-displacement load case. Jacking an inclined support is rejected.

10.5 Hinges, closures and sections#

A stage hinge releases, for the subsequent increments, local components at one end of a bar. It is allowed only where the cumulative force on these components is zero (∣f∣≤10−9max(1,∥f∥∞​)): releasing a loaded component would shift the equilibrium, which is a matter for deactivation. Closure restores continuity: the relative rotation Δθopen​ acquired while the hinge was open remains frozen, at zero force, and only the subsequent increments see the continuity. Closure may in addition force a relative jump δ (axial gap, twist, rotations about the two section axes) in the closed joint, applied as a concentrated dislocation at the end:

ΔF=Kd∗(δ)(10.7)

free on a statically determinate structure, it creates a self-equilibrated stress state on a statically indeterminate one; this is the tool for adjustment by jacking at closure.

The section of an active bar may be changed: the internal forces acquired remain, and the subsequent increments see the new stiffness. The change is rejected for a concrete that creeps (the locked-in stress of the old section would creep like that of the new one), for a bar of variable inertia and for a composite bar. Concrete added to an existing section is cast as one part of a composite section: each part is born stress-free, has its own age and its own internal forces, and creeps with an implicit step of modulus

Eeff​=(E1​+Eref​(a1​)φ(a2​,a1​)​)−1(10.8)

over the interval [a1​,a2​], the earlier history of the part producing an imposed strain. The implicit step remains stable when a part almost entirely restrained by the others creeps strongly, which an explicit step does not.

10.6 Loads#

A load case named in a stage is applied once, to the active substructure of that stage, and then remains in the cumulative state. A span load on an inactive bar is not applied (the chronology check reports it). A form traveller is placed, moved forward and removed by load cases (next section); the weight of a segment is the load of the stage in which it is activated.

10.7 Form traveller#

A form traveller (or a launching girder) is a rigid body of weight W, with its centre of gravity at (xG​,yG​) in the plane normal to gravity, resting on the deck at N≥3 points. Its reactions are those of a rigid body. With three non-aligned points, statics gives them:

i∑​Fi​=W,i∑​Fi​xi​=WxG​,i∑​Fi​yi​=WyG​(10.9)

With more than three points, keta assumes identical elastic supports: the rigid body moves along a plane, the reactions are linear in the plane, Fi​=a+bxi​+cyi​, and (10.9) determines (a,b,c). This is the minimum-norm distribution. For example, 600 kN centred 1 m from the front line of a traveller on four supports 5 m apart give 240 kN on each front support and 60 kN on each rear support. A support whose reaction would be negative signals overturning, unless it is an anchorage, which can pull. The loads on the deck are the forces −Fi​ along gravity; a move of the traveller is a single load case: removal at the old position and placing at the new one. Supports of different stiffnesses or a flexible traveller require the traveller to be modelled in the structure.

10.8 Creep during the stages#

10.8.1 Imposed strains#

Between the date of the previous stage and that of the current stage, each active concrete bar (except those cast in the stage) receives its creep and shrinkage strain as an imposed strain, which the active structure, if statically indeterminate, redistributes. The methods for computing the creep strain are those of chapter 9. For a bar, the three generalised stress components N/A, My​/Iy​ and Mz​/Iz​ give the creep axial strain and the two creep curvatures:

Δε0​=C[AN​],Δκy​=C[Iy​My​​],Δκz​=C[Iz​Mz​​](10.10)

where C denotes the creep operator of the chosen method (for example ∑j​Δσj​[φ(a2​,tj​)−φ(a1​,tj​)]/Eref​(tj​) for the superposition of increments). The imposed curvatures have the sign that adds creep to the elastic deflection. The shrinkage of the interval is added to the axial strain. By default, the stress of the bar is taken at its central station and the imposed strain is uniform over the bar; as an option, it is computed at each station and applied as the piecewise linear field that it is, by two concentrated dislocations at the Gauss points of each interval between stations, which reproduce without error, for this field, the nodal forces and the displacements at the stations. This option is needed as soon as a bar is long compared with the variation of the stress: a span whose moment changes sign along its length.

10.8.2 Redistribution#

The textbook case is that of two simply supported loaded spans, then made continuous over their common support. With a non-ageing law φ(t−t0​)=φ∞​(1−e−α(t−t0​)) at constant modulus, the Volterra integral equation of continuity over the support is solved by Laplace transform:

MB​(t)=Mcont​1+φ∞​φ∞​​(1−e−α(1+φ∞​)(t−t0​))(10.11)

where Mcont​ is the support moment of the beam cast continuous. For two 20 m spans under 50 kN/m, Courbon's law (φ∞​=2, α=0.006 d−1) and creep computed along the bars, the superposition of increments gives, at 100, 1 000 and 10 000 days, errors of +7.8 %, −0.23 % and −0.11 % with four sub-steps per decade, and of +0.4 %, −0.06 % and −0.06 % with forty. Kelvin chains, with dates given at eight per decade, differ by +3.5 %, −1.9 % and +0.15 %; with only the three reading dates, their explicit integration oscillates (errors of +80 %, −84 % and +183 %). The single step, with eight dates per decade, tends towards the moment of the continuous beam: it overestimates the redistribution by 50 %. With the mid-bar scheme and four bars per span, the asymptote is overestimated by 4 %.

10.8.3 Order and consistency#

The creep of the interval is computed on the cumulative state at the start of the stage and applied after the activations of the stage: a bar cast in the stage does not creep over the interval, but it is part of the structure that redistributes the creep of the others. The time-dependent losses of the tendons over the interval are computed with the creep strains of the same engine (chapter 8), before the elastic redistribution of the creep increment. A bar carrying a strand still held by its bed shrinks freely, without creeping.

10.9 Prestressing in stages#

10.9.1 Stressing and destressing#

A stressing operation brings a tendon to a target fraction of its force P0​; the increment is the difference from the fraction already applied, and its equivalent loads are those of chapter 8, all the bars of the tendon having to be active. The tendons of a given group are stressed simultaneously (K=0 between them); the successive groups, and the later increments, cause losses in the tendons already anchored (K=1). A target below the applied fraction is a destressing: the jack releases the corresponding share of the current effective force,

ΔP=−fapplied​fapplied​−ftarget​​Peff​(10.12)

and the structure receives the opposite of the equivalent loads of this share, together with, if losses are kept locally, the same share of the loss field. A cycle of stressing followed by full destressing of a single tendon, without creep, leaves no internal force. Partial destressing restarts the relaxation clock on the new stress; full destressing takes the tendon out of service, and it may be restressed later.

10.9.2 Ungrouted tendons and grouting#

An ungrouted tendon slides in its duct: the variations of concrete strain along the tendon do not make it lose point by point. When losses are kept locally, keta first computes the force field that bond would give, Ti​, then projects it onto the profiles admissible under Coulomb friction: on each interval of the tendon grid, with friction exponent aj​=μ(Δθj​+khj​)+φhj​,

Nmin​ i∑​wi​(Ni​−Ti​)2subject toe−aj​Nj​≤Nj+1​≤eaj​Nj​(10.13)

Without friction, the solution is the weighted mean over the free length; otherwise, the intervals where a constraint is active slip and the others stick. Grouting makes the tendon bonded from that stage onwards. Partial grouting, over an interval of the chain, makes the tendon bonded over that interval; each neighbouring free run, anchored by the grouted zone, then slides between its own anchorages.

10.9.3 Pretensioning on a bed#

A strand stressed on its bed does not act on the structure; it relaxes during the intervals in which it remains there, the loss being taken by the bed. At release, it undergoes the thermal loss of curing, transfers its force by bond according to its force profile (transmission laws, chapter 8), then undergoes its own elastic shortening as a loss. Local losses distinguish the loss of the plateau from that of the transmission zones.

10.9.4 Shell tendons#

Tendons carried by shells are stressed by fractions of their force profile. They are bonded: each increment strains them by the mean strain of the concrete along their profile, and the loss, which reduces the whole profile uniformly, is iterated as for bar tendons. Between dates, they lose through creep and shrinkage through the structure and through EN 1992-1-1 relaxation in equivalent time. Their losses are not coupled with those of the bar tendons of a mixed model.

10.10 Catenary cables in stages#

A catenary cable (chapter 7) enters the staged analysis when an activation list names it; its two anchorages must then belong to the active structure. Its unstressed length L0​ is determined from its data (unstressed length, end tension or horizontal tension) at the positions of its anchorages at that moment. From then on, each increment is coupled:

KΔu=ΔF+c∑​[qc​(x+Δu; L0′​)−qc​(x; L0​)](10.14)

where qc​ is the force of the elastic catenary of cable c on its nodes, evaluated at the true positions x of its anchorages (cumulative displacements included). keta solves it by the modified Newton method on K+Kt​, Kt​ being the tangent stiffness of the cables at the start of the increment, factorised once, until the true residual is at the level of round-off errors. The change in the cable forces then becomes a nodal load of the increment, and the rest of the computation is linear. On an installed cable, a stage may impose a new unstressed length, a horizontal tension or an end tension (reached at the equilibrium of the increment, the anchorages moving with the adjustment, by a secant on L0​), a thermal expansion L0′​=L0​(1+αΔT), or rupture: the force of the cable is released statically, and a dynamic factor ϕ≥1 gives in addition the peak (ϕ−1) times this release, to be added to the cumulative state for the accidental situation. Catenary cables are rejected with pseudo-plastic soil, unilateral supports or rigid links; the frame remains under small displacements.

10.11 Analysis regimes#

10.11.1 Second order#

A stage may be declared second-order. Each increment then solves the equilibrium of the total state with the geometric stiffness of the total axial force:

[K+Kg​(N1​)]Δu=ΔF−e∑​[kg,e​(N1​)−kg,e​(N0​)]de​(10.15)

where N0​ is the cumulative axial force of each bar, N1​=N0​+N(Δu) is obtained by fixed-point iteration, and de​ is the displacement of the bar since its stress-free activation, increased by the geometric imperfection introduced in the stage. A later axial load thus amplifies an earlier deflection. On a 5 m cantilever column loaded laterally in a linear stage, then by 0.4Pcr​ in a second-order stage, keta amplifies the deflection by 1.657 619, against 1.657 619 for the analytical solution δ=H(tankL−kL)/(Pk). Stages that are all second-order give the result of a single second-order analysis. The analysis is rejected beyond the critical load (detected by the inertia of the factorisation), and with imposed displacements, installed catenary cables, nonlinear soil, unilateral supports, rigid links or shells. The creep and shrinkage increments of a second-order stage go through the same increment.

10.11.2 Large displacements#

A separate driver chains steps under large displacements (corotational formulation of chapter 17): each step activates bars and cables, adds its loads and may redefine unstressed lengths of cables, then is brought to equilibrium from the previous state. A bar placed between a node in place and a new node is placed stress-free in the deformed geometry; a bar placed between two nodes already in place (a closure) is placed with its own geometry, so that the gap strains it:

r(u)=Fint​(u)−k≤n∑​Fk​=0(10.16)

This driver handles neither creep nor prestressing, and rejects rigid links, constraints and skew supports.

10.12 Incremental launching#

A girder can be pushed along its axis only if this axis is a curve of constant curvature and torsion: a straight line (possibly on a gradient), a circle in plan or a helix. Launching is then a screw motion that preserves the curve and the direction of gravity, and each position is congruent to the girder in its reference position, the supports having moved along it. keta therefore keeps the mesh fixed and moves the supports: a pier at ground abscissa P carries, for an advance a, the point of the girder at abscissa

σ=P−a+Lg​(10.17)

measured from the rear end (a=0 puts the front of the girder, of length Lg​, at ground abscissa 0). The contact is placed at its true abscissa: a prismatic bar is split at that point in the model of the position (at the same fraction of the chord as of the alignment interval); within 10−7 of the bar length from a node, the contact is carried by the node. Splitting a bar of variable section, with rigid arms or carrying a tendon is rejected. Each engaged pier holds the girder vertically and laterally in the alignment axes at the contact; the first engaged pier in the list also holds the longitudinal direction and the torsion (the launching jacks). A position in which fewer than two piers carry the girder is declared unstable and is not computed.

For each position, under self-weight, keta gives the vertical reaction and the guiding force at each pier, the moment in the girder at the contact, the uplifted piers (negative reaction) and the front overhang (the launching nose, modelled by the leading bars) and rear overhang; it also gives the envelope of the moments at the stations. For a 40 m girder on three piers 20 m apart, it recovers, with the span L=20 m, the reactions 3/8, 10/8 and 3/8 of qL and the moment qL2/8 over the central pier; for a 15 m rear overhang, the moment q⋅152/2 at the contact.

10.13 States and factoring#

10.13.1 Registers by origin#

On request, keta keeps the cumulative state in three registers according to the origin of each increment, whose sum is the cumulative state:

S=S(G)+S(P0​)+S(ΔP)(10.18)

S(G) receives the loads, the creep and shrinkage of the structure (redistribution is classified with the permanent actions, as is customary) and the releases of locked-in forces; S(P0​) the stressing and destressing operations and the immediate losses they cause; S(ΔP) the time-dependent losses (creep, shrinkage, relaxation) and the elastic losses they bring about, with their sign: a loss is a negative prestressing effect. A deactivation, a removal or modification of a support, or jacking mix the origins in the same increment: the attribution is no longer unambiguous after them, keta records this and then rejects factored states.

10.13.2 Factored states#

A factored state multiplies each register by its coefficient:

Sfactored​=S+(kG​−1)S(G)+(kP0​​−1)S(P0​)+(kΔP​−1)S(ΔP)(10.19)

BPEL 91 (art. 4.1.2) defines the characteristic values P1​=1.02P0​−0.80ΔP and P2​=0.98P0​−1.20ΔP, that is the coefficients (1, 1.02, 0.80) and (1, 0.98, 1.20). EN 1992-1-1 §5.10.9 applies a single coefficient to the total prestress, rinf​ or rsup​, on S(P0​)+S(ΔP), the values being supplied by the engineer. The envelope of the factored states retains, component by component and independently for each origin, the favourable or unfavourable coefficient of each pair (eight coefficients: favourable and unfavourable kG​, kP0​​ and kΔP​, or kP​ for the total prestress). These states cover displacements, reactions, and internal forces in bars and links, not shells.

10.13.3 Primary and secondary effects#

At each recorded stage, keta computes the primary effect of the tendons acting on the structure, at the stations, by (8.19) with the effective force, and derives from it the secondary effect

Shyp=S(P0​)+S(ΔP)−Siso(10.20)

zero on a statically determinate structure, and linear between the supports of a continuous beam (EN 1992-1-1 §5.10.7). The primary effect is computed only for the internal forces at the stations (N, Vy​, Vz​, My​, Mz​): not for torsion, end forces or displacements.

10.14 Chronology check#

Before the analysis, keta can run through the plan dry, in the order of the engine, on a simulated state (bars, supports, links, cast parts, tendons, date), and report all the problems at once. An error is a condition that the analysis would reject or an operation that makes no sense; a warning, an operation that is legitimate but has no effect:

SeverityExamples
Errordate going backwards, unknown bar or node, bar already active, deactivation of an inactive bar or of a bar on soil, live tendon on a removed bar, removal of an inactive support, jacking of an inclined support, change of section of a concrete that creeps, hinge already inserted, closure without a hinge, catenary cable anchored outside the structure, second-order stage with elements that are not allowed
Warningspan load on an inactive bar, age given to a bar that the stage does not cast, extension without a node in place, grouting of an ungrouted tendon that was never stressed

An empty report proves that the references resolve and that the order is consistent; it proves neither the convergence of the analysis nor that a hinge is inserted where no force is locked in, which depends on the computed state.

10.15 Resuming a staged analysis#

The state of a staged analysis between two stages can be resumed to continue with another plan, without recomputing the common prefix: variants of the end of construction, study of several closure dates. The resumed state comprises the cumulative state, the active substructure, the accumulated forces of each bar and each support, the state of the tendons (fractions, losses, local fields, relaxation clocks), the creep memories of each station and the casting dates. The model of the continuation must contain the footprint of the prefix: same nodes, same bars, same supports, same tendons already stressed; it may add bars, tendons and cases. Since each increment was solved on the active substructure alone, the continuation is then identical, bit for bit, to the analysis of the whole chain on the new model:

Snresumed​=Snfull chain​(10.21)

A model that does not contain the footprint, or an option on which the increments depend, is rejected with the reason. The identity of the constitutive laws, which are functions, is the responsibility of the engineer.

10.16 Results provided#

For each stage: the cumulative state and the increment of the stage (nodal displacements, reactions, internal forces at the ends and at the stations of the bars, link forces), the applied force, the cumulative loss and the effective force of each tendon, the state of the catenary cables (unstressed length, horizontal tension, end tensions), the internal forces of each part of the composite sections and, on request, the registers by origin, the primary effect and the active substructure, whose tangent stiffness serves for subsequent analyses (eigenmodes, stability) about the state of the stage.

10.17 Scope#

  • The increments are linear, except for second-order stages and catenary cables; the large-displacement driver is separate and has neither creep nor prestressing.
  • Shells are not activated in stages.
  • Jacking resets the displacement of a support to zero; resetting its reaction to zero, imposing an absolute displacement and jacking by a factor are not offered.
  • A hinge cannot be replaced directly by another: it must be closed and a new one inserted.
  • Launching knows only the self-weight of the girder, on a straight, circular or helical alignment.
  • In the incremental fibre method, the arrangement of the fibres (two in a plane, four in space) must match the bending plane studied: two fibres arranged along the local y axis do not see bending about that axis.
