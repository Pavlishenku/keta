Chapter 14 Fatigue

At every passage of a vehicle, a bridge undergoes a stress variation in each of its details. These variations, repeated millions of times, damage the steel of reinforcement, tendons and connections well below its static strength. A fatigue verification calls for three ingredients: a fatigue loading representative of the traffic, the stress history it produces at the detail studied, and the count of the cycles of that history, which is finally set against the resistance curve of the detail. keta provides the first two and the count: the road fatigue load models of EN 1991-2 §4.6, the service trains and railway traffic mixes of annex D, the dynamic factor specific to railway fatigue, the history of the effect of a vehicle crossing the structure, and rainflow counting. Damage summation and resistance curves are not programmed, with the exception of the quantities of the simplified method of EN 1992-1-1 §6.8. The influence lines on which the vehicles travel are those of chapter 11; the static load models are those of chapters 12 and 13.

14.1 Assumptions#

  • The structure is linear elastic in small displacements: the stress at a point is a linear combination of the internal forces of the section, and its history under a vehicle is read on the influence line of that stress.
  • The passage of a vehicle is quasi-static: the history of the effect is the sequence of the static states of the vehicle at its successive positions. Dynamic amplification is a multiplying factor (railway fatigue) or is included in the loads (road models).
  • A vehicle is a set of point axles and uniform loads at fixed distances; the wheel contact areas and the transverse arrangement of the wheels are not modelled on an influence line.
  • The count is made on a series of scalar values; the frequency and the order of the vehicle passages in actual traffic are not simulated.

14.4 History of the effect of a vehicle#

When the reference of the vehicle stands at r, the effect studied is, as in chapter 12,

E(r)=j∑​Pj​η(r+aj​)+k∑​ℓk​Pk​​∫r+bk​r+bk​+ℓk​​η(s)ds(14.8)

and the history is this function run through from the entry of the vehicle onto the line to its exit. For a fibre stress σ=∑i​ci​Si​, a linear combination of the internal forces Si​ of the section, the line ησ​=∑i​ci​ηi​ is used: this gives the history of the stress itself, which the combination of the extremes of the internal forces does not give.

A uniform sampling (500 steps by default) may miss a peak. keta therefore offers a sampling at the breakpoints: the positions r=si​−aj​ where an axle passes over a point si​ of the line, and r=si​−bk​, r=si​−bk​−ℓk​ for the ends of the uniform loads. For a piecewise affine line and point axles, E(r) is affine between two consecutive positions of this set: the extrema of the history, hence the ranges counted afterwards, are among the positions computed. For a cubic line or uniform loads, keta adds eight interior points per interval; the result is then approximate, the error being that of sampling a polynomial of degree three or four with ten points. The service trains go through the same calculation, and the dynamic factor is applied by multiplying the history.

14.5 Rainflow counting#

The history is first reduced to its turning points: plateaus (consecutive equal values) are merged, the first and the last points are kept, and an interior point is kept when the slope changes sign. The cycles are then counted by the three-point method of ASTM E1049-85 §5.4.4. The points are pushed onto a stack one by one; at each addition, the most recent range X is compared with the previous one Y:

X=∣pn​−pn−1​∣,Y=∣pn−1​−pn−2​∣,X≥Y ⇒ count Y(14.9)

If Y contains the starting point of the history, it is a half cycle and the starting point leaves the stack; otherwise it is a full cycle and its two points leave the stack. The comparison is repeated as long as it counts a cycle, then the next point is read. At the end, the ranges remaining in the stack are half cycles. Each cycle is returned with its range, its mean (pmax​+pmin​)/2 and its count (1 or 0.5), in the order in which it is found. The cost is proportional to the number of points. On the series of the example of the standard, keta recovers its result: 0.5 cycle of range 3, 1.5 cycles of range 4, 0.5 of range 6, 1 of range 8 and 0.5 of range 9.

A single passage of a vehicle over an influence line gives mostly half cycles, since the history starts from zero and returns to it. For a traffic mix, the user concatenates the histories of the passages in the order of their choice, or counts each passage separately and multiplies by the number of passages; these two counts differ in the treatment of the residual half cycles.

For reinforced concrete, keta provides the three quantities of the simplified method of EN 1992-1-1 §6.8: the design fatigue strength of concrete (expression 6.76), the resistance stress range of straight and bent bars at N∗=106 cycles, ΔσRsk​ = 162.5 MPa (table 6.3N, or 175 MPa according to the German National Annex), and the damage-equivalent stress range:

keta contains neither the S-N curves of the detail categories of EN 1993-1-9 or of tables 6.3N and 6.4N of EN 1992-1-1, nor the Palmgren-Miner damage summation,

D=i∑​N(Δσi​)ni​​≤1(14.11)

nor the damage equivalence factors λ of road and railway bridges. The verification is made outside keta: the pairs (range Δσi​, number ni​) from rainflow counting, multiplied by the number of passages of each vehicle or train, are set against the resistance curve of the detail. Fatigue verifications of prestressing steel and of concrete in compression or tension are not provided either.
