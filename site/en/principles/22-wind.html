Chapter 22 Wind

This chapter deals with the action of wind on a framed structure: deck, pylon or pier. As in EN 1991-1-4, the wind is split into a ten-minute mean velocity, which increases with height, and a zero-mean turbulence. The mean velocity gives static loads; the turbulence gives random forces that the structure filters through its natural modes. When the structure moves, the flow around it changes: the resulting forces, called aeroelastic, modify the apparent damping and stiffness and can lead to an instability, flutter. keta computes in turn the wind profile and the static load cases, the response to turbulent wind in the frequency domain and then in the time domain, the critical flutter velocity, the equivalent static loads and the concomitant internal forces.

The natural modes used are those of chapter 15, possibly computed about a loaded state (chapter 17). Vortex shedding is the subject of a short section at the end of the chapter.

22.1 Assumptions#

  • the wind blows horizontally, in a given direction d; the vertical component of the direction supplied is ignored. z denotes the upward vertical and a=z×d the pitch axis, horizontal and perpendicular to the wind;
  • the mean velocity follows the logarithmic profile of EN 1991-1-4, with a constant orography factor;
  • the turbulence is a stationary Gaussian process; its three components u (along-wind), v (lateral) and w (vertical) are mutually uncorrelated;
  • the aerodynamic forces are computed by strips: each bar is a strip of length L, represented by its midpoint, where the flow is two-dimensional. Cables receive no wind;
  • a single aerodynamic section (reference width B, coefficients CD​, CL​, CM​) applies to the whole model;
  • the turbulence forces are quasi-steady (Davenport and Scanlan theory), possibly corrected by an admittance; they are linear in u and w;
  • the structure responds linearly about its mean state, on a finite number of modes normalised to unit mass;
  • flutter is treated with the self-excited forces of the Theodorsen flat plate or with Scanlan flutter derivatives given as a table.

22.2 Mean wind#

22.2.1 EN 1991-1-4 profile#

The terrain is characterised by its roughness length z0​ and the minimum height zmin​ of its category. The terrain factor, the roughness factor and the mean velocity at height z are those of EN 1991-1-4 §4.3.2, expressions (4.3) to (4.5):

kr​=0.19(0.05z0​​)0.07,vm​(z)=kr​ln(z0​ze​​)c0​vb​,ze​=max(z,zmin​)(22.1)

where vb​ is the basic wind velocity and c0​ the orography factor. The turbulence intensity and the peak velocity pressure are those of expressions (4.7) and (4.8):

Iv​(z)=c0​ln(ze​/z0​)kI​​,qp​(z)=[1+7Iv​(z)]21​ρvm​(z)2(22.2)

with by default kI​=1, ρ=1.225 kg/m³, vb​=26 m/s and z0​=0.05 m (category II). Below zmin​, the profile is held at its value at zmin​. The upper bound zmax​=200 m of the standard is not enforced. The figure below shows qp​(z) computed by keta for the terrain categories of the standard.

22.2.2 Static load cases#

The mean wind on a strip of width B produces, per unit length, a drag along d, a lift along z and a pitching moment about a:

D=qBCD​,L=qBCL​,M=qB2CM​(22.3)

The moment is positive nose-up: it raises the windward edge. keta builds two load cases of this form, which differ by the pressure q:

  • the peak wind case takes q=qp​(z) from (22.2). It applies to each bar a linearly varying load between the pressures at its two ends, evaluated at their heights, and multiplies it by the yaw factor sin2β=1−(ex​⋅d)2, where ex​ is the bar axis: only the component of the flow perpendicular to the bar loads it (independence principle). A bar parallel to the wind receives nothing;
  • the mean wind case of the turbulence model takes the mean pressure q=21​ρvm​(z)2, with neither gust factor nor yaw factor, and with the coefficients read from the incidence table at the mean incidence α0​. It is the mean state about which the following sections compute the fluctuations.

In both cases, the distributed moment, varying linearly between m1​ and m2​ at the ends of a bar of length L, is replaced by two nodal moments L(m1​/3+m2​/6) and L(m1​/6+m2​/3), which are its consistent nodal forces for a linear interpolation.

22.2.3 Coefficients as functions of incidence#

The coefficients depend on the incidence α of the wind on the deck. They are given by a table (αk​,CD,k​,CL,k​,CM,k​), with strictly increasing incidences, interpolated linearly. The slope used by the turbulence and aeroelastic terms is that of the segment containing α:

C′(α)=αk+1​−αk​Ck+1​−Ck​​,αk​≤α<αk+1​(22.4)

At a point of the table, the slope is the mean of the two adjacent segments. Outside the table, the coefficients are held constant and their slopes are zero: a mean incidence outside the measured range does not create a slope by extrapolation. A section described only by its coefficients and two slopes CL′​, CM′​ is converted into a linear table over [−1,1] rad; its drag has no slope.

22.3 Atmospheric turbulence#

22.3.1 Spectra of the three components#

At a point at height z, the mean velocity U=vm​(z) and the standard deviation σu​=Iv​(z)U come from the profile. The transverse standard deviations are taken in proportion, σv​=rv​σu​ and σw​=rw​σu​, with by default rv​=0.75 and rw​=0.5. The along-wind integral length scale follows EN 1991-1-4, expression (B.1),

Lu​(z)=300(200ze​​)α,α=0.67+0.05lnz0​(22.5)

and the scales of the other components in proportion: Lv​=Lu​/4 and Lw​=Lu​/12 by default. The standard gives only the u component; the ratios for v and w are user parameters, whose default values are usual orders of magnitude.

The one-sided spectrum Sa​(n) of component a, in (m/s)²/Hz, is written in reduced form as a function of the reduced frequency f=nLa​/U. keta offers the form of EN 1991-1-4, expression (B.2), applied to each component on its own length scale, and the von Kármán forms:

σa2​nSa​(n)​=⎩⎨⎧​(1+10.2f)5/36.8f​(1+70.8f2)5/64f​(1+283.2f2)11/64f(1+755.2f2)​​EN 1991-1-4, a=u,v,wvon Kaˊrmaˊn, a=uvon Kaˊrmaˊn, a=v,w​(22.6)

These forms are normalised: the integral of Sa​ over all frequencies equals σa2​. This is what permits their use with the standard deviation taken from the profile; the Kaimal forms written with the friction velocity (200f/(1+50f)5/3 and similar) carry between 6 and 18 times the variance when used with σu2​, and keta does not use them. Numerical integration of the three forms returns σa2​ to within 4⋅10−5 for the form of the standard and to within 2⋅10−4 for the von Kármán forms, whose published constant 70.8 is rounded.

22.3.2 Spatial coherence#

Two points j and k separated by Δy laterally (along a) and by Δz vertically see fluctuations that are less correlated the higher the frequency. keta takes the Davenport coherence, real, per component:

coha​(n)=exp(−Uj​+Uk​2n(Cay​Δy)2+(Caz​Δz)2​​)(22.7)

The reference velocity is the mean of the velocities at the two points. The default decay coefficients are Cuy​=Cuz​=10, Cvy​=Cvz​=6.5 and Cwy​=Cwz​=3. The longitudinal separation (along the wind) does not enter the coherence. The cross-spectral density of the velocities at points j and k is then

Sa,jk​(n)=Sa,j​(n)Sa,k​(n)​coha​(n),(22.8)

and the three components form a symmetric 3N×3N cross-spectral matrix, with uncoupled blocks, for N points.

22.3.3 Aerodynamic admittance#

A gust shorter than the width of the deck does not load all of it: the actual force is lower than the quasi-steady force. This is accounted for by an admittance χ2(K), a function of the reduced frequency on the half-width K=ωB/(2U)=πnB/U. keta offers four choices: the quasi-steady admittance χ2=1; the exact Sears function for a flat plate traversed by a vertical gust,

S(K)=[J0​(K)−iJ1​(K)]C(K)+iJ1​(K),χ2=∣S(K)∣2,(22.9)

where C(K) is the Theodorsen function (22.18); the Liepmann approximation χ2=1/(1+2πK); a table (K,χ2) interpolated linearly and held constant outside its bounds. The admittance may differ for each of the three forces (drag, lift, moment). It applies in amplitude, χr​ on force r, so that the cross terms of two forces carry χr​χs​.

22.4 Response to turbulent wind#

22.4.1 Buffeting forces#

On a strip subjected to the fluctuations u and w, the relative velocity and the incidence change; to first order, the forces per unit length fluctuate by

​DLM​​=UqB​​2CD​2CL​2BCM​​CD′​−CL​CL′​+CD​BCM′​​​[uw​]=A[uw​],q=21​ρU2(22.10)

with the coefficients and their slopes at the mean incidence α0​. The drag follows the wind, the lift is vertical and the moment acts about a. The lateral component v (yaw) is not taken into account. Each strip has its own mean velocity, standard deviations and length scales, evaluated at the height of its midpoint.

22.4.2 Modal forces#

For mode i, φi,s​ denotes the vector (translation along d, vertical translation, rotation about a) at the midpoint of strip s, the mean of the modal values at its two nodes. The modal force is Qi​=∑s​Ls​φi,s​⋅As​(us​,ws​). Since u and w are uncorrelated, the matrix of cross-spectral densities of the modal forces is

SQ​(n)=Gu​Su​(n)GuT​+Gw​Sw​(n)GwT​,Gc,is​=Ls​φi,s​⋅As,c​χs​(n)(22.11)

where Su​ and Sw​ are the cross-spectral matrices (22.8) of the strip midpoints and As,c​ the column of A associated with component c, each row being multiplied by the admittance of its force. The cost of one frequency is O(mN2) for m modes and N strips.

22.4.3 Modal response#

The modal coordinates q, with unit masses, satisfy

q¨​+(2ζΩ+Cae​)q˙​+(Ω2+Kae​)q=Q(t)(22.12)

where Ω and ζ are the natural circular frequencies and the structural damping ratios, and Cae​, Kae​ the aeroelastic matrices of the next section (zero if they are not requested). With the transfer function H(ω)=[Ω2+Kae​−ω2I+iω(2ζΩ+Cae​)]−1, the spectral density of a response r=ψ⋅q (a nodal displacement, ψ being the row of the modes at that degree of freedom) and its variance are

Sr​(n)=ψTH(2πn)SQ​(n)H(2πn)∗ψ,σr2​=∫0∞​Sr​(n)dn(22.13)

The cross terms between modes come directly out of this matrix product: there is no modal combination rule, and two close modes whose forces are coherent add up as the physics requires. The integral is computed by the trapezoidal rule on a logarithmic frequency grid of 300 intervals between 0.02n1​ and 5nm​, supplemented by 120 points distributed over ni​(1±25ζi​) around each natural frequency: the resonance peak, of width ζi​ni​, is thus described by several tens of points whatever the damping. The full modal covariance Cq​=∫HSQ​H∗dn is also supplied; it is used for the equivalent loads.

22.4.4 Background and resonant parts, peak value#

The background (quasi-static) part is the response obtained by replacing H(ω) by H(0)=(Ω2+Kae​)−1; the resonant part is the remainder:

σB2​=∫0∞​ψTH(0)SQ​H(0)Tψdn,σR2​=σr2​−σB2​(22.14)

For a lightly damped oscillator, σR2​ tends to the Davenport formula πn0​SQ​(n0​)/(4ζω04​), from which it differs by a term in O(ζ). The peak value over the duration T (600 s by default) is gσr​, with the Davenport up-crossing rate and peak factor:

ν=∫Sr​dn∫n2Sr​dn​​,g=2ln(νT)​+2ln(νT)​0.5772​(22.15)

The peak factor is set to zero when νT≤1. The quantities supplied for each requested response are σr​, σB​, σR​, ν and g; the extreme value is rˉ±gσr​, where the mean response rˉ comes from the static analysis of the mean wind case. The up-crossing rate and the peak factor are those of the total response: keta does not compute a separate peak factor for each part.

22.4.5 Aeroelastic terms#

When the strip moves with velocity (x˙D​,x˙L​) and rotates by θ, the relative wind becomes u−x˙D​, w−x˙L​ and the incidence α0​+θ. The corresponding quasi-steady forces, moved to the left-hand side of (22.12), give the modal matrices

Cae,ij​=s∑​Ls​φi,sT​[As​0]φj,s​,Kae,ij​=−s∑​Ls​qs​Bφi,sT​​000​000​CD′​CL′​BCM′​​​φj,s​(22.16)

The first is an aerodynamic damping coupled between modes, which includes the drag damping ρUBCD​; the second an aerodynamic stiffness due to the change in incidence. For a deck in pure heave with mass per unit length m, the classical aerodynamic damping ratio ζa​=ρUB(CL′​+CD​)/(4mω) is recovered. Before any calculation, keta checks that all the eigenvalues of the state-space system [0−(Ω2+Kae​)​I−(2ζΩ+Cae​)​] have a strictly negative real part. Otherwise the calculation is refused: a negative total damping (galloping), a divergence or a quasi-steady flutter has no stationary spectral response.

Note

A second, simpler calculation takes only the drag under the u component alone, with a single spectrum, a single coherence coefficient and the square-root-of-sum-of-squares combination of the modes. It ignores the cross terms between modes and is suitable only for well-separated modes.

22.5 Flutter#

22.5.1 Theodorsen flat plate#

Flutter is a dynamic instability: beyond a critical velocity, the self-excited forces supply more energy than the damping dissipates, and a coupled bending and torsional oscillation grows. For a flat plate of half-chord b, whose elastic axis lies ab behind mid-chord, in heave h (positive downwards) and pitch α (positive nose-up), Theodorsen's theory gives the lift L (upwards) and the moment M about the elastic axis:

LM​=πρb2(h¨+Uα˙−baα¨)+2πρUbC(k)[h˙+Uα+b(21​−a)α˙]=πρb2[bah¨−Ub(21​−a)α˙−b2(81​+a2)α¨]+2πρUb2(a+21​)C(k)[h˙+Uα+b(21​−a)α˙]​(22.17)

Theodorsen's circulation function, of the reduced frequency k=ωb/U, is written with the Hankel functions of the second kind:

C(k)=F(k)+iG(k)=H1(2)​(k)+iH0(2)​(k)H1(2)​(k)​≈1−1−0.0455i/k0.165​−1−0.3i/k0.335​(22.18)

keta evaluates the exact form through Bessel functions (C(0.1)=0.8319−0.1723i and C(1)=0.5394−0.1003i, values from the tables of Bisplinghoff, Ashley and Halfman) for the Sears admittance, and R. T. Jones's approximation, on the right, in the flutter calculations. The difference between the two stays below 0.015 in modulus for k from 0.01 to 10; it is largest near k=0.4.

The two-degree-of-freedom section has mass per unit length m, pitch inertia I about the elastic axis, static unbalance S, uncoupled circular frequencies ωh​, ωα​ and damping ratios ζh​, ζα​. Its equation of motion is [mS​SI​]x¨+Cs​x˙+Ks​x=(−L,M), where the forces (22.17) are moved to the left-hand side as aerodynamic mass, damping and stiffness matrices that depend on k.

22.5.2 p-k method#

At a given velocity U, each branch (the heave branch starts from ωh​, the pitch branch from ωα​) is tracked by the p-k method: the aerodynamic forces are frozen at the current circular frequency ω, the eigenvalues p of the quadratic problem

det[p2M(ω)+pC(ω)+K(ω)]=0(22.19)

are computed through its state-space form of double order, the eigenvalue with positive imaginary part closest to ω is retained, and the process is repeated with ω=Imp until Imp no longer changes (relative difference 10−9, at most 60 iterations). The damping ratio of the branch is ζ=−Rep/∣p∣. The velocity is swept in regular steps over the requested interval; the flutter velocity is the first at which the damping of the least damped branch changes from positive to negative, refined by 60 bisections. The flutter frequency is that of the branch that loses its damping.

22.5.3 Scanlan derivatives#

A real deck is not a flat plate. Its self-excited forces in harmonic motion are measured in a wind tunnel and given by the Scanlan flutter derivatives, functions of the reduced frequency K=Bω/U on the full width. With L and h positive downwards, M and α nose-up:

LM​=qB[KH1∗​Uh˙​+KH2∗​UBα˙​+K2H3∗​α+K2H4∗​Bh​]=qB2[KA1∗​Uh˙​+KA2∗​UBα˙​+K2A3∗​α+K2A4∗​Bh​]​(22.20)

which amounts, in harmonic motion, to adding to the left-hand side the impedance

Z=−qK2[H4∗​+iH1∗​B(A4∗​+iA1∗​)​B(H3∗​+iH2∗​)B2(A3∗​+iA2∗​)​],(22.21)

whose real part is a stiffness and whose imaginary part divided by ω is a damping. The eight derivatives are given as a table as functions of K, interpolated linearly and held constant outside the table. The drag derivatives Pi∗​ are not taken into account. keta can also derive the flat-plate derivatives from (22.17), and recovers the closed forms of Simiu and Scanlan, for example H1∗​=−2πF/K and A2∗​=−π(K/4−G−KF/4)/(2K2), to within 3⋅10−14.

22.5.4 Multimodal analysis#

On a model, each bar is a strip of length Ls​ that moves as xs​=(hs​,αs​)=Ts​q: hs​ is minus the vertical translation of its midpoint and αs​ its rotation about a, for each retained mode. Drag is ignored. The modal impedance is the sum of the projected strip impedances:

Zq​(U,ω)=s∑​Ls​TsT​Zs​(U,ω)Ts​(22.22)

and the p-k problem becomes det[p2(I+Ma​)+p(2ζΩ+Ca​)+Ω2+Ka​]=0, each branch starting from a natural circular frequency (tolerance 10−10, at most 100 iterations). The strips carry either the Theodorsen matrices (22.17) or the Scanlan derivatives. Two modes of a section model return the velocity of the two-degree-of-freedom calculation; a simply supported deck, whose bending and torsional modes are sines, reduces exactly to a section with masses 1/∑s​Ls​φs2​.

Important

The critical velocity is detected by the sweep. A range of negative damping that opens and closes between two velocities of the sweep is not seen: the step must be reduced if several branches cross in the interval. Nor does the calculation say whether the flutter is pure torsional or coupled: reading the branches shows it.

22.6 Time-domain response#

22.6.1 Wind field synthesis#

A step-by-step analysis needs velocity histories at the N strip midpoints that respect the spectra and the coherences. keta uses the spectral representation of Shinozuka and Deodatis with double-indexed frequencies. For one component (u or w), let H(n) be the Cholesky factor of the N×N cross-spectral matrix, S(n)=HH∗; the velocity at point j is

vj​(t)=m=1∑j​l=1∑nf​​2Δn​​Hjm​(nml​)​cos(2πnml​t+ϕml​),nml​=(l−1)Δn+Nm​Δn(22.23)

with Δn=nmax​/nf​ and phases ϕml​ uniform on [0,2π[, drawn by a generator with a fixed seed, independent of the platform (the Davenport coherence being real, the phase angles of the terms of H are zero). The sum is evaluated by one fast Fourier transform per point, over the period T=N/Δn divided into a power-of-2 number of steps, at least four times the number of frequencies. All the frequencies being distinct, the cosines are orthogonal over one period: the time variance and covariances of the series are exactly ∑ml​ΔnHjm​Hkm​, that is the discretised target spectrum, and not a statistical estimate. The variance carried differs from the integral of the continuous spectrum by the discretisation in Δn and by the truncation: the part of the spectrum beyond nmax​ is lost.

22.6.2 Step-by-step integration#

The modal forces are formed at each step with the quasi-steady matrix (22.10), without admittance (the admittance is a frequency filter that has no direct equivalent in time). The modal system (22.12), with its aeroelastic terms if requested, is integrated by the average-acceleration Newmark method, unconditionally stable and second-order accurate:

[Ω2+Kae​+Δt2​(2ζΩ+Cae​)+Δt24​I]qk+1​=Qk+1​+…(22.24)

The force history of one period is repeated (two periods by default); the first is discarded as the transient. For each requested displacement, keta supplies the retained series, its standard deviation about its mean, its maximum and its minimum. On a 200 m deck in twenty strips, without admittance, with three modes and 1024 frequencies up to 2 Hz, the mean of the standard deviations of four realisations recovers the standard deviation of the spectral calculation of the mid-span deflection to within 0.1 %.

22.7 Equivalent static loads#

A spectral calculation gives standard deviations of responses, not a load distribution that could be entered in a static analysis to obtain the concomitant internal forces. keta builds this distribution by Kasperski's load-response correlation method. For loads F with covariance CF​ and a target response r=I⋅F, the load

Fe​=gITCF​I​CF​I​(22.25)

gives exactly r(Fe​)=gσr​, since ITCF​I=σr2​, and it is the most probable load distribution among those that produce this value. In modal space, with the covariance Cq​ of the modal coordinates and r=ψ⋅q, one forms qe​=gCq​ψ/ψTCq​ψ​, then the inertia forces

F=MΦΩ2qe​(22.26)

which reproduce these modal amplitudes in a static analysis, since K−1Mφi​ωi2​=φi​. The background and resonant parts and the cross terms between modes are included together. A linear static analysis of this case returns the target peak value gσr​ to rounding, and supplies at the same time all the concomitant internal forces. For a single mode, the load reduces to gσqi​​ωi2​Mφi​. The total design load is the sum of the mean wind case and this peak load.

22.8 Nonlinear loop#

On a structure in high tension or high compression (cable-stayed deck, arch, pylon), the axial force modifies the natural frequencies, and the wind itself modifies the axial force. keta iterates the following chain, to second order (chapter 17):

  1. second-order static analysis under the permanent loads, the mean wind and, from the second iteration on, the equivalent peak load; the axial force of each bar is taken from it;
  2. natural modes about this axial force (geometric stiffness);
  3. spectral response of the target response on these modes, then peak load (22.26) by the modal load-response correlation;
  4. second-order static analysis under permanent loads, mean wind and peak: extreme value of the target response and new axial force.

The iteration stops when the standard deviation and the extreme of the target response change by less than 10−6 in relative value (at most 20 iterations, 6 modes by default). Without axial force, it returns the ordinary spectral calculation in two iterations. On a simply supported beam of span L tensioned by a force T, the frequency of the first bending mode becomes

f1​=f0​1+π2EITL2​​(22.27)

and the response decreases accordingly. The loop relies on the geometric stiffness of the axial force: it does not take the tangent state of a large-displacement analysis with cables.

22.9 Concomitant internal forces#

The design of a section requires triplets of concomitant internal forces, for example (N,My​,Mz​), and not three extremes taken separately. For k Gaussian effects with mean m and covariance Σ, the equiprobability ellipsoid (E−m)TΣ−1(E−m)=g2 contains the peak states. Following Capra and Davidovici (Calcul dynamique des structures en zone sismique, 1980), keta replaces it by the vertices of a circumscribed polygon or polyhedron. For two effects with standard deviations σa​, σb​ and correlation ρ, the eight sides are the tangents

σa​x​±σb​y​=±g2(1±ρ)​,∣x∣≤gσa​,∣y∣≤gσb​(22.28)

(x, y deviations from the mean), cut by the rectangle of peak values; each side touches the ellipse at a point inside the side. For three effects, this octagon is built in each of the three coordinate planes (24 vertices), and the third coordinate of each vertex is its conditional mean Ec​=mc​+Σc,ab​Σab−1​(Ea​−ma​,Eb​−mb​). A rank-deficient covariance is treated in its principal axes: an octagon in the plane at rank 2, m±gσ at rank 1, the mean alone at rank 0.

The polyhedron takes a given covariance. keta supplies the covariance of the displacements through the modal covariance; that of the internal forces of a section is not computed directly.

22.10 Vortex shedding#

A bluff section in a steady wind sheds alternating vortices at the frequency n=StU/b. Resonance with a mode of frequency n1​ occurs at the critical velocity, and its sensitivity is measured by the Scruton number (EN 1991-1-4 §E.1.3):

vcrit​=Stn1​b​,Sc=ρb24πmζ​,bymax​​=St2ScKKw​clat​​(22.29)

The third expression is the amplitude of Annex E of EN 1991-1-4 (approach 1), with by default St=0.18, K=0.13, Kw​=0.6 and clat​=0.7. These coefficients are input data: keta does not iterate the dependence of clat​ and Kw​ on the amplitude and the mode shape, which the standard requires to be established. This estimate is only an order of magnitude.

22.11 Scope#

The scope and its limits are as follows:

  • one aerodynamic section and one mean incidence for the whole model; no incidence specific to each bar and no wind frame attached to the bar;
  • no lateral component v in the buffeting forces, no drag derivatives Pi∗​ in flutter, no admittance in the time-domain calculation;
  • strips at the bar midpoints: the accuracy of the calculation depends on the division of the deck into bars, like that of a one-point-per-bar integration;
  • linear response on a finite number of modes: the modal truncation must be checked by the user, in particular for the background part;
  • the flutter velocity is that of the first crossing detected by the sweep;
  • the nonlinear loop is limited to second order.
