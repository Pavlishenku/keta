Chapter 8 Prestressing

This chapter deals with the prestressing tendons of a bar frame: internal tendons, bonded or not, external tendons, and pretensioned strands. The tendon is a tensioned steel member that transfers a force to the concrete through its anchorages, through its deviation in curves and through friction against its duct. The problem is twofold. First, the force in the tendon must be known at each point, allowing for friction, anchorage draw-in and the losses that follow stressing. Next, the effect of this force on the structure must be known; keta represents it by a system of equivalent loads applied to the bars and solved as an ordinary load case.

Time-dependent losses depend on the behaviour of concrete over time, described in chapter 9; their computation through staged construction belongs to chapter 10. Stresses and losses at the level of a cross-section, with several tendons and passive reinforcement, are dealt with in chapter 21. Stay cables and suspension cables are the subject of chapter 7.

8.1 Assumptions#

  • the tendon is represented by its profile along a chain of consecutive bars, given by its eccentricities ey​(s) and ez​(s) in the section axes of each bar, with the origin at the centroid; s is the abscissa along the chain;
  • the tendon force is directed along the tangent to its profile; unless otherwise specified, the slope of the tendon relative to the bar is assumed small: the axial force transmitted is P and not Pcosα;
  • friction follows the exponential law of EN 1992-1-1 §5.10.5.2, with coefficients constant along the tendon;
  • the prestressing steel is linearly elastic, of modulus Ep​ and area Ap​; a tendon whose area is not given is a “force tendon”: it acts on the structure but undergoes neither anchorage draw-in nor losses due to the deformation of the concrete;
  • a bonded tendon follows the strain of the concrete at its level; an ungrouted tendon slides in its duct (chapter 10);
  • the geometry is that of the undeformed model: the equivalent loads are computed once and for all on the initial profile.

8.2 Tendon profile#

8.2.1 Description by segments#

The profile is a sequence of contiguous segments [s0​,s1​] along the chain. On each segment, each eccentricity is a third-degree polynomial in s:

e(s)=c0​+c1​(s−s0​)+c2​(s−s0​)2+c3​(s−s0​)3(8.1)

This form covers the straight line, the parabola and Hermite transitions. Two curves that are not polynomials in s are read on the curve itself: the parametric cubic (s,ey​,ez​)(u)=∑k​pk​uk, u∈[0,1], and the circular arc X(ϕ)=C−RcosϕU+RsinϕD. For a given abscissa s, keta finds the parameter of the curve by Newton's method safeguarded by bisection, then computes the derivatives from

e′=X′Y′​,e′′=X′3Y′′X′−Y′X′′​(8.2)

where X is the component of the curve along the chain and Y the eccentricity. The curvature of the profile, which gives the radial thrust, is therefore that of the actual curve and not that of an interpolation.

8.2.2 Usual profile constructions#

The profile is built from design data in several ways:

  • a polyline through given points, each side being a straight line;
  • a parabola through three points, at abscissae 0, S/2 and S of the tendon length S;
  • a natural cubic spline through given points: continuity of slope and curvature at the interior points, zero curvature at the ends;
  • points with imposed slopes (angle in elevation, angle in plan) and straight alignments, each interval being a parametric Hermite cubic whose tangents at the points are the imposed slopes, the direction of the neighbouring alignments or, failing that, those of the C2 spline;
  • a polyline through vertices, each vertex given a radius receiving a circular fillet.

The parabola through the three points e0​=e(0), em​=e(S/2), e1​=e(S) reads

e(s)=e0​+(4em​−3e0​−e1​)Ss​+2(e0​+e1​−2em​)S2s2​(8.3)

Its curvature is constant, e′′=4(e0​+e1​−2em​)/S2; for a tendon anchored at the centroid at both ends with a sag f at mid-length, e′′=8f/S2.

8.2.3 Circular fillets#

At vertex k, the two alignments meeting there have the unit directions d1​ and d2​, which make the angle θ=arccos(d1​⋅d2​). The fillet of radius R is the circular arc tangent to both alignments; its tangent points lie at the distance

T=Rtan2θ​(8.4)

from the vertex, A=Pk​−Td1​ and B=Pk​+Td2​, and the alignments are shortened up to them. The arc is represented as such, in space. If T exceeds half of either alignment, keta reduces it to that half (the effective radius is then T/tan(θ/2)) and issues a warning; a profile that turns back along the chain is rejected. The points without a radius remain points of the spline, which is tangent to the neighbouring alignments.

8.2.4 Translation, symmetry and copy#

A profile may be shifted along its chain by a length Δx, or mirrored about the abscissa p:

et​(s)=e(s−Δx),em​(s)=e(2p−s)(8.5)

Both transformations preserve the nature of the three types of segment: the cubic in s is re-expanded by the binomial formula, the parametric cubic is reparametrised by u↦1−u, and the circular arc has its centre reflected and its direction of travel reversed. The half-profile completed by its mirror image is continuous at the pivot; it is differentiable there only if e′(p)=0, otherwise the kink of slope 2e′(p) is kept (a deviator may lie at midspan). A tendon may also be copied, with its profile and its force, onto another chain of bars of the same length, to within 10−9 in relative terms. The anchorages, the imposed force profile, the free lengths and the live anchorage follow the transformation.

8.2.5 Three-dimensional profiles and deviations#

The tendon is a space curve with unit tangent

t(s)=1+ey′2​+ez′2​​(1, ey′​(s), ez′​(s))​(8.6)

in the axes of the bar. The cumulative angular deviation θ(s) that enters friction is the total angle swept by this tangent from the anchorage:

θ(s)=∫0s​​dσdt​​dσ(8.7)

It counts the curvatures in both elevation and plan, and remains correct for a steep profile, where the sum of the ∣e′′∣ds would overestimate the angle by a factor 1+e′2. keta evaluates it by summing the angles between neighbouring tangents, sixteen per step of the computation grid. A kink in the profile, that is a jump in ey′​ or ez′​ between two segments (polyline, deviator), is a concentrated deviation: the angle arccos(t−⋅t+) is turned through over zero length.

The length of the tendon is its arc length, greater than that of the chain where the tendon is inclined:

ℓ(s)=∫0s​1+ey′2​+ez′2​​dσ(8.8)

computed by a three-point Gauss quadrature on eight sub-steps of each grid step. Wobble friction and anchorage draw-in act over this length.

8.3 Force after friction#

The tendon is stressed to the force P0​ at its live anchorage. Along the tendon, the force decreases by friction against the duct according to EN 1992-1-1, expression (5.45):

P(s)=P0​e−μ(θ(s)+kx(s))−φx(s)(8.9)

where μ is the coefficient of friction in curves [rad−1], k the unintentional angular displacement per unit length [rad/m] and x(s) the arc length (8.8) that rubs against the concrete. The term φ [m−1] is a friction per unit length independent of μ, useful to reproduce data of the form e−(fα+φx) with f=0; it is zero by default. Over the free lengths of an external tendon (the straight runs between deviators, where the tendon does not touch the concrete), wobble friction does not act: x is the arc length less these runs, while the deviations at the deviators count in θ.

The tendon may be stressed at its start, at its end or at both. Stressed at both ends, it carries at each point the larger of the two friction curves, max(P1​(s),P2​(s)). It may also start and finish inside its bars: the force is zero outside the anchored interval [sb​,se​], and friction runs from these anchorages.

keta evaluates the force on a grid of twenty intervals per metre of anchored length (at least four), supplemented by each kink of the profile, entered twice (left and right limits) so that the force drop e−μΔθ of a concentrated deviation is a jump and not a ramp. The force between two points is interpolated linearly. The default values are μ=0.19 rad−1, k=0.01 rad/m, an anchorage draw-in of 6 mm and Ep​=195 GPa; they are to be replaced by the values of the technical approval of the prestressing system.

Finally, the force may be given directly, point by point, along the chain: this imposed profile then replaces friction and anchorage draw-in, which are deemed to be included in the values given. This is the form taken by a pretensioned strand.

8.4 Anchorage draw-in#

8.4.1 One live anchorage#

At lock-off, the wedges slip and the anchorage moves back by a length g. The tendon shortens near the anchorage; friction there reverses and now opposes this return. The force after lock-off P′(s) is the reflected friction curve: over the affected zone [0,λ], the loss per unit length is the same as at stressing, in the opposite direction. For the exponential law, this reflection reads

P′(s)=min(P(s), P(s)C​),C=P(λ)2(8.10)

On a logarithmic scale, P′ is the mirror image of P about its value at λ. The length λ is set by compatibility: the shortening of the tendon over the affected zone equals the movement of the anchorage,

∫0λ​[P(s)−P′(s)]dℓ=gEp​Ap​(8.11)

keta solves this equation in C by bisection (one hundred iterations), the integral being computed on the grid with the change of sign of P−C/P located linearly within its interval. If the affected zone reaches the opposite anchorage, the same equation lowers the whole reflected curve; if even a fully slack tendon cannot take up the movement, the force is zero. Anchorage draw-in is computed only for a tendon whose area Ap​ is given.

When friction is roughly linear near the anchorage, with slope p=−dP/ds, the classical form is recovered

λ≈pgEp​Ap​​​,ΔP(0)≈2pλ(8.12)

For the tendon of the figure below (a 30 m parabola with a sag of 0.6 m, μ=0.19, k=0.005 rad/m, g=6 mm, Ep​Ap​=585 MN, P0​=4.185 MN), equation (8.11) gives λ=21.1 m and a loss of 332 kN at the anchorage; the approximate form gives 20.7 m and 339 kN. The force computed by keta along the beam differs from the reflection integrated on a very fine grid by less than 6⋅10−5P0​.

8.4.2 Two live anchorages#

A tendon stressed from both ends is locked off at one anchorage, then at the other, in the declared order. Rule (8.10) is applied twice, the reference curve of each lock-off being the friction curve issuing from the anchorage being released:

P(1)(s)=min(P(s), P1​(s)C1​​),P(2)(s)=min(P(1)(s), P2​(s)C2​​)(8.13)

Each constant is set by (8.11), applied to the curve in place at the time of lock-off. On a short tendon, the two affected zones overlap and the result depends on the order: the second lock-off acts on a curve that has already been lowered (figure above, dotted curve: the force at the end released second is lower than at the start).

8.5 Equivalent loads#

8.5.1 Actions of the tendon on the concrete#

The concrete is isolated: the tendon exerts on it the force of its anchorages, the pressure of its curves and the friction along its duct. These actions form a self-equilibrated system, which keta applies to the bars of the chain in the local axes of each. In a bar, F(x) denotes the force that the tendon cut at x exerts on the downstream part, under the small-slope assumption:

F(x)=(P, Pey′​, Pez′​)(8.14)

At a start anchorage, the tendon pushes the concrete with F, applied at the point (ey​,ez​): a force and the moment (G, Pez​, −Pey​) about the centroid, with G=ey​Fz​−ez​Fy​. At an end anchorage, it exerts the opposite. Along the bar, the change in F is taken by the concrete: the load density is dF/dx, that is

n(x)=P′,py​(x)=(Pey′​)′,pz​(x)=(Pez′​)′(8.15)

The axial component is the friction drag; the transverse components contain the radial thrust Pe′′ and the friction term P′e′. The drag P′ acts at the level of the tendon and not at the centroid: it is accompanied by a distributed couple of density P′e, and not by a shear force. Finally, a tendon with a lateral eccentricity that deviates vertically twists the bar: the distributed torque is dG/dx, since ey′​Fz​−ez′​Fy​=0.

At a kink in the profile, the deviation is concentrated: the bar receives the point force F(x+)−F(x−), which includes the rotation of the tangent and the force drop by friction, together with the couple of the force jump at the eccentricity. A parabola of sag f over a span L, without friction, thus produces the uniform radial thrust

p=Pe′′=L28Pf​(8.16)

8.5.2 Discretisation of the distributed loads#

The part of each bar travelled by the tendon is divided into eight pieces, plus the kinks of the profile. On each piece [xa​,xb​] of length h, keta replaces each density by a linear load w(x)=wa​+(wb​−wa​)(x−xa​)/h that has the same resultant and the same moment as the actual load:

∫xa​xb​​wdx=F(xb​)−F(xa​),∫xa​xb​​(x−xa​)wdx=hF(xb​)−∫xa​xb​​Fdx(8.17)

the second equality following from an integration by parts; the integral of F is evaluated by the composite Simpson rule on eight sub-intervals. This gives wb​=6M1​/h2−2R/h and wa​=2R/h−wb​, R and M1​ denoting the resultant and the moment. The distributed couple P′e and the torque are treated in the same way. The resultants telescope from one piece to the next and from one bar to the next: the load system is self-equilibrated to within round-off errors, whatever the number of kinks in the profile.

Without friction, on a polynomial segment, the density Pe′′ is at most linear and the fit reproduces it without error; with friction, the force profile is that of the grid of section 8.3. The internal forces are then obtained by the ordinary solution of chapter 4.

8.5.3 Tendon inclined relative to the bar#

As an option, the bar receives only the component of the force along its axis, and the corresponding transverse component:

N=Pcosα,cosα=1+ey′2​+ez′2​​1​,F=Pcosα(1, ey′​, ez′​)(8.18)

The difference from the small-slope assumption is 2.5 % for a slope of 0.23. This option is used mainly for steeply inclined external tendons.

8.6 Primary and secondary effects#

Let the structure be cut at a section through which the tendon passes. The cut tendon exerts on the section the force P at the point (ey​,ez​): this is the primary (isostatic) effect of prestressing, independent of the restraints. With the sign convention of chapter 2,

Niso=−Pc,Myiso​=−Pcez​,Mziso​=Pcey​,Vziso​=−Pcez′​,Vyiso​=−Pcey′​(8.19)

where c=cosα for a tendon computed with the exact angle, c=1 otherwise, and P is the effective force (losses deducted, including the local loss field if it is kept). Under friction, dMyiso​/dx is not Vziso​: the difference is the distributed couple P′e met above.

On a statically determinate structure, the internal forces obtained with the equivalent loads are those of (8.19). On a statically indeterminate structure, the redundant restraints oppose the deformations imposed by the tendon and develop reactions: the secondary (hyperstatic) effect is

Mhyp(x)=M(x)−Miso(x)(8.20)

Since it is produced only by support reactions, it is linear between two supports (EN 1992-1-1 §5.10.7). For two equal spans of length L, with a parabolic tendon of sag f in each, centred on the three supports and without friction, the secondary moment is Pf at the central support, in absolute value, and puts the bottom fibre in tension; the total moment at midspan reduces to Pf/2. keta recovers both values to within 10−9 with bars without shear deformation (with it, the relative stiffness of the spans changes slightly and the difference reaches 0.2 % for a rectangular section 1.5 m deep over a 30 m span).

In staged construction, keta keeps the primary effect of the tendons up to date at each stage and derives the secondary effect from it (chapter 10).

8.7 Immediate losses#

8.7.1 Elastic shortening#

A bonded tendon, already anchored, follows the concrete at its level: any strain increment Δεc​ of the concrete at that location changes its force by

ΔP=Ep​Ap​Δεc​,Δεc​=E1​(AN​+Iy​My​ez​​−Iz​Mz​ey​​)(8.21)

(a compression shortens the tendon: ΔP<0 is a loss). This is the so-called “K=1” rule: each computation increment of a staged analysis (load, stressing of another tendon, creep, shrinkage, deactivation) makes the already anchored tendons lose or gain what the strain of their concrete imposes on them. By default, the strain is the length-weighted mean, over the active part of the tendon between its anchorages, of the value at the middle of each bar, and the loss is a uniform fraction of the whole force profile. Two options refine this computation: the mean over all the stations of the anchored length, or a loss evaluated point by point and kept as a field along the tendon, which a pretensioned strand requires, since its transmission zones do not lose in the same way as the plateau.

The loss itself loads the structure, which deforms and makes the tendons lose again: keta iterates until the correction becomes negligible (10−9P0​), at most two hundred times. For a concentric tendon in a bar of rigidity EA, stressed after a first identical tendon, the loss of the first one is thus

ΔP1​=1+ρρ​P2​,ρ=EAEp​Ap​​(1+Iy​Aez2​​+Iz​Aey2​​)(8.22)

instead of ρP2​ with a single pass (option). For E=35 GPa, A=0.5 m², Ap​=3000 mm² and P2​=4 MN, keta gives 129.389 kN, and the formula 129.389 kN.

The tendons stressed in a single operation (one group of a stage) are simultaneous: the jack compensates for their mutual shortening and they cause no loss to one another (K=0). The successive groups of a stage, on the other hand, cause losses to one another in order.

8.7.2 Units stressed one after another#

A tendon made of n identical units stressed in succession loses on average the fraction (n−1)/(2n) of its own elastic shortening (EN 1992-1-1, expression (5.44)). keta applies this rule with a single pass; with iterated losses, it simulates the sequence: unit j is stressed while the first j−1, anchored, follow the concrete with the fraction (j−1)/n of the area. The total loss is

ΔP=j=2∑n​nj−1​1+ρ(j−1)/nρP/n​ρ→0​2nn−1​ρP(8.23)

For n=2 and the previous data, keta gives 32.879 kN against 33.429 kN for rule (5.44), in agreement with (8.23).

8.8 Time-dependent losses#

Shrinkage and creep shorten the concrete at the level of the tendon, and the steel relaxes. keta computes these losses in two ways during a staged analysis, or estimates them by the lump-sum rules of the former codes.

8.8.1 Losses computed through the structure#

This is the default mode of a staged analysis. Between two dates, the creep and shrinkage of each bar are imposed on the structure (chapter 10). Each bonded tendon loses Ep​Ap​ times the free creep and shrinkage strain of the concrete at its level,

ΔP=Ep​Ap​(Δεcc​+Δεcs​)(8.24)

averaged over the active part of the tendon as in (8.21); this loss loads the structure, whose response makes all the tendons lose again, iteratively as for (8.21). Since the loss itself creeps in the next time step, the result follows the hereditary integral of chapter 9. The relaxation of each tendon is computed on its current stress by the equivalent-time method (chapter 9). On a concentric bar, with a non-ageing creep law of final coefficient 2, the loss computed by keta after 10 000 days (243.054 kN for P0​=4 MN and ρ=0.033) coincides with the solution of the Volterra integral equation of the problem to within 2⋅10−8.

8.8.2 EN 1992-1-1 formula#

As an option, each time interval [t1​,t2​] (or each sub-step of the interval) applies expression (5.46) of EN 1992-1-1 in incremental form:

Δσp​=1+EAc​Ep​Ap​​(1+Ic​Ac​zcp2​​)(1+0.8Δφ)−Ep​(Δεcc​+Δεcs​)+cr​Δσpr​​(8.25)

where Δεcc​ is the creep strain of the concrete at the level of the tendon that the creep engine computes for the interval (the same one that loads the structure), Δεcs​ the free shrinkage of the interval, Δφ=φ(t2​,t1​) the creep coefficient of the interval and Δσpr​ the relaxation of the interval. The term Ac​zcp2​/Ic​ is extended to both bending planes, Ac​(ez2​/Iy​+ey2​/Iz​). All the section quantities are length-weighted means over the active part of the tendon. The loss is applied to the tendon before the elastic redistribution of the creep increment; it receives no K=1 pass of its own, the denominator taking its place. With a single step for the interval, keta reproduces the value of (5.46) (246.044 kN in the previous example, against 243.054 kN by the computation through the structure).

At the level of an isolated section, the same compatibility is written for several tendons and for the passive reinforcement, which restrains creep and shrinkage (chapter 21).

8.8.3 Relaxation in the losses#

The pure relaxation Δσpr​ is given by the laws of chapter 9. It enters (8.25) with a coefficient cr​ that depends on how it is computed:

cr​=0.8 if Δσpr​=σpi​[ρ(t2​−tt​)−ρ(t1​−tt​)],cr​=1 if Δσpr​ is computed by equivalent time(8.26)

In the first case, the relaxation is read on the curve of the initial stress σpi​, from the stressing date tt​, and the factor 0.8 of expression (5.46) accounts for the drop in stress due to the other losses. In the second (BPEL law, or EN 1992-1-1 law with the losses computed through the structure), the relaxation is read on the current stress: the reduction is already included. Heat curing adds the equivalent time teb​ to the relaxation clock (EN 1992-1-1 §10.3.2).

8.8.4 Lump-sum code losses#

For preliminary design, keta provides the lump-sum losses of the Instruction provisoire n° 2 and of the BPEL, from a final concrete stress at the level of the tendon σb​ (compression positive) given by the engineer. With n=6, the modular ratio imposed by these texts:

Δσel​=2n​σb​=3σb​,Δσr​=εr​Ep​,Δσfl​={2nσb​2.5nσb​​(IP2)(BPEL)​(8.27)

The elastic loss covers the non-simultaneity of the stressing operations. The BPEL adds five sixths of the final relaxation, computed by

Δσρ​=1006​ρ1000​(Rg​σpi​​−μ0​)σpi​≥0,Δσdiff​=Δσr​+Δσfl​+65​Δσρ​(8.28)

with μ0​=0.43 for ρ1000​≤2.5 %, 0.35 up to 8 % and 0.30 beyond. The IP2 takes the given pure final relaxation Δσρ​, reduced by the interaction:

Δσdiff​=Δσr​+Δσfl​+Δσρ​(1−σpi​−0.55Rg​Δσr​+Δσfl​​)(8.29)

as long as Δσr​+Δσfl​<σpi​−0.55Rg​; beyond that, the stress drop absorbs the relaxation and Δσdiff​=Δσr​+Δσfl​. The lump-sum shrinkage is εr​=4⋅10−4 (IP2) or 3⋅10−4 (BPEL) in a dry atmosphere (relative humidity below 65 %), and 3⋅10−4 or 2⋅10−4 above. Some printed editions associate the higher value with the more humid atmosphere, which contradicts the shrinkage law of the same text; keta adopts the physical reading, and the value of εr​ can be imposed to reproduce a computation made with the other. Example: σb​=10 MPa, σpi​=1395 MPa, Rg​=1860 MPa, ρ1000​=2.5 %, humidity 70 %; the BPEL gives 30 MPa of elastic loss, 39 + 150 MPa of shrinkage and creep and 67 MPa of relaxation, that is 274.8 MPa in total.

These rules give an order of magnitude. The losses of a structure built in stages are computed by the two previous methods.

8.9 Pretensioning#

8.9.1 Bed, release and shortening#

A pretensioned strand is first stressed on a bed: it carries P0​ but does not act on the structure. While it remains on the bed, it relaxes (the loss is taken by the bed), and the surrounding concrete, which slides on the bed and is restrained by the strand only in the ratio Ep​Ap​/(Ec​Ac​), shrinks freely, without creeping since it is not loaded. At release, the strand first undergoes the thermal loss of steam curing (EN 1992-1-1 §10.5.2), then transfers its force to the concrete by bond. Once bonded, it undergoes its own elastic shortening:

ΔP=Δσθ​Ap​+1+ρρ​(P0​−Δσθ​Ap​)(8.30)

with ρ defined in (8.22). For a strand with an eccentricity of 0.3 m in a bar of 0.5 m² and 0.05 m⁴, a thermal loss of 20 MPa and P0​=4 MN, keta gives 295.301 kN, and the formula 295.301 kN.

8.9.2 Transmission laws#

Near each bonded end, the force rises from zero to P0​ over the transmission length. After an unbonded length g (debonding sleeve), the fraction of force at the distance d from the start of bond follows one of the following laws:

P0​P​=min(1,l1​d​),P0​P​=1−(1−min(1,l1​d​))2,P0​P​=⎩⎨⎧​αd/l1​α+(1−α)l2​−l1​d−l1​​1​d<l1​l1​≤d<l2​d≥l2​​(8.31)

respectively linear, parabolic and bilinear; without a law, the force jumps to P0​ right at the bonded end. The force at a point is the smaller of the two values counted from each end. The parabola is represented by 256 chords, which bounds the error on the force to 2−17P0​. A tendon that represents n identical strands carries n times the force and the area of one strand.

Note

These three laws and the debonding sleeves are built when a data file is read. The Python module does not offer them under this name: it takes the force profile of the strand point by point, which can be derived from (8.31).

Over the transmission length, the change in force P′ is taken by bond at the level of the strand: this is the axial drag of (8.15), accompanied by the distributed couple P′e. It produces no shear force, as the Vz​ diagram of a beam prestressed by straight strands confirms.

8.11 Scope#

  • The equivalent loads are those of small slopes (N=P), unless the exact-angle option is used; they are computed on the initial geometry.
  • The force is interpolated linearly between the points of a grid of twenty intervals per metre; the kinks of the profile are double points of it.
  • Anchorage draw-in and the losses due to the deformation of the concrete require the area Ap​ of the tendon; a force tendon undergoes none of them.
  • The parabola through three points accepts only the abscissae 0, S/2 and S; construction by successive connected parabolas is not offered (the C2 spline takes its place).
  • In a staged analysis, time-dependent losses follow EN 1992-1-1 (expression (5.46)) or the computation through the structure; the BPEL combination with five sixths of the relaxation alone exists only in lump-sum form.
  • The lump-sum losses require the final concrete stress at the level of the tendon, which keta does not predict.
