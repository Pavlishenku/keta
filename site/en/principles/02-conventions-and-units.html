Chapter 2 Conventions and units

A computed result can only be read together with its conventions: in which axes a displacement is expressed, in which direction a moment is positive, which fibre it puts in tension. This chapter fixes these conventions, to which the other chapters refer. It describes the units, the global axes and the six degrees of freedom of the nodes, the construction of the local axes of a bar and the role of the angle β, the numbering of the unknowns, then the definition and sign of internal forces, stresses and imposed strains. The matrices of the beam element written in these axes are the subject of chapter 3; the axes specific to skew supports and elastic links are described in chapter 6, those of shells in chapter 24.

2.1 Assumptions#

  • the geometry is described by the coordinates of the nodes in a single Cartesian system;
  • a bar is a straight segment between two distinct nodes, possibly offset from the nodes by rigid arms (chapter 3);
  • the y and z axes of a cross-section are its principal axes of inertia, through its centroid; the shear centre coincides with the centroid, except in the non-uniform torsion analysis of chapter 19;
  • rotations are small: they combine as vectors, and the displacement of a point rigidly connected to a node is obtained by a cross product.

2.2 Units#

keta works in the base units of the International System and performs no conversion: the numbers supplied are taken as they are, and the results are returned in the same units. A model described in kilonewtons and millimetres would give wrong results without any error being raised; it is up to the user to convert the input. The consistent units of the main quantities are as follows.

The self-weight of a bar follows from the density of its material, the area of its cross-section and the gravity vector:

qsw​=ρAg,g=(0, 0, −9.80665) m/s2 by default(2.1)

A material without density (ρ=0) weighs nothing; reinforced concrete is described with ρ=2500 kg/m3 or thereabouts, and not with a unit weight of 25 kN/m³. The ages and durations that enter the laws of concrete (creep, shrinkage, relaxation) are counted in days, as in the standards that define them; this is the only unit that is not an SI base unit.

2.3 Global axes and degrees of freedom#

The global axes (X,Y,Z) form a right-handed orthonormal system, with the Z axis vertical and pointing upwards. Default gravity is therefore directed along −Z, and a downward load has a negative component FZ​. Each node carries six degrees of freedom, three translations and three rotations, collected in the vector

un​=(uX​, uY​, uZ​, θX​, θY​, θZ​)T(2.2)

in this order, which is also that of the components of nodal forces and moments, reactions and support stiffnesses. A rotation is positive according to the right-hand rule about the positive axis. Rotations being small, the displacement of a point P rigidly connected to node n (rigid arm, rigid link, offset) is

uP​=un​+θn​×(xP​−xn​)(2.3)

where un​ and θn​ denote the three translations and the three rotations of the node. This relation underlies every kinematic constraint in the kernel.

2.4 Local axes of a bar#

2.4.1 Construction#

Each bar carries a right-handed orthonormal local system (x,y,z). The x axis runs from the start i to the end j of the element; if the bar is offset, it is the ends shifted by the rigid arms, and not the nodes, that define it (chapter 3). For a bar that is not vertical, the reference axes y0​ and z0​ are built from the vertical:

ex​=∥xj​−xi​∥xj​−xi​​,ey0​​=∥eZ​×ex​∥eZ​×ex​​,ez0​​=ex​×ey0​​(2.4)

The y0​ axis is thus horizontal and perpendicular to the bar, and z0​ lies in the vertical plane containing the bar, pointing upwards (ez0​​⋅eZ​>0). For a horizontal bar directed along +X, this gives y0​=Y and z0​=Z; for a bar directed along +Y, y0​=−X and z0​=Z. In the common case of a horizontal deck, the z axis of the bars is therefore vertical and upwards and the y axis horizontal, to the left when looking from i towards j.

2.4.2 Angle β#

The principal axes of the cross-section do not always coincide with y0​ and z0​: tilted beam, inclined truss chord, section laid on its side. The angle β rotates the axes of the cross-section about x, positive according to the right-hand rule:

ey​=cosβey0​​+sinβez0​​,ez​=−sinβey0​​+cosβez0​​(2.5)

Seen from end j, with the x axis pointing towards the observer, a positive angle turns y towards z0​, anticlockwise. The dimensions of a parametric cross-section refer to the rotated axes: the width b of a rectangle is measured along y, its depth h along z, and Iy​=bh3/12 is the second moment of area for bending in the xz plane.

2.4.3 Vertical bars#

For a vertical bar, eZ​×ex​ vanishes and the construction (2.4) no longer makes sense. keta treats as vertical any bar whose direction makes an angle of less than about 0.26° with the vertical (∣ex​⋅eZ​∣>0.99999) and then takes the global Y axis as reference:

ez0​​=∥ex​×eY​∥ex​×eY​​,ey0​​=ez0​​×ex​(2.6)

which gives ey0​​=eY​ for an exactly vertical bar. An upward bar (from bottom to top) therefore has its z0​ axis along −X, a downward bar along +X. The angle β is then applied as for the other bars. A pier described from the foundation to the crosshead thus has y along Y and z along −X: the second moment of area Iy​ acts in bending in the vertical plane XZ. The rule is not continuous near the vertical: a nearly vertical bar inclined towards +Y has its z0​ axis close to −Y, and not to −X. For slightly inclined pier shafts, it is wise to check the orientation obtained and, if necessary, to fix it with β.

2.4.4 Transformation matrix#

The rotation matrix R has as its rows the vectors ex​, ey​, ez​ expressed in the global axes. It transforms the global components of a vector into local components, and the block-diagonal matrix T does the same for the twelve degrees of freedom of a bar:

vℓ​=Rvg​,uℓ​=Tug​,T=diag(R, R, R, R)(2.7)

Since R is orthogonal, vg​=RTvℓ​, and a local matrix k becomes TTkT in global axes.

2.5 Numbering#

Nodes, materials, cross-sections, bars and load cases receive an integer number when they are created, assigned in order of creation starting from 1. Results are identified by these numbers. The degrees of freedom of a bar are ordered, in its local axes, as

uℓ​=(ui​, vi​, wi​, θxi​, θyi​, θzi​, uj​, vj​, wj​, θxj​, θyj​, θzj​)T(2.8)

where u, v, w are the translations along x, y, z. For assembly, the degree of freedom of component c∈{0,…,5} of the node of rank n (rank in order of creation, starting from 0) occupies position 6n+c of the global vector; the restrained degrees of freedom are then removed and the others renumbered in this order (chapter 4). This internal numbering does not appear in the results.

2.6 Internal forces#

2.6.1 Definition and sign#

The bar is cut by the cross-section at abscissa x. The internal forces N, Vy​, Vz​, T, My​, Mz​ are the components, in the local axes, of the resultant force and of the resultant moment about the centroid of the actions that the part of the bar beyond the cut (x′>x) exerts on the part before it (x′<x). In terms of the stresses in the cross-section, they are

NT​=∫A​σxx​dA,=∫A​(yτxz​−zτxy​)dA,​Vy​My​​=∫A​τxy​dA,=∫A​zσxx​dA,​Vz​Mz​​=∫A​τxz​dA,=−∫A​yσxx​dA​(2.9)

The following reading rules result:

  • N>0 is tension;
  • My​>0 puts the fibre z>0 in tension; for a horizontal beam whose z axis points vertically upwards, a span moment (bottom fibre in tension) is therefore negative and a moment over an intermediate support positive;
  • Mz​>0 compresses the fibre y>0;
  • Vz​>0 acts along +z on the face with outward normal +x of the part x′<x; on a simply supported beam loaded downwards, Vz​ is negative near the left support and positive near the right one;
  • T>0 is a torsional moment directed along +x on that same face.

This convention, known as the vector convention, treats the six components in the same way and makes the equilibrium relations identical in the two bending planes up to sign. It differs from the usual plane strength-of-materials convention, in which a span moment is positive: to compare with a hand calculation made in that convention, change the sign of My​.

2.6.2 End forces#

The end forces of a bar, f=(f1​,…,f12​) in the order (2.8), are the forces and moments that the nodes exert on the bar, expressed in its local axes. At the start, the part x′>0 is the whole bar and the part x′<0 the node; the internal forces at x=0 are therefore the opposites of the end forces at i, and those at x=L are equal to the end forces at j:

(N,Vy​,Vz​,T,My​,Mz​)(0)=−(f1​,…,f6​),(N,Vy​,Vz​,T,My​,Mz​)(L)=(f7​,…,f12​)(2.10)

the second equality assuming that no point load acts exactly at x=L (a load placed on the end is transferred to the node and does not enter the forces of the bar).

2.7 Strains, rotations and curvatures#

The bending rotation in the xz plane is the rotation θy​ of the cross-section about +y. In Euler-Bernoulli theory,

θy​=−dxdw​,θz​=+dxdv​(2.11)

The minus sign comes from the right-handed system: a deflection w increasing with x turns the cross-section in the negative direction about y. It is applied throughout the kernel (stiffness, mass, geometric stiffness, elastic foundation, thermal loads, recovery of displacements), and the rotations returned at the output sections follow it. The elongation of a fibre with coordinates (y,z) is written with these rotations

ε(y,z)=dxdu​+zdxdθy​​−ydxdθz​​,My​=EIy​dxdθy​​,Mz​=EIz​dxdθz​​(2.12)

Imposed strains (thermal expansion, shrinkage, creep, fabrication defect) follow the same logic. A generalised imposed strain consists of an elongation ε0​ at the centroid, two curvatures κy​, κz​ and a rate of twist κx​; it elongates the fibre (y,z) by

ε∗(y,z)=ε0​+κz​y−κy​z,κy​=−dxdθy∗​​,κz​=−dxdθz∗​​(2.13)

A positive curvature κy​ elongates the fibre z<0; a free horizontal bar then deflects downwards in the span, as under a downward load. A temperature change ΔT(y,z)=Δt+gy​y+gz​z, with coefficient of thermal expansion α, is the imposed strain

ε0​=αΔt,κz​=αgy​,κy​=−αgz​(2.14)

so that a beam whose top face is the warmer (gz​>0) lifts in the span. Chapter 3 gives the equivalent nodal forces of these fields.

2.8 Stresses#

The normal stress at a point (y,z) of the cross-section follows from the internal forces by Navier's formula, in principal axes:

σ=AN​+Iy​My​z​−Iz​Mz​y​(2.15)

positive in tension. The signs of the two bending terms follow directly from (2.9). The shear stress and the equivalent stress returned at the stress points of the cross-sections are described in chapter 4; the exact stresses at any point of an arbitrary cross-section in chapter 20.

Each result is returned in the axes in which it is naturally read. The following table summarises them.

A reaction is the force that the support exerts on the structure: under a downward load, the vertical reaction of a simple support is positive. The sum of the reactions therefore balances the sum of the applied loads, to within rounding, which provides a first check of any model.
